> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered on July 8, 2016.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ugK5Kwr--10).

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. Whoever Allah guides, no one can lead astray; whoever He leads astray, no one can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. He conveyed the message, fulfilled the trust, sincerely counselled the community, and strove for Allah as He should be striven for. May Allah reward him on our behalf and on behalf of the Muslim community with the finest reward. To continue:

### Death Is Closer than a Sandal Strap

Ramadan has gone. A saying is reported: “Death is closer to one of you than his sandal strap.”[^1] What is a sandal strap? It is the strap that touches your foot—may Allah honour you. Only your skin lies between you and the shoe you wear, and death is closer than that. It is as though it touches that skin, and all that remains between it and me is for it to pass beneath the skin, as though it were less than a second away. Have you ever felt this when you step on something and suddenly stop?

### A Stranger or a Traveller Passing Through

The Prophet ﷺ said, in meaning: “Be in this world as though you were a stranger or a traveller passing through.”[^2] A stranger, as you know, is polite, careful and alert. He does not take liberties with people he does not know, speak at length where it is not appropriate, or become familiar with those around him until he trusts them and can rely on them. Many scholars discuss this saying in connection with not expecting a long life. The truth is that someone travelling tomorrow does not leave many things behind.

Here is a question from everyday experience. If you are in a hotel and your flight leaves early in the morning, does it make sense to return to your room for two hours' rest carrying bags full of food that you will not eat and cannot take onto the plane? That is not sensible. You make do with very little, perhaps a bottle of water. If a little remains, you may say: this will be useful until I reach the airport entrance, because you will not take the water through to the plane. This is the way of thinking we use in our worldly lives.

### A Report about Nuh, Peace Be upon Him

It is reported that when Nuh, peace be upon him, was asked about life, he said it was like a house with two doors: you enter through one and leave through the other.[^3] If I asked you, at forty, fifty or sixty years of age, to kindly tell us about your life and experiences, you might sum up your whole life in less than five minutes, pausing at a few moments you remember. There may even have been very important situations, among the hardest you ever faced, that you no longer remember.

### A Rider Resting in a Tree's Shade

The Prophet ﷺ used to say, in meaning: “What have I to do with this world? I am only like a rider who rests in the shade of a tree, then moves on and leaves it.”[^4] Someone resting beneath a tree does not dwell on its beauty, imagine that he owns it, or try to buy it. He knows that he will not return to it and that it is not in his possession. He treats it as a stay even shorter than a temporary one. He seeks only a little shade over his head for a short while, then leaves and moves on. He keeps looking forward to the moment when he can set off again and continue his journey.

### Ramadan: Serving Allah and Always Needing Him

Allah says, in a translation of the meaning: “Fasting has been prescribed for you, as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become mindful of Allah” [Al-Baqarah 2:183]. O Allah, all praise belongs to You for honouring us with prayer, fasting, the night prayers of Ramadan, and reading the Quran; for allowing us to raise our hands in humble prayer to You; for the sincerity we believe You have placed in our hearts; for the repeated supplications through which we drew close to You; and for our good expectations of You. Glorified are You. Accept this from us. Our Lord, we ask You to keep covering our faults and to grant us complete well-being.

I believe we all know with certainty that we cannot do without the Creator. Does anyone imagine that he can do without his Creator in taking a breath? In moving his feet? In the hand that wears his watch? In his muscles? In his ability to think and express himself? In feeling love for those around him? In disliking what is hateful and loving the truth? If we are certain that we cannot do without the Creator, then we need His gifts. Let us be certain that what Allah holds can be gained only through obedience to Him, glorified is He. Ramadan is servitude to Allah. Just as only Allah truly knows our fasting, only Allah truly knows our hearts. If fasting has taught you this, it must have worked within your heart, so that it remains attached to one God, certain that He alone, glorified is He, knows what it contains.

Let us know that your longing to meet Allah grows with your heart's attachment to Him. Meeting Allah in its full reality comes only through death, but there are stages of such meeting, expressed in your servitude to the Lord of all worlds.

### Six Days of Shawwal

The Prophet ﷺ said, in meaning: “Whoever fasts Ramadan, then follows it with six days of Shawwal, is as though he has fasted for all time.”[^5] This is Allah's generosity, a gift He grants you. Allah deals with us in keeping with how we deal with Him.

It was his practice ﷺ to fast, and he did not hold back from fasting. He balanced the needs of his body and the needs of his spirit. Caring for only one side makes the other unable to function. A man has a job and a home. If he cares only for his work and neglects his home, he loses his home. If he gives all his attention to his family and neglects his work, he loses his work. In the same way, a human being has a self, a spirit and a body. If he cares only for his body, rests a great deal, eats a great deal and enjoys himself a great deal, he loses his spirit. If he cares only for his spirit, he loses his body. Many scholars who speak about detachment from worldly pleasures give greater weight to the spirit, even if they neglect the body alongside it, and they have not suffered much loss. But the loss comes to people like us: we care far too much about the details and small matters of worldly life, and overlook or forget the affairs of the Hereafter.
