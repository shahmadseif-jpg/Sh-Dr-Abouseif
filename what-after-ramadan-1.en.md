All praise is due to Allah; we praise Him, seek His help, seek His guidance and seek His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Allah from the evils of our own selves and from our misdeeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can lead astray, and whoever He leads astray, none can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, His Prophet and His Messenger ﷺ, who conveyed the message, fulfilled the trust, gave sincere counsel to the Ummah and strove in Allah with true striving; so may Allah reward him on our behalf and on behalf of the Ummah of Islam with the best of rewards. To proceed:

### Death is nearer than the strap of the sandal

Ramadan has gone, and it is related in the athar that "death is nearer to one of you than the strap of his sandal."[^1] And what is the strap of the sandal? It is the strap of the sandal that touches your foot, may Allah honor you. Between you and the shoe you wear there is only the leather, and death is nearer than that; it is as if it were stuck to that leather, and there is nothing between me and it except that it enter into the depths of the leather, as if in less than a second. Have you ever tried this feeling, when you step on something with your feet and stop?

### A stranger or a passer-by

And the Prophet ﷺ said: «Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a passer-by.»[^2] And the stranger, as you know, is well-mannered, reserved and on guard; he does not presume upon people he does not know, does not go on talking in the wrong place, and does not presume upon those around him unless he trusts them and relies on them. Many of the people of knowledge count this sentence under the discussion of shortening one's hopes, and in truth the one who is travelling tomorrow does not leave many things behind him.

A question from experience: if you are in a hotel and your flight is early in the morning, is it reasonable to return to your room to rest for two hours having carried your bags with a quantity of food that you will not eat and will not be able to carry with you to the plane? This is not sensible. You content yourself with very little, perhaps a bottle of water, and perhaps a little of it remains and you say: This will serve me up to the airport door, because you will not pass through with water onto the plane. This is the sense with which we deal with our worldly life.

### Of what is related from Noah, peace be upon him

It is related that Noah, peace be upon him, when asked about life, said that it is like a house with two doors; you entered by one and left by the other.[^3] And if I said to you, and the age of one of us is forty or fifty or sixty years: Be so kind as to tell us about your life and your experiences, the servant might summarize his whole life in under five minutes, stopping at a set of stations that are the ones he mentions. Indeed there may be very important events from the farthest of what passed over you, and yet you do not remember them.

### A rider who sheltered under a tree

And the Prophet ﷺ used to say: «What have I to do with the world! I am in the world only like a rider who sought shade under a tree, then went on and left it.»[^4] And the one who shelters under the tree does not contemplate its beauty, does not imagine that it belongs to him, and does not try to buy it, because he knows that he will not return to it and that it is not in the grip of his hand. He treats it as a stay less than temporary, hoping from it for no more than a little shade over his head for a short time, then he leaves it and departs, ever looking forward to the time when he will set out afresh and complete his journey.

### Ramadan: servitude and lack of self-sufficiency

The Exalted said: ﴿كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ﴾ [البقرة: 183] (decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous). O Allah, to You is the praise for what You honored us with, of performing the prayers, the fasting, the night prayer of Ramadan and the recitation of the Qur'an, and of the raising of our hands in supplication to You, and of a sincerity that we reckon You placed in our hearts, and of the earnestness in supplication by which we drew near to You, and of a good opinion of You. Glory be to You, accept it from us, and we ask You, our Lord, for the continuance of Your covering and the completeness of Your well-being.

That we could do without the Creator is a matter I believe we are all certain is impossible. Does anyone think he can do without his Creator in his breaths? In the movement of his feet? In his hand that carries its watch? In his muscles? In his ability to think and to express? In his feeling of love for those around him? In hating what is hateful and loving the truth? And if we are certain that we cannot do without the Creator, then we are in need of His giving, so let us be certain that what is with Allah is not attained except by obeying Him, glory be to Him. Ramadan is servitude to Allah, and just as no one but Allah looks into the fast, so too no one looks into hearts but Allah. So if the fast has taught you this, then it must have had an effect inside the heart so that the heart stays attached to one God, certain that none looks into what is in it but Him, glory be to Him.

And let us know that in proportion to the attachment of your heart to Allah is your longing to meet Him. And the meeting with Allah is not truly realized except by death, but it has stage-by-stage realizations, represented in your servitude to the Lord of the worlds.

### Six from Shawwāl

And the Prophet ﷺ said: «Whoever fasts Ramadan and then follows it with six days of Shawwāl, it is as if he fasted the whole of time.»[^5] This is a generosity from Allah, which He grants you as a gift, and Allah treats us in proportion to how we treat Him.

And it was his habit ﷺ to fast, and he did not refrain from fasting, and he balanced between the needs of his body and the needs of his spirit. For attending to only one of the two sides cancels the effectiveness of the other. A man has a job and a home: if he attends only to his job and neglects his home he loses it, and if he attends entirely to his family and neglects his job he loses it. So too the human being has a self, a spirit and a body: if he deals with his body, so that he rests a great deal, eats a great deal and enjoys a great deal, he loses his spirit, and if he deals only with his spirit he loses his body. And many of the people of knowledge who speak about asceticism give the preponderance to the spirit even if they neglect the body beside it, and they did not suffer much loss. But the loss comes to someone like us: we pay great attention to the details and particulars of the worldly life, and we forget or neglect the matters of the Hereafter.
