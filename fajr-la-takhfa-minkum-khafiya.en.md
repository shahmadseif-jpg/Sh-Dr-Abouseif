And bless for us, O Allah, what You have provided us, and protect us, O Allah, from the punishment of the Fire. Then we pause with the words of Allah the Exalted: ﴿يَوْمَئِذٍ تُعْرَضُونَ لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌ﴾ (“On that Day you will be presented; not a secret of yours will remain hidden”)[^1]

### A Pause at the Word “Khāfiyah”

We stop at the word in an attempt to grasp its meaning. “Khāfiyah” differs in wording from “tukhfūn” (“you hide”). If we say “you hide,” there is an effort made by the person to conceal something, and that thing may not be concealable in the first place. Someone who has something large tries to put it in his pocket, and it remains visible and prominent no matter how much he tries to quiet it. But something very small, which, wherever it is placed, disappears from sight, is a “khāfiyah.”

### Questions About Concealment

If we say “khāfiyah,” we have a set of questions. The first: why did its owner try to conceal it? The answer is that he does not want it to show; he has contraband, so he puts it in the “magic” pocket inside the bag, among the hidden corridors, and tries to stash it away. And yet “it does not remain hidden”: the scanner reveals it even if the human eye does not, and if the scanner does not reveal it, they have other means that sniff out the smell and bring the thing out. So we are before causes by which hidden things, the concealed items inside other things, are discovered.

So what will we want to conceal on the Day of Resurrection before Allah, glory be to Him, the Exalted, when He says: ﴿يَوْمَئِذٍ تُعْرَضُونَ﴾ (“On that Day you will be presented”)?

### The Scene at the Checkpoint

The difficulty is at the moment you walk up to the inspection point; that moment alone carries awe and gravity, and by it the one who hid something is distinguished from the one who did not. Cameras and surveillance devices watch you, in an airport for example, and there is someone looking at the people passing by and reading faces; if he suspects anyone he stops him, and with the procedures, other troubles besides the matter of hiding something may come to light.

If this is so in this world, then His words ﴿يَوْمَئِذٍ تُعْرَضُونَ﴾ ask: before whom will you be presented? Before whom will you stand? And what are the instruments for detecting lying, deceit, fraud, cheating and concealment?

Imagine a person doing something wrong while knowing there is an ambush on the road, and that they have received word that something prohibited will pass through. If he understands that there is an ambush and a lookout, he will understand at once that the one standing there is not merely a policeman; there is an expert in every detail of what might be concealed, especially what they have been told has been reported. This is the reality we live in.

So when you say “On that Day you will be presented”: who is waiting for you at that moment? Who will look into your hidden things? He who knows the secret and what is even more hidden, glory be to Him, exalted in His majesty. So, by Allah, what sane person carries drugs with him while passing through an ambush, knowing that those standing there are specialists in this field? You have brought them only to hand yourself in!

So when the servant understands the meaning of ﴿لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌ﴾ (“not a secret of yours will remain hidden”), he has known that the “khāfiyah,” which is what has reached such a degree of concealment that it does not appear in itself, “does not remain hidden” either; and if we say that it does not remain hidden, then there is an eye that sees it, all-seeing, all-aware.

### The One Given His Record in His Right Hand

﴿فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ فَيَقُولُ هَاؤُمُ اقْرَءُوا كِتَابِيَهْ﴾ (“As for he who is given his record in his right hand, he will say: Here, read my record”)[^2] He boasts of his righteous deeds. But the question is: among whom? He shouts on the Day of Resurrection, calling out to the people: Rejoice with me! And who will rejoice with you? Without doubt, and Allah the Exalted knows best, he is calling those who knew him in his life, those who lived alongside him and who bore witness for him of good, or even of evil, those who were accustomed to hearing a kind word from him and those who were troubled by some of his positions and circumstances. It is as though he is saying to them: How are you today? Read my record, my page is white!

So what had he done before Allah, Blessed and Exalted? ﴿إِنِّي ظَنَنتُ أَنِّي مُلَاقٍ حِسَابِيَهْ﴾ (“Indeed, I was certain that I would meet my account”). And “zann” (“thinking”), when it occurs in the Qur'an on the tongue of the people of faith, means certainty: I was certain that I would meet my account.

If it was in the sense of certainty, why was the word “zann” used? Because this matter is a matter of conjecture for other people, and the believer's conjecture is not the disbeliever's conjecture. The believer's conjecture, even when he thinks ill, carries him toward the possible good aspects in it, and he accuses no one. This is his makeup and his mold; he walks along this line and knows no other, so his eye does not fall on evil or indecency, nor does he look upon wrongdoing, except in the course of attempts at reform, and attempts at reform are not made through violence or accusation but through reform itself.

### A Pleasant Life

So if we review His words ﴿لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌ﴾ (“not a secret of yours will remain hidden”) and see this great scene before the Lord of the worlds, and then the words of the one given his record, ﴿إِنِّي ظَنَنتُ أَنِّي مُلَاقٍ حِسَابِيَهْ﴾ (“Indeed, I was certain that I would meet my account”), Allah says: ﴿فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٍ رَّاضِيَةٍ﴾ (“So he will be in a pleasant life”)[^3]

Who is the one who is pleased here, to begin with? And who is it that should be pleased? It seems that he is pleased with his life, but the life itself is “pleased” (“rāḍiyah”): as though He is telling you that this life is comfortable in itself, so everyone who comes to it finds ease and no one is made miserable by it. In this world, a person may find comfort in a place where another is made miserable: one loves engineering, is passionately devoted to it and sees himself in it, and another, forced into it, sees it as the hardest work in the world. One loves to sit here and speak a few words, at ease and happy, feeling that he is offering a service, and another, compelled, attends and endures. But there, the life itself is pleased and at ease, so everyone who comes to it is pleased.

We ask Allah, glory be to Him, to bless us and you in what lies before us of avenues of good and its means, to make our hearts and minds steadfast in obedience to Him, to cleanse our hearts, minds and bodies of whatever angers Him, glory be to Him, and that we meet Him on the Day of Resurrection with sound hearts, pure souls and minds that are tranquil, confident and certain. O Allah, amen. And we ask Him, on this noble day, to grant us and you lawful, good, blessed provision, by His mercy.
