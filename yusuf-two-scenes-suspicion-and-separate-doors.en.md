In the story recited in the Fajr prayer we have two very quick and simple moments at which we pause; and both situations create suspicion in people's minds.

### The First Scene: "Bring Me a Brother of Yours"

﴿ائْتُونِي بِأَخٍ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَبِيكُمْ أَلَا تَرَوْنَ أَنِّي أُوفِي الْكَيْلَ وَأَنَا خَيْرُ الْمُنْزِلِينَ﴾ ("Bring me a brother of yours from your father. Do you not see that I give full measure and that I am the best of hosts?") then: ﴿فَإِنْ لَمْ تَأْتُونِي بِهِ فَلَا كَيْلَ لَكُمْ عِنْدِي وَلَا تَقْرَبُونِ﴾ ("But if you do not bring him to me, there will be no measure for you with me, and do not approach me.").[^1]

What is happening? Yūsuf, peace be upon him, asks this group of men, who came claiming to be sons of Yaʿqūb and that they had come from such-and-such a place and were asking for food, to bring an additional brother. So why does he ask this of them? And why does he threaten them that if they do not bring him they will get no food? Do you see this as justice? This is a suspicion in people's minds: why does he insist on this brother, such that if they do not bring him there is no measure for them?

The interpretation of this scene, in very simple terms, is that they claimed to be, say, twelve. Let us say the tribe is fifty or a hundred people, and the statement of particulars submitted to the government says a hundred men. When we tally and count, and we have a rough example, you said you were twelve brothers, so where are they? You are eleven, so show me the twelfth. So it is as though the matter came from Yūsuf, peace be upon him, by way of a survey and an inquiry into the particulars laid out before us in the papers.

This is like going to get assistance from the government and saying: I have five children, and it appears you have four, so where is the fifth? Bring me proof. And you go to many government offices and the clerk says to you: I want proof that you are the same person, give me this and that, especially in sensitive places, and in this great crisis that had encompassed that land. So Yūsuf, peace be upon him, framed the request to see his brother, but in a legal form, in a form that did not place him in a position of suspicion with these people: if you do not bring him to me, there is no measure for you with me, and do not come near this place ever.

### Their Merchandise in Their Saddlebags

And he put their merchandise in their saddlebags. What does that mean? They had come offering what they had of money to buy food with and to return with provisions of food and drink, so he put their money back in their saddlebags. When they returned they found their merchandise in their saddlebags and said: Our father, this is our merchandise, returned to us. That is, he had not accepted the transaction.

And how did he send them back without food? No, he did not send them back without food; rather he sent them back with a small provision, as though he gave them enough food to cover these people's need until they came back to him with the proofs. And the proof this time is not on paper but practical: I named ten persons in my file, and I want to prove that I have ten persons. In this way any suspicion is removed from the attempt of Yūsuf, peace be upon him, to get his brother through some pressure on these people; for when you speak about this matter you are speaking about a hunger that twists the bellies of people for a set period. So he asked for legal proof, then gave them enough money for this interval in which they would return to the government to prove the claim recorded in the government papers.

### The Second Scene: "Do Not Enter by One Gate"

﴿لَا تَدْخُلُوا مِنْ بَابٍ وَاحِدٍ وَادْخُلُوا مِنْ أَبْوَابٍ مُتَفَرِّقَةٍ وَمَا أُغْنِي عَنْكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ مِنْ شَيْءٍ إِنِ الْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَعَلَيْهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ الْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ﴾ ("Do not enter by one gate, but enter by different gates; and I cannot avail you against Allah at all. Judgment is only for Allah. Upon Him I have relied, and upon Him let those who would rely rely.").[^2]

What did Yaʿqūb intend? What information have we inherited? That he feared envy for them. That is all the information we have: that Yaʿqūb, peace be upon him, feared envy for them. But when I read the events I see that these people were asked for more evidence.

A friend of mine says he applied for the "Green Card" twenty years ago. I asked him: how twenty years? He told me a very simple sentence: Listen, if doubt arises at the start of the application, the file takes another track; every move you submit and every paper for which more proofs are requested is more than for the one who went along the first, sound track. So a preliminary check takes place: the papers are in order, the account is logical. And likewise at the airport: you put your bag through the scanner and it goes along, then suddenly it is diverted to another track, so you get ready and stand before the officer because you know this bag will be searched. Why did the track change? Because we have a kind of doubt about something that is not in order, and I need to inquire about it.

So if these men were put in the category of survey, they became subjects of accusation rather than of trust. If they came again as a whole group, the government's first look at them would be as though they had come under watch, and accordingly they would have to justify all their positions. This is like what is said in some circumstances, that there are to be no gatherings of more than three persons, and this is used either to preserve security or to threaten security; so we have a legitimate purpose behind this tracking. So they became tracked, and Yaʿqūb, peace be upon him, did not know the reality of the matter up to that moment, but on the basis of his inference from the situation he warned them against a gathering that would stir the security sense of the guards who received the delegations coming to this country. That we enter three or four or two or five is better than that we all enter, appearing as a group entering to assault something or to take their right by force.

### "A Need in the Soul of Yaʿqūb Which He Fulfilled"

When the Lord of the worlds, glory be to Him and exalted, called it ﴿إِلَّا حَاجَةً فِي نَفْسِ يَعْقُوبَ قَضَاهَا﴾ ("except a need within the soul of Yaʿqūb, which he satisfied")[^3] this understanding of the situation is derived from the connotations of the Qur'anic words. So why was the word "qaḍāhā" used? And what does this word, with its connotations, have to do with this context? It is tied to the context of tracking that existed for these people since the ruling was issued against them that they would not be given food like the rest of the people. We have doubt, we have suspicion here, a criminal suspicion in the matter: these people are circumventing the law to obtain food. Someone might say: the circumvention of the law will occur with just one man, so do not hold them to account, our master. It is answered: if we want to apply a principle, we apply it at all levels, and one always begins with a small case and then what lies behind it comes; the rope is pulled afterward. Glory be to Allah.

### Conclusion

I only wanted us to recall together how wise Yūsuf, peace be upon him, was in his conduct, and when I read Yaʿqūb's position I understand where Yūsuf got this wisdom from. And we have a third situation that will happen shortly: he puts the drinking cup in his brother's saddlebag, then ﴿أَيَّتُهَا الْعِيرُ إِنَّكُمْ لَسَارِقُونَ﴾ ("O caravan, indeed you are thieves"); so when it comes to us in the recitation, God willing, we will pause at it. May Allah bless you, and reward you with good, and make you happy.
