There is a question that is timeless: what is the secret of this spot? What is the source of excitement that makes hearts yearn for it and minds cling to it, and souls never cease remembering it and longing for it? The season of Ḥajj is at the door, in which people move from distant horizons to the precincts of the House of the Lord of the worlds, and we ask Allah to bring us to it. I have chosen for today's sermon a title that suits what we are approaching: "Awakened Consciences."

Praise be to Allah, we praise Him, seek His help, seek His guidance and seek His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Allah from the evils of our souls and from the wrongs of our deeds; whomever Allah guides, none can misguide, and whomever He leaves astray, none can guide. And we bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that our master Muhammad is His servant, His prophet and His messenger ﷺ. To proceed,

A movement went on across thousands of years, of a very small group of individuals, in an environment empty of bustle and crowd: a simple logic and an easy language, a calm in every movement, and complete focus on what was happening on the ground, with no distractions but the slightest. And at the center of this movement was Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, then Hājar, then Ismāʿīl. And I want to stop at one thing that unites them: the conscience.

### Good news that tests hearts

Good news came to Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, and receiving good news is a kind of test. Among people are those who are given good news and become greedy and ask for more; and among them are those who are given good news and are distressed because what they were told is less than their ambition; and among them are those whose eye is on the Giver and not on the gift, so he is content with Him, little or much, late or early, and appreciates the wisdom of his Giver. And so you see Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, in the scene of al-Dhāriyāt, did not comment at all on the good news of his son, but his wife commented: an old woman, there is no logic in the matter.[^4] As if Ibrāhīm's conscience was moving inside him with reassurance and steadiness: the blessing of my Lord I do not reject, I do not deny, and I do not hasten for it, though I long for it.

### A valley with nothing in it

And in another scene this old man is commanded to take his infant Ismāʿīl and his mother Hājar to a land in which there is nothing. No rescue aircraft, no support operations, no instant communications, no radars watching. A measure of water in a vessel that will run out after a while, and food that no one knows how it would be kept. And the scene in itself, if looked at by minds attached only to matter, is a deliberate suicide operation, or a man killing his wife and son by leaving them there and going away: premeditated killing. This is how bare human minds think. But the believer has a conscience that has covenanted with Allah to keep to what He commands him, even if it is harsh on the soul, even if he does not grasp it immediately and logically. I have believed, and faith has requirements, the first of which is submission. And submission does not know: why, nor how, nor when, nor what; these are questions that do not suit submission whatever the judgment and decree of Allah may be. I have believed, and that is all.

Then the moments are renewed, and we see the man going and coming from time to time, no one knows when but Allah, yet he moves in a motion whose orders issue from the sky. The woman is not dearer to him than the command of Allah, nor the child more tightly attached to him than it. He proceeds on a compass moved by the sky, and his conscience does not let him act contrary to the command of Allah even for a moment.

And here you move with your mind and heart to a woman who has become alone, and her title is submission. She asks: to whom are you leaving us? Then she asks: Did Allah command you this? He said: Yes. She said: Then He will not neglect us.[^1] Why did this question come from her? Because she knew that her husband does not move on his own, and that he is for her the source of trust, who does not act randomly nor move from his own head, but is a Muslim, peaceable man who lives life under the guidance of Allah. So the answer came to her: Yes. And her answer was the more wondrous: Then He will not neglect us. What conscience inside this human being makes her look around her and see nothing but Allah's open space and the vastness of His universe, and yet submit, because a word came out of her husband's tongue: Allah commanded me?

And he returns and sees the woman as he left her: with her chastity, her guarding of herself and her protection of her child, the firmness of her religion and the strength of her character. She did not deviate because her husband was absent for years or many months, Allah knows. And you know how much time the distance takes by walking on a camel or a horse, so what of one who went out on a mission and a message, not knowing when he would return, and who would not return until he had carried it out? And the woman was alone, with nothing with her but this newborn, and those who came afterward came because of the presence of water. How did the conscience of motherhood speak, and how did the restraint of religiosity move in this soul that Allah protected? The causes of temptation did not move her to look right or left, nor to change her decisions in life. She is Allah's maidservant, not a prisoner of the body nor of desire. She did not turn into a browser of the screen, watching indecent or measured scenes, and enjoying moments of love and infatuation taking place between actors on a screen, small or large. She did not do that, but her conscience stood.

### Then comes the command to slaughter

And after long years another command comes: Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, visits us with a new message, in which he is commanded to slaughter his son after this age. He is a messenger of the Ulū al-ʿAzm, he takes in the divine command and commits to it and carries it out, neither delaying nor deviating nor disputing nor debating. Though the excuses are many, and he is the master of logic in the world, who taught the world how dialogue is, how the methods of persuasion are, and how the context runs to where he wants, yet he did not debate his Lord over a single letter. And had he wished he would have delayed the execution for a while in the hope that Allah would be gentle with him, but he did not.

And if we say that this mother lived a long time with one of Allah's prophets, so she acquired from his character and absorbed from his traits and became accustomed to his pattern, we would say she is at the same level of submission. But what do we say of a boy who lived from childhood relying on himself by his Lord's leave, whose father visits him from time to time to check on his condition, then a command comes to him: you are to be slaughtered tonight? What logic is there? But Ismāʿīl, peace be upon him, was raised before everything on a conscience moving within him.

### "Rather, man is a witness against himself"

And when you open the Muṣḥaf and look at the verses that speak of the matter of the conscience, you find the words of Allah, the Exalted: ﴿بَلِ الْإِنسَانُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ بَصِيرَةٌ ۝ وَلَوْ أَلْقَىٰ مَعَاذِيرَهُ﴾ [القيامة: 14-15] (Rather, man, against himself, will be a witness, even if he presents his excuses). In Sūrat al-Qiyāma, which is one of the names of the Last Day. For the conscience does not move while asleep, but it must have a blazing wakefulness and continuous alertness. "Man is a baṣīra against himself": his eye is open, not outward, not to the color of your clothes or the conditions of people, but inward. And had the word been "mubṣir" it would have served the purpose, but it is "baṣīra," and baṣīra means the complete comprehension of all the particulars of what is in front of me, so I know my soul and its movement and its fluctuations and its inclination and its crookedness and its uprightness. And when our Lord knew of us the seeking of excuses with every obligation that comes to us, He followed the first verse with the second: ﴿وَلَوْ أَلْقَىٰ مَعَاذِيرَهُ﴾ (even if he presents his excuses).

Then another matter comes: ﴿لَا تُحَرِّكْ بِهِ لِسَانَكَ لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِ ۝ إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُ وَقُرْآنَهُ ۝ فَإِذَا قَرَأْنَاهُ فَاتَّبِعْ قُرْآنَهُ ۝ ثُمَّ إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا بَيَانَهُ﴾ [القيامة: 16-19] (Move not your tongue with it, [O Muhammad], to hasten with recitation of the Qur'an Indeed, upon Us is its collection [in your heart] and [to make possible] its recitation. So when We have recited it [through Gabriel], then follow its recitation. Then upon Us is its clarification [to you]). So what is the connection? As if my Lord is teaching me that this is a model of the drive with which awakened consciences move. Our Messenger ﷺ did not wait until Jibrīl finished his revelation, but wanted to keep up with him out of eagerness that nothing be missed, and our Lord says to him: We will deposit everything with you. And that is the conscience that is keen on the soundness of the meaning and the firmness of the structure. It is the conscience that does not merely move, but also moves others.

Then: ﴿كَلَّا بَلْ تُحِبُّونَ الْعَاجِلَةَ﴾ [القيامة: 20] (No! But you love the immediate). Here consciences are assaulted. Great wealth, the glitter of gold, the gleam of silver, and the excitement of the dollar, the idol of the masses, make the immediate silence the conscience: wait a little and we will talk later. It silences the conscience and mutes the tongues, and pushes desire toward more acquisition, justified or unjustified. And the sūra itself our Lord opened with His words: ﴿لَا أُقْسِمُ بِيَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ ۝ وَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِالنَّفْسِ اللَّوَّامَةِ﴾ [القيامة: 1-2] (I swear by the Day of Resurrection, and I swear by the self-reproaching soul). So the possibility of error in our lives is not remote, but what is remote is that the error not be accompanied by a conscience that stops its owner from time to time: take care, my son, there is an indicator giving off a warning. And this indicator is the conscience.

### A living conscience makes dignity

Whoever's conscience has died has died with a stay of execution on the rest of his body. And whoever's conscience is alive is alive even if his body is disabled from movement. And whoever rouses the conscience in himself lives with dignity all his life, neither dying nor being humbled, because he is connected to the sky, his supply is from his Lord and his provision from his Creator, he does not need a hand extended to people, but his hand is always raised upward, not receiving on the horizontal level but receiving from the sky. And that is the greatest gift a human being obtains in life.

And you see the people of conscience from time to time withdrawing from people, not enduring a great deal of mixing. And, by Allah, I feel for that Companion who came to the Prophet ﷺ saying: O Messenger of Allah, I wanted to withdraw from people and stay in my house. And the Prophet ﷺ encouraged him to endure with what carried him to more striving and perseverance, for mixing with people and being patient with their harm is better for you,[^2] as if he were saying to him: seek help from Allah, for the support is from Him.

And our consciences we carry as fathers who do not neglect our dependents, as husbands who do not neglect our wives, and the consciences of our sisters as wives who do not neglect their men, and as owners of businesses, whether owners or workers, and as bearers of a message about Islam in this land. Let that never be absent from your mind. We are not here for a morsel that will go to a place worse than the one by which it entered the body, nor for money that they know how to give us and take back again, nor for the enjoyment of this world, for bodies, however much they enjoy, will return to under the earth. We are here for a purpose, and whoever's aim is lost, his identity is lost, and whoever's identity is lost is lost in the midst of the rest of the people.

And the acts of worship are all a system working in the first place on awakening consciences. Allah commanded you to come to the mosque five times in the day and night, so your whole life stops, like a "refresh" that returns to your awareness the accomplishment of your conscience. And in the prayer you watch the word, the movement, the stillness, the standing, the bowing and the prostration, and you watch the verses you recite, and you know what spoils your prayer and what saves it, and it is a kindling of awareness that happens five times a day. Then a training course comes to you in the year, in which Allah commands you to refrain from the lawful so that this conscience awakens in you and lives again. When the conscience is upright, life is upright, the compass of the world is set, and the servant reaches his aim in establishing himself before Allah.

### A little patience

My brothers, one of us spends his life from the first grade of primary school until his aim is achieved on the day he obtains the doctorate. He imagines all this time for the sake of that moment, so why do we not exercise a little patience until we reach the place of honor before Allah? And I admonish myself before others: if there is a doubtful matter of wealth in our hands, leaving it for Allah's sake is more honorable and noble. And if there is a temptation in our hands that turns us away from our homes, then seeking protection with the Lord of the worlds is more fitting and more eloquent. And if it is a temptation of desire or a deviation in conduct that none knows but Allah, then patience is sweeter in taste at the end and more hopeful for reward at the greatest meeting. When you meet Allah and stand before Him, He draws over you from His covering and engulfs you with His mercy, and reminds you of what He had covered for you in the world, and says: and today I cover it for you,[^3] take him to Paradise. And it is said to you: ﴿سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ﴾ [الزمر: 73] (Peace be upon you; you have become pure; so enter it to abide eternally therein). So do not seek any aim other than this. And if those who died came back again they would tell us about the pettiness of this world. And the older we grow, the more maturity increases and the faculties of perceiving the reality of life multiply, so today we laugh at what we quarreled over yesterday. And a prompt awakening is better than coming regret.

O Allah, we ask You not to let our consciences die, and to awaken them in our souls as long as we live. Our Lord, come between us and what angers You, make our hearts sound for us, and preserve our well-being for us. O Allah, give us security in ourselves and our families, draw over us Your covering, and mend our brokenness and our hearts, O Most Merciful of the merciful. O Allah, grant Palestine and its people covering and help, be for every oppressed person on the earth, protect the blood of the Muslims in every land and time, and make us firm with the firm word in the life of this world and in the Hereafter.
