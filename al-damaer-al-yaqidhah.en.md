> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T2YWPryFN3c).

There is a question that has lasted through the ages: What is the secret of this place? What draws hearts towards it, holds minds to it, and keeps souls remembering and longing? The Hajj season is approaching, when people travel from distant lands to the House of the Lord of all worlds. We ask Allah to let us reach it. I have chosen a title for today's sermon that suits what lies ahead: “Awake Consciences.”

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. Whoever Allah guides, no one can lead astray; whoever He leads astray, no one can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that our master Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

Across thousands of years, a very small number of people carried out a course of action in a setting free of bustle and crowds. Their reasoning was simple, their language clear, and every movement calm. They gave their full attention to what was happening on the ground, with very few distractions. At the centre of this movement was Ibrahim, peace be upon him, then Hajar, then Ismail. I want to consider one thing they shared: conscience.

### Good News That Tests Hearts

Ibrahim, peace be upon him, received glad tidings. Accepting good news is itself a kind of test. Some people receive it and become greedy, asking for more. Others become distressed because what they have been promised falls short of their hopes. Others keep their eyes on the Giver rather than the gift. They are content with it, little or much, early or late, and recognise the wisdom of the One who gives. That is why, in the scene in Surat al-Dhariyat, you see no comment from Ibrahim, peace be upon him, on the news of his son. It is his wife who comments: an old woman—this seems to make no sense.[^4] It is as though Ibrahim's conscience speaks within him with calm and assurance: I will not reject or deny my Lord's blessing, nor demand that it come sooner, even though I long for it.

### A Valley with Nothing in It

In another scene, this elderly man is commanded to take his infant Ismail and the child's mother, Hajar, to a land where there is nothing. No rescue aircraft, no support operations, no instant communications, no radar watching over them. A little water in a container will soon run out, and there is food they do not know how to preserve. To minds concerned only with material things, the scene itself looks like deliberate suicide, or a man killing his wife and child by leaving them there and walking away: premeditated killing. That is how unaided human minds think. But the believer has a conscience that has pledged to Allah to obey His commands, even when they are hard on the soul, even when the believer cannot immediately make sense of the situation or its reasoning. I have believed, and belief has requirements. The first is submission. Submission does not ask: Why? How? When? What? Such questions do not belong with submission, whatever Allah's ruling and decree may be. I have believed. That is enough.

Time moves on, and we see the man coming and going from time to time. Only Allah knows when. But his movements follow commands issued from heaven. The woman is not dearer to him than Allah's command, nor is his attachment to the child greater than his attachment to it. He follows a compass directed by heaven, and his conscience will not let him act against Allah's command, even for a moment.

Now turn your mind and heart to a woman who has been left alone, whose defining quality is submission. She asks: Who are you leaving us to? Then she asks: Has Allah commanded you to do this? He says: Yes. She says: Then He will not let us be lost.[^1] Why did she ask that question? Because she knew that her husband did not act on his own initiative. To her, he was a source of trust. He did not behave at random or act on ideas of his own. He was a Muslim, a peaceful man, living under Allah's guidance. The answer came: Yes. Her answer was even more remarkable: Then He will not let us be lost. What kind of conscience lived inside this woman, who looked around and saw nothing but Allah's open space and the vastness of His creation, yet submitted because of a statement from her husband's lips: Allah has commanded me?

He returns and finds the woman as he left her: chaste, guarding herself and caring for her child, firm in her faith and strong in character. She did not go astray because her husband was absent for years or many months—Allah knows best. You know how long that distance takes on a camel or a horse. What, then, of someone who has left on a task and a mission, not knowing when he will return, and who will only return after completing it? The woman was alone with this newborn. Those who came later came because there was water. How did her mother's conscience speak, and how did the restraint of faith work within this soul whom Allah protected? Temptations did not move her to look this way or that, or to change her decisions in life. She was Allah's servant, not a prisoner of the body or desire. She did not become someone scrolling through a screen, watching scenes, indecent or otherwise, and enjoying moments of love and passion acted out by performers on a screen, small or large. She did not do that. Her conscience stood firm.

### Then Comes the Command to Sacrifice

After many long years comes another command. Ibrahim, peace be upon him, appears before us with a new message: he is commanded to sacrifice his son after all this time. He is one of the messengers of firm resolve. He understands the divine command, commits himself to it and carries it out. He does not delay, turn aside, argue or debate. There were many possible excuses. He was the great teacher of reasoning in the world, who taught people how to hold a discussion, how to persuade, and how to guide an argument towards its purpose. Yet he did not dispute a single word with his Lord. If he had wished, he could have delayed carrying it out for a while, hoping Allah would ease it for him. But he did not.

We might say that this mother had lived for a long time with one of Allah's prophets. She had learned from his character, absorbed his qualities and grown used to his way of life, so we could understand her reaching the same level of submission. But what of a boy who had grown up relying on himself, by his Lord's permission, whose father visited from time to time to see how he was doing, and who then receives this command: Tonight you are to be sacrificed? What reasoning can explain that? Yet Ismail, peace be upon him, had been raised, before anything else, with a conscience alive within him.

### “Rather, the Human Being Is a Witness against Himself”

When you open the Quran and look at verses about conscience, you find Allah's words, translated in meaning: “Rather, the human being is a witness against himself, even if he offers his excuses” [al-Qiyamah 75:14–15]. This is in Surat al-Qiyamah, the Resurrection, one of the names of the Last Day. Conscience cannot act while asleep. It needs a keen readiness and constant wakefulness. A person is a witness against himself: his eyes are open, looking not outward at the colour of your clothes or other people's affairs, but inward. Even a word meaning “one who sees” would have served the purpose. But the word is *basirah*: an insight that fully takes in every detail before me. I know myself, my movements and changes, my leanings, my crookedness and my uprightness. Our Lord knew that we would seek excuses whenever a duty came to us, so He followed the first verse with the second, in meaning: “Even if he offers his excuses.”

Then comes another command, translated in meaning: “Do not move your tongue with it to hurry it along. It is for Us to gather it and have it recited. When We have recited it, follow its recitation. Then it is for Us to explain it” [al-Qiyamah 75:16–19]. What is the connection? It is as though my Lord is teaching me that this is an example of the resolve that moves awake consciences. Our Messenger ﷺ could not wait for Jibril to finish conveying the revelation. He wanted to keep up with him, anxious not to miss anything. Our Lord tells him: We will entrust everything to you. This is the conscience concerned with sound meaning and sound expression. It is a conscience that not only moves, but moves others too.

Then, in a translation of the meaning: “No! Rather, you love the immediate life” [al-Qiyamah 75:20]. Here consciences are destroyed. Great wealth, the glitter of gold, the shine of silver, and the excitement of the dollar, worshipped by the crowds, allow the immediate life to silence conscience: Wait a little; we will talk later. It silences conscience and stills tongues, while pushing desire to gather more, justified or not. Our Lord began this same surah with words meaning: “I swear by the Day of Resurrection, and I swear by the self-reproaching soul” [al-Qiyamah 75:1–2]. The possibility of making mistakes in life is not remote. What should be unthinkable is a mistake without a conscience that stops its owner from time to time: Be careful, my son. An indicator is sounding an alarm. That indicator is conscience.

### A Living Conscience Creates Dignity

Someone whose conscience has died is dead, with the sentence merely suspended for the rest of his body. Someone whose conscience lives is alive, even if his body can no longer move. Whoever stirs his conscience into action lives with dignity throughout his life. He does not die or become humiliated, because he is connected to heaven. His support comes from his Lord and his provision from his Creator. He does not need a hand stretched out towards people. His hand is always raised upwards. He does not receive on the horizontal level; he receives from heaven. This is the greatest gift a person can gain in life.

From time to time, you see people of conscience withdraw from others, unable to bear too much company. By Allah, I feel for that Companion who came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: Messenger of Allah, I want to withdraw from people and stay at home. The Prophet ﷺ encouraged him to be patient, urging him to keep striving and persevering: mixing with people and patiently bearing their harm is better for you.[^2] It is as though he says to him: Seek Allah's help, for support comes from Him.

We carry our consciences as fathers who do not neglect their children, as husbands who do not neglect their wives, and our sisters carry theirs as wives who do not neglect their husbands. We carry them in our work, whether owners or employees, and as people entrusted with a message about Islam in this land. Never let that leave your mind. We are not here for a mouthful of food that will leave the body in a worse place than where it entered. Nor are we here for money that others know how to give us and take away again, or for worldly enjoyment. However much our bodies enjoy, they will return beneath the soil. We are here for a purpose. Whoever loses his purpose loses his identity; whoever loses his identity becomes lost among everyone else.

All acts of worship form a system whose first task is to wake consciences. Allah commands you to come to the mosque five times each day and night, bringing your whole life to a pause. It is like a “refresh” that brings the work of your conscience back into your awareness. In prayer, you watch each word, movement, stillness, standing, bowing and prostration. You attend to the verses you recite, knowing what invalidates your prayer and what preserves it. This kindles awareness five times a day. Then comes a training course each year, when Allah commands you to refrain from what is lawful, so that this conscience wakes within you and lives again. When conscience is upright, life becomes upright. The compass of worldly life is set right, and the servant reaches his purpose in standing before Allah as he should.

### Be Patient a Little Longer

My brothers, a person spends his life from the first year of primary school until he achieves his aim and receives a doctorate. He can imagine going through all that time for that moment. Why, then, can we not be patient a little longer until we reach a place of honour before Allah? I advise myself before anyone else. If money of doubtful lawfulness lies before us, leaving it for Allah is more dignified and honourable. If a temptation draws us away from our homes, holding firmly to the Lord of all worlds is better and more fitting. If it is a temptation of desire or a hidden fault in conduct known only to Allah, being patient will taste sweeter in the end and hold greater hope of reward at the great meeting. When you meet Allah and stand before Him, He draws His covering over you, surrounds you with His mercy, reminds you of what He concealed for you in this world, and says: Today I will conceal it for you too.[^3] Take him to Paradise. You are told, in the meaning of the verse: “Peace be upon you. You have become pure, so enter it to remain forever” [al-Zumar 39:73]. Seek no purpose other than this. If those who have died came back, they would tell us how little this world is worth. As we grow older, our judgement improves and our ability to grasp the truth of life grows. Today we laugh at what we fought over yesterday. Waking up now is better than regretting later.

O Allah, we ask You not to let our consciences die, but to keep them awake within us for as long as we live. Our Lord, stand between us and what angers You. Keep our hearts sound and preserve our well-being. O Allah, grant safety to us and our families, draw Your covering over us, mend our brokenness and comfort our hearts, Most Merciful of those who show mercy. O Allah, grant protection and help to Palestine and its people. Stand by everyone oppressed on earth, protect Muslim lives in every place and time, and make us firm with the steadfast word in this worldly life and in the Hereafter.

[^4]: Editor's note: The glad tidings in this scene concern Ishaq, peace be upon him, and the wife who expressed amazement was Sarah (Hud 11:71; al-Dhariyat 51:28–29). In Surat al-Hijr, Ibrahim, peace be upon him, asked about these glad tidings in amazement, in the meaning of the verse: “Do you give me good news when old age has reached me? What good news are you bringing me?” (al-Hijr 15:54). This was a question of amazement, not objection. The infant whom Ibrahim was commanded to settle with his mother in the valley was Ismail, and his mother was Hajar. That is a different story from the glad tidings of Ishaq. The body text was therefore amended where it had made that same infant the result of these glad tidings.
