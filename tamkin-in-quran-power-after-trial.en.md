*A Qur'anic study of tamkīn (empowerment), a term attributed first to God and then used as a measure for the affairs of states and individuals. By Dr. Ahmed Muhammad Ali Abouseif, President of the American Imams Academy (AIA).*

Yūsuf (Joseph), peace be upon him, stands before the king, having only just come out of prison, and the king says to him: ﴿إِنَّكَ الْيَوْمَ لَدَيْنَا مَكِينٌ أَمِينٌ﴾ ("Today you are, in our presence, established and trustworthy") (Yūsuf 12:54). Then Yūsuf asks to be placed over the storehouses of the land: ﴿إِنِّي حَفِيظٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾ ("I am a knowing guardian") (Yūsuf 12:55). And once that is done, the Qur'an comments in words that return the credit to its true owner: ﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَكَّنَّا لِيُوسُفَ فِي الْأَرْضِ يَتَبَوَّأُ مِنْهَا حَيْثُ يَشَاءُ ۚ نُصِيبُ بِرَحْمَتِنَا مَن نَّشَاءُ وَلَا نُضِيعُ أَجْرَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ﴾ ("And thus We established Yūsuf in the land, to settle in it wherever he willed. We grant Our mercy to whom We will, and We do not let the reward of those who do good go to waste") (Yūsuf 12:56). At the very beginning of the story there had already been a promise built on the same root: ﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَكَّنَّا لِيُوسُفَ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلِنُعَلِّمَهُ مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ﴾ ("And thus We established Yūsuf in the land, so that We might teach him the interpretation of events") (Yūsuf 12:21). So in this sūrah tamkīn is a promise at the start and a fulfilment in the middle, and between the two lie the well, slavery, temptation and prison.

### The Linguistic Root and Its Meaning

The root m-k-n in Arabic turns on firmness and stability in a place; from it come *makān* (place) and *makānah* (standing). One says "I gave him *tamkīn* over the thing" when you give him power and authority over it, and "I established him (*makkantu lahu*)" when you give him a fixed place. Some scholars of language and Qur'anic exegesis have distinguished between *makkanahu* and *makkana lahu*: the first carries the verb directly to its object and conveys giving him the ability to do something; the second, with the preposition, conveys making his affair firm and consolidating his position. It is worth noticing that in the Qur'an the tamkīn of Sūrat Yūsuf comes in this latter form: *makkannā li-Yūsuf*, that is, "We made his affair firm and consolidated his position for him."

In Qur'anic usage, tamkīn is God's granting a servant ability, authority and firmness in the land, such that he is able to establish what he has been charged with. It is not merely political influence: the Qur'an applies it to individuals and to nations, in matters of religion and matters of this world alike.

### Models of Tamkīn in the Qur'an

Anyone who reflects on the Qur'an will trace several forms of tamkīn that differ in their content.

There is worldly tamkīn granted to earlier nations, with a warning that it does not last: ﴿أَلَمْ يَرَوْا كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّن قَرْنٍ مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مَا لَمْ نُمَكِّن لَّكُمْ﴾ ("Do they not see how many generations We destroyed before them, whom We had established in the land as We have not established you?") (al-Anʿām 6:6). Tamkīn does not prevent destruction when its conditions are lost.

There is the tamkīn promised to the righteous believers: ﴿وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنكُمْ وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُم فِي الْأَرْضِ كَمَا اسْتَخْلَفَ الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ وَلَيُمَكِّنَنَّ لَهُمْ دِينَهُمُ الَّذِي ارْتَضَىٰ لَهُمْ﴾ ("God has promised those among you who believe and do righteous deeds that He will surely make them successors in the land, as He made those before them successors, and that He will surely establish for them their religion which He has approved for them") (al-Nūr 24:55). Here tamkīn is tied to the religion itself, that is, to their religion being made firm and strong for them.

There is the tamkīn of the oppressed after oppression: ﴿وَنُرِيدُ أَن نَّمُنَّ عَلَى الَّذِينَ اسْتُضْعِفُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَنَجْعَلَهُمْ أَئِمَّةً وَنَجْعَلَهُمُ الْوَارِثِينَ ۝ وَنُمَكِّنَ لَهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ﴾ ("And We wished to show favour to those who were oppressed in the land, and to make them leaders and make them the inheritors; and to establish them in the land") (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:5-6). This promise, in the story of Mūsā (Moses) and the Children of Israel, parallels what happened to Yūsuf: oppression first, then tamkīn.

There is the tamkīn of Dhū al-Qarnayn: ﴿إِنَّا مَكَّنَّا لَهُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَآتَيْنَاهُ مِن كُلِّ شَيْءٍ سَبَبًا﴾ ("We established him in the land and gave him the means to everything") (al-Kahf 18:84). And Dhū al-Qarnayn returned the credit to his Lord: ﴿مَا مَكَّنِّي فِيهِ رَبِّي خَيْرٌ﴾ ("What my Lord has established me in is better") (al-Kahf 18:95); he declined the human payment and offered what he had of strength.

And there is the attribute of *makīn* in the description of the noble angel: ﴿ذِي قُوَّةٍ عِندَ ذِي الْعَرْشِ مَكِينٍ﴾ ("possessing strength, and held in standing with the Lord of the Throne") (al-Takwīr 81:20), which shows that *makīn* is a description of lofty station and rank.

### The Hallmark of Yūsuf's Tamkīn: From Affliction to Office

What distinguishes Yūsuf's tamkīn in the Qur'an is that it came late and by stages, and at every stage Yūsuf faced a test. In the house of the *ʿAzīz* (the governor) he was given standing among the people of the household, and was tested by temptation. In prison he was given standing in the hearts of its inmates, until they said: ﴿إِنَّا نَرَاكَ مِنَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ﴾ ("Indeed we see you to be of those who do good") (Yūsuf 12:36), and he was tested by length of confinement and by being forgotten. Then he was given standing in the whole land, and was tested by authority and by deciding the matter of his brothers.

This pattern offers a lesson in understanding tamkīn: it is not a sudden gift but the fruit of a fitness that is tested again and again. In Sūrat Yūsuf that fitness appears in two qualities, mentioned first by the king and then by Yūsuf: "trustworthy" (*amīn*) and "a knowing guardian" (*ḥafīẓ ʿalīm*). Trustworthiness is the king's word, guardianship and knowledge are Yūsuf's, and together they are the two conditions of authority. This agrees with what is reported in the story of Mūsā: ﴿إِنَّ خَيْرَ مَنِ اسْتَأْجَرْتَ الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ﴾ ("Indeed the best one you can hire is the strong, the trustworthy") (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:26). Strength and trustworthiness, or guardianship and knowledge, are the two pillars of competence in the Qur'anic conception of authority.

### The Prophetic Witness: Tamkīn Is a Trust One Will Be Questioned About

When Abū Dharr, may God be pleased with him, asked for a position of command, the Prophet ﷺ struck his shoulder with his hand and said: «يا أبا ذر، إنك ضعيف، وإنها أمانة، وإنها يوم القيامة خزيٌ وندامة، إلا من أخذها بحقها، وأدّى الذي عليه فيها» ("Abū Dharr, you are weak, and it is a trust, and on the Day of Resurrection it will be a cause of disgrace and regret, except for one who takes it by its right and fulfils what is required of him in it")[^1]. The ḥadīth defines the proper face of tamkīn: not an honour to boast of, but a heavy trust. The difference between Yūsuf's request for the storehouses and the Prophet's discouraging Abū Dharr from command lies in the condition of the one asking: Yūsuf's request is grounded in "a knowing guardian," and in a situation in which the coming famine and the people's need had become evident, so that the request served the public good and not a personal interest. From this the scholars understood that whoever is personally bound to safeguard a right that no one else will uphold may seek office for that reason.

### A Reading in Light of the Higher Objectives (Maqāṣid)

The objectives of tamkīn point to three things. First, to show that might and power belong to God, who takes them away and bestows them: ﴿قُلِ اللَّهُمَّ مَالِكَ الْمُلْكِ تُؤْتِي الْمُلْكَ مَن تَشَاءُ﴾ ("Say: O God, Owner of all sovereignty, You give sovereignty to whom You will") (Āl ʿImrān 3:26). Second, to bind power to responsibility: the verse of al-Ḥajj states the purpose of tamkīn: ﴿الَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَمَرُوا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ﴾ ("Those who, if We establish them in the land, establish prayer, give zakāh, enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong") (al-Ḥajj 22:41). Tamkīn is a means of establishing the truth, not an end in itself. Third, to bring out that God does not let the reward of those who do good go to waste: "and We do not let the reward of those who do good go to waste" (Yūsuf 12:56), so that tamkīn is a recompense for doing good in the earlier stages.

Among the points worth noting in this subject is that God mentioned the condition of gratitude in the context of tamkīn: ﴿وَلَقَدْ مَكَّنَّاكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَجَعَلْنَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَعَايِشَ ۗ قَلِيلًا مَّا تَشْكُرُونَ﴾ ("And We have established you in the land and made for you therein means of livelihood; little do you give thanks") (al-Aʿrāf 7:10). Tamkīn is a test of gratitude just as affliction is a test of patience.

### Conditional Tamkīn: Laws That Show No Favouritism

The Qur'an shows that tamkīn is not a permanent guarantee but is subject to divine laws (*sunan*). In Sūrat al-Aḥqāf: ﴿وَلَقَدْ مَكَّنَّاهُمْ فِيمَا إِن مَّكَّنَّاكُمْ فِيهِ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُمْ سَمْعًا وَأَبْصَارًا وَأَفْئِدَةً فَمَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُمْ سَمْعُهُمْ وَلَا أَبْصَارُهُمْ وَلَا أَفْئِدَتُهُمْ مِّن شَيْءٍ إِذْ كَانُوا يَجْحَدُونَ بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ﴾ ("And We had established them in that wherein We have not established you, and made for them hearing, sight and hearts; but their hearing, their sight and their hearts availed them nothing, since they used to deny the signs of God") (al-Aḥqāf 46:26). Those peoples were more firmly established than Quraysh, yet tamkīn did not hold off punishment from them when they denied. The lesson is that possessing the tools does not make up for a sound purpose.

On the other side, the Qur'an recalls a tamkīn given to Quraysh before Islam for a wisdom, as in His saying: ﴿أَوَلَمْ نُمَكِّن لَّهُمْ حَرَمًا آمِنًا يُجْبَىٰ إِلَيْهِ ثَمَرَاتُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ رِّزْقًا مِّن لَّدُنَّا﴾ ("Have We not established for them a secure sanctuary to which are brought the fruits of all things, as provision from Us?") (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:57). This is tamkīn in security and provision, and it comes in the context of arguing against those who pleaded that following the guidance would cost them their safety. The answer came: the security you enjoy is a gift from God, not from your idols.

### How Did Yūsuf Read His Own Tamkīn?

Office did not change Yūsuf's view of himself. In his first conduct after being established, the qualities of the just leader appeared: economic competence (the seven-year plan), fairness in distribution (measuring out to people according to their need), generosity in hospitality (he said to them: do you not see that I give full measure and that I am the best of hosts? 12:59), and pardon in the moment of power ("No blame upon you today," 12:92). Tamkīn showed in his conduct as a character trait and not merely as an office.

More striking still, he did not use his authority to take revenge on his brothers for himself, though he could have. This is what made tamkīn in his story a model: power joined with pardon, authority joined with trustworthiness, and success returned to God's favour. And when he said at the end of the story: ﴿رَبِّ قَدْ آتَيْتَنِي مِنَ الْمُلْكِ﴾ ("My Lord, You have given me of sovereignty") (Yūsuf 12:101), he did not stop at sovereignty but followed it with a supplication: ﴿تَوَفَّنِي مُسْلِمًا وَأَلْحِقْنِي بِالصَّالِحِينَ﴾ ("Take me in death as a Muslim and join me with the righteous"). His tamkīn in this world thus became a prelude to asking for a good end.

### Tamkīn and Istikhlāf: Two Neighbouring Terms

In the verse of al-Nūr, tamkīn is paired with *istikhlāf* (succession, vicegerency): "He will surely make them successors in the land... and will surely establish for them their religion." The difference between them is subtle: *istikhlāf* is the granting of authority and succession in the land, while tamkīn is making the religion firm and consolidating it, that is, that people live their religion in safety and not in fear. That is why the verse follows with His words: ﴿وَلَيُبَدِّلَنَّهُم مِّن بَعْدِ خَوْفِهِمْ أَمْنًا ۚ يَعْبُدُونَنِي لَا يُشْرِكُونَ بِي شَيْئًا﴾ ("and He will surely replace their fear with security, for they worship Me, associating nothing with Me") (al-Nūr 24:55). The true fruit of tamkīn is security and pure worship, not mere dominance.

### The Contemporary Practical Dimension

Many people today live waiting for "tamkīn": in a job, a project, a standing. Some fall into one of two errors: hurrying tamkīn by illegitimate means, or giving up because it has not yet come. The Yūsufian model says: do good where you are, even if it is a well or a prison; for standing is built by mastering the small before the great is given. In the house of the *ʿAzīz* Yūsuf was trustworthy, and in prison he was one who did good, and that qualified him for what came after.

On the side of leadership: whoever has received tamkīn should ask himself the questions the Qur'an asks: Why am I here? Have I safeguarded what I was entrusted with? Have I established with it what I was commanded to establish? For tamkīn that is not harnessed to establishing the truth may be a gradual drawing-in (*istidrāj*), as God Most High says: ﴿سَنَسْتَدْرِجُهُم مِّنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ﴾ ("We will draw them on gradually from where they do not know") (al-Aʿrāf 7:182). This is not said categorically of everyone who has been blessed; it is only a reminder that the one who has been blessed needs to review his condition.

### The Tamkīn That People Do Not See

Not every tamkīn is an office or authority. A servant may be established in the hearts of his family through dutifulness, or in his knowledge through diligent study, or in his craft through mastery, or in his religion through steadfastness. Sūrat Yūsuf itself establishes that Yūsuf's standing in the hearts of the people of the prison preceded his standing in the land. So whoever has not found an office should not think himself deprived of tamkīn; many an established person in his home, his neighbourhood or among his students has an influence greater than those who held high posts. And the measure in the Qur'an is doing good (*iḥsān*): "and We do not let the reward of those who do good go to waste."

### Conclusion

Tamkīn in Sūrat Yūsuf is the end of a journey, not the beginning of a leap. In the Qur'an it is a favour from God that He gives to whom He wills, but a favour with conditions and consequences: trustworthiness, guardianship, knowledge, gratitude and the establishing of the truth. Whoever understands this does not rush what its time has not yet come, nor grows proud of what he has been given, and knows that the One who raised Yūsuf from the well to the storehouses is able to raise whom He wills after trial.
