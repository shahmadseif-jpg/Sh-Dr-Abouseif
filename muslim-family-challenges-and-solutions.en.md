May Allah be good to you, be pleased with you, and keep you in goodness, well-being, security, and His good pleasure and generosity. I ask Allah, the Most High, the All-Powerful, to open for us and for you the doors of His mercies, to bestow upon us His outward and inward blessings, to prepare for our souls a good that will set our hearts at ease, to guide our minds and preserve our well-being. O Allah, amen. Now: this is a blessed night in which we're illuminated by the light of these shining faces and radiant smiles. Were it not for the remembrance of Allah, no life of ours would be settled, and were it not for faith, we would sense no meaning to our existence in this world. The safety valve of our life is our religion, nothing else: our religion first and last, before everything, during it and after it, in the beginning and at the end.

### The Foundation We Start From

We must establish this foundation before we speak about any kind of problem: what's our reference point? Our reference point is the religion. I've believed in Allah, so what the religion says, we adhere to. The greatest problem in people's lives, the one from which every problem that follows is born, is that a person sets himself up as a rival to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic: Allah says and I say, Allah shows me and I see, Allah commands me and I answer back. I don't say he objects outright, but at the very least he claims to know his own situation better than anyone else. So he's told, "The Prophet said," and he turns arrogant. Arrogance isn't only in speech; it's in deed and in means. This principle applies to men and women alike.

### Challenges or Problems?

When we say "The Muslim Family: Challenges and Solutions," I had thought we would be speaking about problems and solutions. Is there a difference between the two words? Yes, a great difference. A challenge means that two are present together, and before them is something they take on; you and I take on so-and-so. Problems, however, don't have this unity or this harmony between us; we aren't on one footing or one line, we're different to begin with, so we take on one another before we take on anyone else. So between many husbands and wives, are there challenges or problems? What do you choose? What you say, we'll go by. You chose problems? Fine, they're the ones who chose.

So what's a problem? A problem, in short, is a word expressing a **gap between two parties**. The first party may be a husband and the second a wife, or a father and a son, or a male partner and a female partner, or a male neighbor and a female neighbor, or a brother and a brother, or a sister and a sister. A gap in a space that came to exist after the original state had been cohesion and union. We have understood that a problem is a gap that has occurred. So what's the cause of the gap? What led to it? How do we return the original state to its place? All of this will come, but first I want to understand the meaning of "problem." The gap may be psychological: we agree on everything, we cooperate and complement one another, but we aren't at ease with each other, we feel no security, no comfort and no tranquility. Or it may be intellectual: I love you very much, you're my beloved, my neighbor, my brother, you pray beside me, but we differ in viewpoint and can't meet; I love you and I don't agree with you. When these gaps are left alone, they spoil relationships and carve up the common ground, until nothing is left between us.

### What Is the Family? A Unit of Mind, Heart and Body

Then what does the family mean? The family is a **unit**. Pay attention to the definition: what does "unit" mean? A group of things that came together to form one thing, that complemented one another until they became one thing. This phone, for example, is a unit: it has the screen, the internal board, the SIM card and so on, all gathered to be one thing. That's why we don't say of Allah, glory be to Him, the Exalted, "wāḥid" (one) like the other ones; rather we say "aḥad." What is the difference between wāḥid and aḥad? The aḥad isn't composed of parts. As for the wāḥid, you can say of it a half, a quarter, a fifth; the wāḥid can be divided, while the aḥad can't be. The family is a group of individuals who agreed from the beginning that they would complete one another, so they live together as though they were one thing.

That's why the Lord of the worlds, glory be to Him, the Exalted, when He describes the relationship between husband and wife, describes it as a solemn covenant[^1]. Why did He describe it as a solemn covenant? Because it's a covenant that isn't undone and not severed, on condition that the relationship is built upon the foundation of the word of Allah, Blessed and Exalted. Building relationships on the word of Allah exists among all people: the groom places his hand in the hand of the officiant, and the officiant says to him: on the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger and on the named dowry. Very beautiful words, but many people don't truly bring them into the matter. So I must understand that, in order to make this unit, I bring the man with his mind, heart and body, and I bring the woman with her mind, heart and body, and I place them together.

Look at what goes on in the minds of young people: the moment the words "wedding night" come up, he laughs and is delighted and turns shy, and only one scene comes into his head. We haven't understood that this unity, as it's in the bed, is the same in the kitchen, and at the table, as we talk and eat and drink; it's there as we think about the future and as we raise our children. The unity is the coming together of two minds, the coming together of two hearts, and the coming together of two bodies. Unfortunately, the picture that appears to all people, except those on whom Allah has mercy, in the matter of marriage is only the coming together of the two bodies.

### The Prophetic Safeguards Before Approaching

That's why the initial prophetic safeguards were: before you approach the matter, take care while you're still at its threshold. «تُنكَح المرأة لأربع»[^2] ("A woman is married for four things"), and these are the human instincts and psychological motives that may be present: for her wealth, her beauty, her lineage and her religion. So what does the Prophet ﷺ say? «فاظفر بذات الدين» ("So win the one who has religion"). Why? So that the unit may be complete, so that when the woman of religion comes to you, she obeys Allah in you. What does "obeys Allah in you" mean? Is it that you say to her, "Come to bed," and she says, "Right away"? Not that; the matter goes far beyond that. That you come home sour-tempered and loud-voiced and she bears it, this is obeying Allah in you. That today is the end of the month and we've no money, and she's patient, this is obeying Allah in you. That Ahmed comes collecting donations and takes what you have from the community's money, as he's building the mosque, and then takes the two bangles from you, and she still obeys Allah in you. That I've an idea of divorce circling in my head, and she obeys Allah in me at that moment. So the matter isn't a matter of the bed; the matter is that the coming together of these two souls, these two bodies and both minds is what produced this unit.

### The Family Is a Large Social Unit

So we've understood that the family is a unit. They call it a social unit, so what does social unit mean? It's the one made up of a group of individuals. Who are these individuals? A husband and a wife at first, but before the husband and wife I've a father of the husband and a mother of the husband, a father of the wife and a mother of the wife, a brother of the husband and a sister of the husband, a brother of the wife and a sister of the wife, and cousins from this side and from that side. So when we marry, are we marrying or holding a celebration? In truth we hold a celebration, and why? Not in order to expose ourselves and show off, but because we're many people, because we're a large number, and because I've a right in my brother's wife, just as my brother has a right in my mother, my son and my sister, and so on for the whole family. This is the unity: that Allah, Blessed and Exalted, brings these parties together so that they represent one entity.

Let's try: the person closest to me in terms of contact, if I need something right now, who is he? My son or my daughter's husband? What do you think? Well, it depends. My daughter's husband is older than my son and wiser than he's, and this is the original and natural state, and he has social relationships, standing and work. So if I speak about some matter, whom do I speak to? I speak to my daughter's husband. As we sit together I don't say to him, "Pass me the glass," for it's the son who performs this task. So the matter is a matter of using the mind in deploying these relationships with one another, and this combination requires understanding and awareness of the dimensions of the kinds of dealings that exist.

### What All Humans Share

So far we've arrived at two statements: a problem is a gap between two parties, and the two parties may be two minds that don't understand each other. As for the kinds of problems that exist among all human beings, whether the woman is Italian or American or Afghan or Yemeni or Moroccan or Jordanian or Tunisian or Algerian (and I leave out the Egyptian women so that some ladies don't get angry), find me one of all these who isn't jealous over her husband. Any nationality in the world that has a woman who isn't jealous? There isn't one in the whole universe. Rather she's jealous, and jealousy produces emotional reactions, and it's a praiseworthy thing in a woman, but if it goes beyond its limit it turns into its opposite, and everything that goes beyond its limit turns into its opposite, we agree. Another: find me one on the whole earth who doesn't love adorning herself and strutting, and the original state is that this be in men, that he be jealous over his wife. So we've common denominators among all humans in the original divine creation. That's why, when it's said, "Beware of marrying an American woman, beware of marrying an Italian woman," you're only speaking about the cultural dimension, customs and upbringing, and that's one category; as for the innate category upon which Allah created people, that's another matter, equal among them all.

### Disagreement: Different Minds

What kinds of problems exist among all people? The first is disagreement. Why do we not agree? One of them tells me: we don't agree because of the level of cultures; the wife isn't educated at all, and our friend who married her has a doctorate, or she studied geography until she won the Nobel Prize and he works in physics. We always say: look at the kind of mentality you're dealing with. Let's put a single problem before us, and bring a doctor, an engineer and a merchant. The engineer looks at the calculation and sees straight and winding lines. The doctor always looks at the side effects: if we do this, such and such may happen. The merchant says: no, this story will lose us money, stop here, and I'll have five more dollars. Allahu akbar! Why? Because people's mentalities differ. So imagine a merchant married to a woman engineer, who runs the house by pencil and ruler, and this costs him money, so they don't agree. Or a woman doctor, whose nature is to look at the site of the illness and work hard to prescribe the remedy.

The mentality of the doctor, by the way, is very close to the mentality of the shaykh, the imam, if the imam is aware and conscious of the dimensions of his task: that he's a healer of all these people with their differing conditions, illnesses, inclinations and desires. So he deals with the logic of the doctor: he looks at the illnesses, assesses them and treats them. The imam adds one very simple thing, which is reconciling opposing parties, so he's always trying to bring people together. That's why the last decision we may take, and Shaykh Mahmoud bears witness with me, is that we agree with people on divorce. We always try to reconcile between them, make peace and set things right. If they were two partners in a business and it was very difficult, I said to them: dissolve this company and give me some rest, for we don't know how to do anything else. Why? Because my brain was built this way. So imagine that you're dealing between two parties, a husband and a wife, and each has a different head in its orientations, its calculations and the dynamics of work inside it, and in the paths and corridors that move inside the brain; I walk this way and she walks that way. By the way, even if the specializations agree, the mentality of the man must differ from the mentality of the woman.

I don't say this to belittle; I ask Allah to bless us all in our families. But look at our sisters, may Allah honor them: as people say, you find one of them driving the car, eating a sandwich and talking on the phone, while preventing the boy in the back seat from misbehaving, and following everything, even the children at home: you, you got down from the bed, get back up again. She has terrifying faculties, you can hardly understand how this brain works. She's like that at home: she cooks, talks and keeps track. Our respectable friend the man has come into his house returning from work, thank God, so what does he do? He sits on the sofa looking at the television, and she speaks: the boy hit the girl, and the girl bumped into such and such, and we went up and came down. He says: uh-huh. We say to him: what do you think? Uh-huh. Did you listen? Uh-huh. This one doesn't respond, he isn't with us on the matter at all, our friend is up in the air. This is my brain: when I concentrate on my work I'm at my work, and she's talking from the Atlantic Ocean and then moves to the other side.

### The Story of the Forty Years

I've a friend, a very good man, very generous, very much a provider, who tells people: for forty years there has been no problem between me and my wife. They said to him: give us an example, how so? He said: I agreed with her from the beginning that the big problems are none of our concern, and the small problems are none of my concern. They said: fine, give an example. He said: the examples of the children: the children ate, the children drank, the children slept, the children washed, the children went to school, entered university, married, divorced, and I've no involvement, small problems. They said: Mashallah, so what are your big topics then? He said: well, the matter of America and Afghanistan, and what do I've to do with that? So our friend isn't here, our friend is in the soft life, taking a side, withdrawn.

The point is that the difference between the two mentalities is something we don't realize. I'm always speaking with my wife imagining that she's with me at the same level, we speak word for word, and the brain is different. I don't raise her status or lower it, I only say: our Lord built this brain in this way and that brain in that way. I know how to deal with numbers, and I deal with people in the street, and I pull and tie and calculate, this is my nature, and my muscles are part of my makeup; as for her, her tenderness is part of her makeup, her quick tear is part of her makeup, her sensitivity is part of her makeup. I want a man, one of the men, to raise his hand and say: I can be patient with a child crying on my shoulder from morning until the end of the day. Are you raising your hand or scratching your head? Young men, you aren't married, so let's be sensible and send blessings upon the Guide. Which of us can be patient with a child who has colic and is crying from morning to the end of the day? We can't. You have your work from morning to the end of the day, then you came and found a child crying, could you stay up with him and then finish your work tomorrow? Our sister can; she spends the whole day in her kitchen going back and forth, then at the end of the day she finds the boy turning right and left, and she stays seated beside him patting, changing, wiping and cleaning, may Allah be pleased with her, until morning, and then she begins her schedule in the morning as usual. What a superhuman ability! How did you do that? I can't.

So men are very weak in some respects and very strong in others, and our sisters are very weak in some respects and very great in others. The problem is that when we deal together I use my points of strength while dealing with the other party as though she had the same points of strength as mine, and if I deal with my points of weakness she should have what I've: no, my dear. The same story applies to her.

### The Remedy: Understanding and Patience, Then Temporary Disagreement

So when we say that disagreement is a common denominator in the problems among all human beings, I must see a solution for it. What is the solution? Understanding: that I understand that I've a nature different from the nature of the other party, and that we must respond to these particulars and deal with them with reason. So am I reasonable? No, I go mad, swear by divorce, and say to her: your father and your mother and your aunt and whoever came and went, and on the tongue of some fool of the forefathers whom I don't know what he has to do with it, and I get angry, flare up and jump about. Uncle, calm down, may Allah protect you, calm down, Hajj! If only someone would pat this man. Then our sister gets angry, gathers her things and leaves, and I say: I leave you the house, lady! Stop for a moment, and look at the kids and these components, where are we going? Why is the nearest thing that comes to our minds anger and separation? The sister who works at a job can't do that with her boss at work if he says a word that upset her. Our brother likewise: if a woman at work were with him and made a certain little move, oh, how gentle and soft and sweet-spoken he would be! Get out of this; be patient a little as you're patient at work with the lady over there, be patient at home a little.

So disagreement is a common denominator among all creation, among all husbands and wives, but the difference between people is that there's a sensible person who gathers the scattered parts of this rift and makes it agreement again, and makes the disagreement temporary, not permanent.

### The Shades of Separation

The other matter among the common denominators is the matter of separation. Separation is either temporary, or successive, or permanent. So when you're upset at home, what do you do? We stay upset. Then? No, we just go on being upset as usual. When do you meet? It doesn't matter, if Allah wills and we meet after a month or so. The world is ruined like this! So there's what they call marriage with suspended execution, or divorce with suspended execution, which is the silent divorce. Its sign is that the two of us live under one roof: peace be upon you, and upon you be peace, how are you today? Fine, thank God, did you cook? Yes. Very formal treatment, word for word, peace be upon you, and upon you peace, no "and the mercy of Allah and His blessings," that addition isn't there. Fine, give us the household allowance. Right, the money is there. I've such-and-such left. By the way, I went to the doctor today and he said such-and-such. This dry language, why do we deal in language like that of the Saʿīdīs? I'm speaking about the Upper Egyptians in our Egypt, I don't know what you have among you; and what do you call them where you're, the Bedouin? May Allah open things for you.

So we find separation, whether temporary or successive, to be one of the permanent problems, or of the common denominators among all human beings. As for permanent separation, it's that we make the decision: the two don't suit each other and we fear for the children, so we sit together in the house, and even if we don't say the word of divorce, the man knows his room and the woman knows her room, and he sleeps at such-and-such an hour and she sleeps at such-and-such an hour, and we're done without disturbance. Meanwhile, who is the respectable intermediary running between the two parties? Satan. He goes here and calms things a little, and goes there and kindles a little, and drives people mad here, and the world keeps running. He's the winner, and the two of us are burned, and the kids are the losers. So far I'm still speaking only about the problem.

### The External Causes: Impressions Drawn Since Childhood

So what are the causes of the problems in all our lives? What makes us differ, whether we failed to agree, or there was a temporary, successive or permanent separation? We have external causes, that's, from outside this marital frame. The first is the impressions drawn in the mind since childhood days. Sometimes a brother and a sister, a husband and a wife, come to me with a problem: a solution, our master. Welcome, you have brought us light, please come in. The first thing I do is give the husband a lovely blank sheet and a nice pen, and I say to him: please, and you too, madam, please, do you know how to read and write? Please, both of you, each of you write for me on this page, front and back, the negatives of the other party and the positives, and be careful, for our Lord sees you, and write with justice and goodness. The two do so, then we collect the two papers and compare: the shared positives and the shared negatives. Sometimes you find a contradiction in the words, and you understand that he's lying. The second step: tell me, madam, about Daddy and Mommy, how were they together in the old days? Tell me about your childhood, when you were three, four, five, nine, thirteen and seventeen, and you began to become a young woman, and then you began to look at a boy walking in the street, and you said I want to marry, and the dreams passed you by. Tell me the history of our life from the beginning until today. You find that Daddy used to beat Mommy with a stick, never mind, or Mommy was obstinate and had the first and last word in the house. So you find very old impressions fixed in the mind of this girl, and when she came to marry she wanted to flex her muscles like Mommy. It isn't right that Mommy walks on one line and the daughter on another line, and then she says: I'm no longer Mommy's daughter.

Our first friend, for example, had a father who was the man of the house, and in our early days when the father said a word, not only the wife heard it but the whole street heard, he said a word and the matter was finished, no discussion. So our friend came with these entitlements, and the sister came with her own mentality too, so each of them wants to flex his muscles over the other. Do you remember what they say in our Egypt? "Slaughter the cat for her on the wedding night." And you have the same among you, for you have all laughed. The last time I went down to Egypt I didn't find one cat in the street, perhaps the brothers did away with them. The point is that each of the two parties comes with prior impressions. Our young men must understand that when you marry a woman you must respect her history that was in her home before she came to you.

That's why, when someone comes to me and says: Shaykh, I want to propose to so-and-so, I say: by Allah, excellent, welcome, are you sure? Do I find you serious or playing? He says: serious, my master. So I say to him: leave us for a bit, and the Shaykh has his own ways by which his own "investigation" runs. So we ask: if the mother runs the house wisely and doesn't muddle things, and the father sits on the sofa from sunset and doesn't speak, is this the nature of the family? I'm joking, may Allah reward you with good.

The point is that before we enter this complex process that will continue with us until the end of life, I must understand the mentality of the other party and where he came from. In conclusion, one word, by Almighty Allah, summed all this up: the Prophet ﷺ said: «تخيّروا لنطفكم، فإن العِرق…»[^3] ("Choose well for your seed, for lineage…"). The Prophet settles the whole matter for you in this saying. What does "choose well for your seed" mean? Choose the one you'll meet one day in your house, and from whom children will come out for you, because lineage runs deep: the mental inheritance she brought from her father and mother in their house will come with her to this house.

### The Mother and the Father: Sincerity Without Understanding the Different Environment

The greater problem is that after we marry and settle and differ and come to see who will go, the mother takes flight from their place and comes to you at the house, whether by phone or by messages or she came herself and honored us and lit up the house: you, beware of letting him do such-and-such, do such-and-such. It must be, my lady, may Allah open things for you, step aside, may Allah be generous to you, let's finish our work together. Likewise the mother or father of the boy, each one wants to impose his control in the manner he wants and knows. So where is the problem? The father and the mother, both parties, are extremely sincere, they're very sincere. So if your son is marrying and you advise him, you aren't betraying him, you're very sincere, but you don't understand that there's a different environment and different natures, and that my son's wife isn't a second copy of my own wife, and that the one who will receive this advice isn't necessarily like that herself. There are different mentalities.

### The Society Around Us

Among the external causes also is the society in which we live. We must understand that if I anger my wife and she leaves me and goes to the mosque (and I'm speaking about society in general), her friend at the mosque says to her: you look changed, are you upset about something? No, I'm fine, thank God. No, this is too much, we're friends and we pray together. By Allah, but a little upset. Why? Abu So-and-so did such-and-such. No, this won't do, I with Abu So-and-so with me when he does such-and-such... and she draws her a vague road map. Our sister, may Allah reward her with good, speaks from her own background: she raised her husband and walked him over the dough so he wouldn't muddle it, and not all men are necessarily like the other man, so she walks over the dough and doesn't spoil it. So these are advice from outside, and we're treating a case that isn't our friend's case.

Let me give an example: I've a married daughter. When my daughter's husband speaks with me about any matter, or sometimes we open a conversation, as though there were a problem of some kind, and I raised her and know her character, small and large, and I know everything about my daughter, so I imagine that she could produce this kind of problem, so I say to him: what do you think? Imagine I was surprised today at the mosque that the girl does such and such. If I see that his face has changed, I understand that the girl did this act; and if I see him listening attentively and calmly, I understand that things are covered, thank God. This is a kind we call social intelligence: how you can deal with the other party. If your daughter's husband comes to consult you, we usually say to the girl a very nice word: don't worry, my daughter, I'll marry you to the master of masters. Why does he jump about at you? No, don't worry, you have men behind you. Calm down: behind us are men who cover and don't expose, behind us are men who gather and don't divide, behind us are men who protect and don't humiliate, behind us are men who preserve the straightness of the straight line and don't divide people, behind us are men of mind, not men of arm and bravado, behind us are men of their word, not men who look from where the benefit comes. We have right men, but what kind of men?

The point is that the environment surrounding the two parties is very strongly influential, and accordingly two basic things are required of us, send blessings upon the Guide, the Bearer of glad tidings. The first matter: that we keep reminding people of these principles always, so we've lectures, and Shaykh Mahmoud says: please speak on this topic, and another guest comes and he says to him: speak on the second topic. We want to establish the pillars of the religion in people's hearts and minds so that they remain settled and firm. This is like maintenance for your car: you take it and fix it every so often, change the oil and adjust, and do a full inspection so that it keeps running with you always. So this is maintenance, and the mosque is the place of maintenance.

The other matter is that I teach my daughter and my son that anyone who tells them a piece of advice that divides, they should not listen to it from anyone in the world, even if it's me; and anyone who tells them a piece of advice that unites, they should think about it and not merely hear it, because thinking is the first way to analyze data. Again: does every adviser advise based on his personal experience and expertise, or based on the reality before him? I tell you advice based on my experience, my reality and my brain, I think this way. That's why I say to everyone: let no one seek advice based on my way of thinking, for I'll do such-and-such but you may be different; so compare the advice I said with what you're able to do, because advice may be perfectly right yet unsuited to the personality of the other party, so he can't carry it out.

Accordingly, when we said that there are external causes represented by the family, for example, we advise every mother: fear Allah regarding your daughter, she isn't a copy of you, and her husband isn't like your husband, and the state of affairs with her may not be the state of affairs with you. Be careful, mother of the girl, she has become the wife of my son or of the son of so-and-so. Be careful when you advise your daughter, for praise be to Allah, our Lord completed His favor upon you and your husband is with you until now. What if the mother is herself divorced and her social or marital life is ruined? We take an example from her. I ask Allah that no one be upset with me for what I'm about to say, but in many cases I'm accepting the résumés of women teachers at my school, and by Allah forgive me, if there were one who is divorced and has many problems and her reputation is well known throughout the community, we may not accept her, and we apologize to her that she doesn't continue with us. Why? What she wants to take of money, money is easy. But her presence inside the school, when she has made problems before and is famous, not covered, won't be an element of reform. Is what I say wrong? She won't be an element of reform, because she won't change and leave but will transmit her experiences and her way of working to the others, so they do the same. So we either spoil or we reform. The point is that every adviser must take into account the state of the one advised or the one seeking advice, because people's circumstances differ according to their reality and condition.

### The Internal Causes: Clinging to One's Opinion

Among the causes of problems is clinging to one's opinion: me and that's all, my opinion is always the right one. How do we do this? Imagine, for example, that you say to your wife: do it to the right, and she says to you: fine, why not to the left? What do you think about it being to the left? You say to her: no, it's right and that's it. Fine, let's do left, perhaps it will be better. No, it's like this. This is clinging to one's opinion. Our friend sometimes asks for something and wants it now, clinging now, the state now. This is the matter of clinging to opinion in its different forms. So someone says to me: why did you call it clinging to opinion? Perhaps it's sharpness and intelligence, and I really am the one who judges and reasons and my opinion is the right one. I say to you: fine, but the one with the right opinion has openness to hearing others, for the person whose opinion is right always tries to complete his opinion with the opinions of others. Among the problems we've is that we consider the counsel of a woman... even in Egypt we've a proverb that says: "A woman's counsel ruins the house for a year." You know my opinion is right, Dr. Mahmoud. But you're busy, so leave this, dear Hajj. This boy is sitting in front of you too, film well. Send blessings upon the Guide. Confession is half of chivalry.

I know that these particulars are very sensitive, and many people can't. This by the way is among the biggest problems occurring in this particular: that the two of us don't sit together and be frank with one another. So she says to me: could you, with your permission, perform ablution? What does it mean? It means: go change this smell of your mouth and clean your nostrils that you snort with, may our Lord bring us a little closer, brother, go clean up and come. Just a word: could you, with your permission, perform ablution and come. So I go and perform ablution and clean up, for example. So is the matter of interests: people sit together, I like such-and-such about you and I don't like such-and-such about you, you affect such-and-such, you go on and on and so on. If that happened it would gather the affairs together.

### The Solutions: Shared Worship, Periodic Reviews, and Reminders of Virtues

As for the solutions, I had written some of them, and I ask Allah to help us and bless you all, from among the shared matters. Be patient with me for two minutes for Allah's sake, may Allah be pleased with you. Send blessings upon the Guide, the Bearer of glad tidings. There is something we aren't paying attention to: do you know painkillers? Painkillers don't uproot the disease, painkillers only soothe. Among the important painkillers inside homes is **shared worship**. Pay attention to two words: shared worship, not individual worship. Imagine a husband and his wife sitting and reading together a juzʾ of the Qur'an. Give me the level of disagreements that occurs between them, I mean they will disagree all day, and then at the end of the day they sit and read the Qur'an and go to bed in good spirits. Right. We're sitting after the Qur'an portion, and before it we reviewed each other about what we did yesterday. Shared worship: I prayed two rakʿahs at night, will you not pray with me? I'm tired. Pray sitting, I'll bring you a chair, please, rest, I know you're exhausted all day long, pray two rakʿahs with me. So we read a juzʾ of the Qur'an together, and prayed two rakʿahs together, and sat saying: make supplication for us, Abu So-and-so, make supplication for us, I feel I need to hear my supplications. Did this happen? Does any of us do this? Or do I do this at all? But I'm speaking about the truth, and this is a very large part of the treatment. Make supplication for us, by Allah, so she raises her hands and supplicates while you say: amen. Fine. But don't supplicate and say: O Allah, replace for me a house better than my house and a husband better than my husband!

The second matter: **periodic reviews**. What are periodic reviews? There is something called a "retreat": the owners of big companies take the whole work team, right, and go out on a vacation for a whole day from beginning to end, breakfast, lunch and dinner, and go to a nice place and sit relaxing, and what's the news of the world and so-and-so did this and so-and-so did that, and there's someone writing down the notes. Then we return to work the next day with a report that came out of yesterday's retreat, and we're going to do this so that we review our affairs. This doesn't happen in our homes, we don't do it even once a year to review what happened last year, what we want to do next year, what the future plans are. We don't do that. That's why the only manager who runs the home from top to bottom is Satan, because he's the only one who has a plan, the only one who knows what he will do tomorrow, the only one who knows when and how he will make you hit the wall.

The third matter: **constantly reminding of the other party's virtues**. What prevents me from hearing from my wife every now and then: may our Lord keep you for us, may we not be deprived of your presence, you feel the world? Just a word, the poor woman lost nothing when she said it. What prevents me from saying to her every now and then: wonderful, by Almighty Allah you tire yourself a great deal, when I go to anyone's I can't eat food like your food, you have exhausted me, you give me no chance anywhere else, your food is delicious, what beauty! What prevents the man from saying to his wife: Oh! Does the sun rise at night? What brought it now, you came in suddenly like that! Any words, any words, at any time, in any chatter. But pay attention: we're laughing now, and by Almighty Allah you don't know the effect of that in changing the general mood inside the home. By that you extinguish the ember of the fire Satan kindled, so that you can spend a good time, long or short according to your ability to understand, be reasonable and take into account the other party and the different mentality.

### The Final Counsel: What Is Behind the Curtain

The last counsel with which we conclude: don't forget that behind the curtain there's Satan. Satan makes the good ugly and the bad attractive, and accordingly he spews his poisons inside society and inside the home to ruin it. Imagine that our master the Prophet ﷺ conveys to us this picture: that Satan sits on his throne on the sea[^6] and waits for the men who come to him, these little kids, the boys, what did you do today? One of them says to him: I didn't cease with so-and-so and so-and-so until he divorced her. He says to him: you, you're the one, and he takes pride in him. That's it. Glory be to You, O Allah, and praise; I bear witness that there's no god but You, I seek Your forgiveness and repent to You.

Then he recited: In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful ﴿وَالْعَصْرِ ۝ إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ…﴾[^7] (By the time. Indeed, mankind is in loss, except those who believe and do righteous deeds and advise one another to truth…)

May Allah reward you with good, and the peace of Allah be upon you.
