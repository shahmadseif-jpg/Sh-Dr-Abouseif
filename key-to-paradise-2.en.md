> Occasion: Summary of a Jumuʿah khutbah delivered at Bayt Al-Karim on May 8, 2026.
> Source: transcription of a recorded khutbah by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif — [watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nHrMG1ItT3Y)

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him, seek His help and His forgiveness, and ask Him to place īmān in our hearts and to keep it safe in our minds as well. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that Muḥammad ﷺ is His final Messenger and Prophet.

Last time we spoke about the keys to Paradise, and about the gates the Prophet ﷺ described: one person is called through the gate of prayer, another through the gate of jihad, and so on. But a list of gates leaves a harder question standing in front of us. If something is required of me before I can enter by any of them, what is it? What is the first step?

The first step is to know.

### Ignorance acts at random

Picture a man who opens his laptop and tries to build software with an AI tool, though he knows nothing about code. He may type with great energy. He may even stumble on something that runs. But he is working blind, and blind work produces nothing, or worse: it breaks what it meant to mend. People without knowledge are like that. They move, but they move at random, and what is in their hands turns out hollow.

This is why the very first word that reached the Prophet ﷺ at the start of his mission was a command to know.

### A word that means more than reading

> ﴿اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ﴾ [al-ʿAlaq 96:1]
> "Read in the name of your Lord who created."

We usually translate *iqraʾ* as "read," and that is correct as far as it goes. But think about what reading actually involves. You look at shapes and tell which letter each one is. You notice that one letter looks like another until you see its position. You hear whether a letter is sounded or silent. Behind the eyes, a mind is following step by step. Reading is a kind of conscious attention, and the word that came to the Prophet ﷺ asked for all of it.

He did not read from a book, and yet he was the greatest of readers. What he read was the world around him. He read people: where each one came from, what moved him, and the best way to speak to him. On one occasion he answered a man in that man's own tribal manner of speech, which swaps one letter for another. Had he not understood the man's background and mindset, he could not have replied in that way.

That is the knowledge we are asked to seek, and it matters for our own mission. Why do we stay in this land? If we have any reason other than carrying the word of Allah, we have already lost everything. If that is our reason, we will feel the weight of that word on our shoulders and work to move people one step further. That is our purpose in this world, and in this country in particular.

### Knowledge comes before action

From there Allah turns to the Prophet ﷺ with a second command:

> ﴿فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ﴾ [Muḥammad 47:19]
> "So know that there is no god but Allah."[^1]

Knowledge first, then speech and action. How could it be otherwise? You cannot act well on something you have not understood.

Our religion arranges this in levels. Some things everyone must know, because everyone is commanded to do them: no Muslim anywhere has an excuse to neglect the prayer. Other duties fall on you only when you reach them. A man with no wealth does not owe zakāh and need not study its details. Someone who is not yet able to go on Ḥajj is not yet required to master its rites. But the moment you step into any of these, learning becomes a must, with no alternative.

Take marriage. Before you enter it, you should know its rulings. Marriage can be obligatory for one person, recommended for another, merely permitted, disliked, or even forbidden, depending on the person and his circumstances. A man who says to his wife, "You are divorced," and only afterwards comes to the shaykh to ask what follows from it, has done the thing in the wrong order. The shaykh sitting in his office did nothing; he only gives the ruling. The one who spoke the word should have learned before he spoke.

The Prophet ﷺ dealt with people exactly this way. When they came with questions, he told one person to do a thing and another person to keep away from it entirely.

### What knowledge builds

Look at the Muslim community in its golden centuries. It held so much knowledge that others came to it. There was a time when people from the West travelled to the East to find a good physician, because they had nothing of their own. We stand now in America and call it the greatest of countries. It is worth asking what position we would hold if we walked the path of knowledge as our predecessors did.

The Prophet ﷺ gave a plain description of the one who sets out to learn:

> «مَنْ سَلَكَ طَرِيقًا يَلْتَمِسُ فِيهِ عِلْمًا سَهَّلَ اللَّهُ لَهُ بِهِ طَرِيقًا إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ»
> "Whoever travels a road seeking knowledge, Allah makes easy for him by it a road to Paradise."[^2]

Why is the reward so great for the one who is only seeking? Because the seeker is easing life for other people. He is making this world lighter for them, and so Allah makes his way to Paradise light. In another hadith the Prophet ﷺ said that everything around the learner is turned toward him, even the fish in the water ask forgiveness for him.[^3] Think of what that means for the person who simply sets out on the road to the masjid for a class.

It also tells us what to ask of one another. When people come to the masjid and care nothing for what is being taught, the carelessness itself wears the teacher down. Imagine sitting in a class where you ignore what the teacher says. He will be disappointed, and in time he may take his work somewhere it is valued.

### Two groups, two outcomes

There is one more reason not to leave this to later. We will stand before Allah and be asked what we did. We want to have something to answer with.

Allah places two groups before us and lets us choose:

> ﴿قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِي الَّذِينَ يَعْلَمُونَ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ﴾ [al-Zumar 39:9]
> "Say: Are those who know equal to those who do not know?"[^4]

Choose the first, and look at what He promises:

> ﴿يَرْفَعِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْعِلْمَ دَرَجَاتٍ﴾ [al-Mujādilah 58:11]
> "Allah will raise those who believe among you, and those who were given knowledge, by degrees."

If the one raising your rank is Allah Himself, where can that take you? Then He tells us how knowledge shapes the heart:

> ﴿إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى اللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ الْعُلَمَاءُ﴾ [Fāṭir 35:28]
> "Only those of His servants who have knowledge truly fear Allah."

Without knowledge we cannot fear Allah as He deserves, nor weigh what we may face on the Day of Judgment if we live carelessly. And when Allah finishes describing the blessings of Paradise, He ends by saying that all of it belongs to a particular kind of person:

> ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ رَبَّهُ﴾ [al-Bayyinah 98:8]
> "That is for the one who fears his Lord."

Paradise has its key, and nothing opens without it. If we stay ignorant, we lose our time, our money, our standing and our dignity.

### Bring your children

You are free to decide what you will do with yourselves. What I am begging you for today is something else: bring your children to us.

They are not at fault for what we do with our own lives, and they have no way to reach us unless you bring them. We have classes, a Qurʾān programme, lessons, a Q&A, and time that we have set aside for them. We do not need your money. We do not need anything from you except your children. I swear by Allah that you will be asked about them more than you will be asked about yourselves. If you want to protect what remains of your life, protect them.

Give them the chance to learn in the masjid who Allah is, why the Prophet ﷺ should be the role model of their lives, and what their actions will lead to in Paradise or in the Fire. Let them understand their dignity as Muslims and what awaits them if they keep to this road. Let them taste tranquility, and see that money is nothing, and that worldly rank is nothing.

As long as we are working for Allah alone, we will find ourselves almost servants to our children. We will treat our spouses with humility, men and women alike, not for our own sake, but for theirs. A child needs to grow up in a good atmosphere and with a sound mind, and his heart must not be bruised by a household where one parent is shouting at the other all day. Give them the chance to taste the sweetness of Islam.
