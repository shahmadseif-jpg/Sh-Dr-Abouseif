> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Al-Huda Mosque on September 12, 2025.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p05Ep4HCDR8).

By the Quran's measure, faith is the one cause of happiness in worldly life and bliss in the Hereafter. Its equation is brief and allows no appeal, in the meaning of the verse: “Whoever turns away from My remembrance will have a life of hardship” [Ta-Ha 20:124].

Is this our reality, as individuals and as a community? Look around you. The Muslim community has reached a state where it is not taken seriously as either an enemy or a friend. No one hesitates to attack us whenever, however much and wherever they wish. We are on the margins of their thinking to begin with. For almost two years, Gaza has been living through events you know well. You know the condition of our people in Yemen and elsewhere, and what is happening in Sudan—and what do you know of Sudan? The media blackout there has reached a point where no one can raise a phone to film a scene without joining the dead, according to reports from inside. Where, then, do Muslims stand? What can we do?

We hear this question from time to time: We live in America, far from these events, and have no part in them. Let us ask ourselves together: During the thirteen years the Prophet ﷺ spent in Makkah, was he fighting and physically defending his religion? Or was he struggling against the unbelief and idolatry surrounding him and his Companions? What occupied the mind of the first caller to the faith in Makkah then?

### Makkah: A Stage of Training in Faith

The answer is that the Prophet ﷺ was raising people who carried faith in their hearts. These individuals, few or many, moved from their training in faith in Makkah to Madinah. There they became Allah's soldiers, defending the religion as it deserved, the first line of defence for the call and its people, for Islam and its followers. Makkah was a stage of preparing souls through faith. We saw Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and others whom Makkah brought forth with pure hearts and minds. This is what is required first: faith within you must be a force that moves you, not a claim.

### Faith That Is Claimed but Does Not Move Us

Listen to Allah's words, translated in meaning: “The desert Arabs say, ‘We believe.’ Say, ‘You have not believed. Rather, say, “We have submitted,” for faith has not yet entered your hearts’” [al-Hujurat 49:14]. Have we tried to think about this wording? We all claim faith as individuals, and we all know that truthfulness brings safety. But how much lying is there in individual lives and in the life of the community? All of us do good and bad, right and wrong. But what does it mean when I do wrong while certain that the opposite is right? I lie deliberately, mislead deliberately, give false testimony deliberately, and cheat deliberately in my sales and trade, while claiming faith! I stand in the rows of worshippers, listen to the Quran, recite its pages, repeat Surat al-Kahf on Fridays and hear the call to prayer. All of this moves my body to the prayer place to say: Allah is greater. Then I return to my business and repeat the cheating, concealment, forgery and deception. It is as though I go to the mosque to take a painkiller for my conscience, then return with the same body to do whatever I please in pursuit of a worldly life I desire, with no desire for anything beyond it.

Where is faith? We all know that when we married, we took other people's daughters into our care through Allah's trust and His word. How many of us are mindful of Allah in treating our households? Our women know, as religious theory, that the man at home is a trust in their care, that he has rights over them and they have duties in their homes. How many of them, many or few, are mindful of Allah in treating their husbands and living well with them? We all know that our children at home are a trust Allah has placed with us. We are required to prepare this generation to carry Islam's identity and message into tomorrow and spread Allah's religion across the earth. Which of us is mindful of Allah in caring for his children, giving them more time than he gives his friends and companions, and treating them as well as he treats himself? Which of us has set his sights on the Hereafter and made the effort it requires?

I do not want to speak at length today. There is plenty of talk here, there and everywhere people stand on pulpits. But we face a crisis: the crisis of acting on what we say. It is easy for us to attack people's honour, pry into their affairs, and gain money through what is forbidden or doubtful. It is easy to betray through our eyes, tongues or bodies. Where is the faith that should run through a Muslim's whole body like blood through all his veins? This needs to be examined again.

### More Than Seventy Branches

Servants of Allah, we cannot do without faith. But silent faith moves neither mind nor body, and false faith leads to something worse. The Prophet ﷺ tells us in a well-known hadith, translated here: “Faith has more than seventy branches. The best of them is saying, ‘There is no god but Allah,’ and the lowest is removing harm from the road.”[^1] What lies between the best and the lowest? What lies between “There is no god but Allah” and “removing harm from the road”? Running factories is among the demands of faith. Managing shops is among the demands of faith. Truly raising a sound generation is among the demands of faith. Honourable political work is among the demands of faith. All work belongs among the demands of faith. Let us neither belittle nor neglect it, but recognise the value of what lies before us.

You all know that Allah's promise to support His believing servants is true. Whoever abandons Allah's religion will be abandoned by Allah, and whoever stands up for His message and His trust will be protected by the Lord of all worlds. We all know His words, in meaning: “We will certainly support Our messengers and those who believe” [Ghafir 40:51], and that Allah's soldiers will prevail. This is what the Noble Quran tells us. The matter is settled. What, then, is happening? What is going on within people? Why all this fear, weakness and submission? There is no reason except that faith does not move these people's hearts.

Pharaoh's magicians stood before Pharaoh, the greatest king of that time. What did they say when they declared their submission to Allah as openly as possible? They said, in the meaning of the verse: “No harm! We are returning to our Lord” [al-Shu'ara 26:50]. Their eyes turned away from their reverent view of Pharaoh the ruler towards the Lord of Might, glorified is He. They moved from serving a person, worldly life, money, dollars and dinars to serving the One who creates causes, breaks through causes, and does whatever He wills in His dominion.

I say these words and ask Allah to forgive me and you.

### Worship Trains Us in Commitment

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds. No one can overturn His judgement or turn back His decree. He is not questioned about what He does, while they are questioned. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, the Sovereign, the clear Truth. I bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger. May Allah bless him, his family and all his Companions.

When Allah commanded people to come to the mosques five times each day and night, He wanted to train them to commit to work done by their own hands and to what they produce. When He commanded them to fast thirty days in succession, He wanted to train their souls to refrain from what is not theirs, and to engage with worldly life only through a faith that moves their bodies. When He commanded Hajj, circling the Kaaba and walking between the two hills, He wanted us to turn our memory, our bodies and our direction of prayer back to the origin from which we came. If one of us makes a mistake, he returns; if he disobeys, he repents. We go back and forth, but we do not leave the circle of Islam or our belonging to the Lord of Might, glorified is He. Allah trains us to put faith into practice in every word and deed of our lives. He asks us to live out Islam and keep its duties. So remain committed, servants of Allah. Do not let time become evidence against us before the Lord of all worlds on the Day of Resurrection. If we stay up at a table playing cards or dominoes, we waste time that could have been invested in benefiting the community, developing minds in knowledge and action, raising children and setting a whole generation in motion. If we speak carelessly and neglect the rights of others around us, we squander our own rights before the Lord of all worlds.

Servants of Allah, I remind myself first, before anyone else, and remind my brothers and sisters: the life we live is a trust that the Lord of all worlds has placed in our care. If we waste it, we lose ourselves on the Day of Resurrection. Only one thing is required today: to feel again that we have an identity. A person with an identity always seeks to demonstrate it, declare it and make it known among people. Everything else will follow. Let us not lie when we claim faith. When we stand before Allah on the Day of Resurrection, He will ask us how truthful our claim was.

O Allah, grant victory to our people in Gaza and give it honour. Strengthen their hearts, make their foundations firm, and support them with forces from Your forces, Lord of all worlds. O Allah, bring about a sound course for the Muslim community, in which those who obey You are honoured, those who disobey You are guided, and every stubborn tyrant is humbled. O Allah, guide us among those You have guided, and make the means of obedience easy for us. Our final prayer is: all praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.
