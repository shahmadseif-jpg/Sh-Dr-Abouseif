> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Dearborn Mosque on March 7, 2025.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mTEGCTy72Q).

Imagine a hungry child looking at his father and asking, “Father, aren't we Muslims?” “Yes, my son,” he replies. “Then why are we in this condition?” His father answers, “Allah is testing us through this hunger.” The child asks again, “Father, didn't Allah command the community to help one another?” “Yes.” “Then where is the Muslim community?” Can we expect a child in this position to keep his sense of belonging to his faith when the people of that faith have abandoned him?

The preacher brought this scene to mind during his sermon, which he titled with a verse from the Book of Allah.

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance, and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

We are on a blessed day in a great month that contains the Night of Decree, a month in which people draw closer to their Lord. The preacher began with the condition of children in Yemen who cannot find food: their numbers run into millions, beyond counting. He said that consolation in a loss like this cannot be only words we say. When we say, “We belong to Allah, and to Him we return,” we are also consoling ourselves. But the question remains: what consolation reaches those whose children have died of hunger?

### The Steep Path That Awaits Us

In a translation of the Quran's meaning: “Yet he has not braved the steep path. And what will make you understand what the steep path is? It is freeing a person from bondage, or feeding, on a day of severe hunger, an orphan who is a relative, or a poor person brought low in the dust” [Al-Balad 90:11–16].

Scholars say that this is the steep path of the Hereafter. A person stands before Allah and cannot make the crossing to Paradise unless he brings this evidence. These deeds are the case he presents for crossing, as though he were saying, “I did this and that, so I deserve to cross.”

### Freeing Someone from Bondage in Our Time

We learned that freeing a person from bondage means freeing a slave, and that this time has passed. Yet reality tells us otherwise. There is the person in debt who cannot leave prison because of the price of a refrigerator, a stove, or something we consider trivial. We who live comfortably in America have resources, whether few or many. None of us denies Allah's favor upon him, and we ask Him to grant us the ability to give proper thanks.

The person you free may also be a poor parent who cannot raise his child well, so you raise the child for him, or someone unable to provide for a girl, so you support her. Then comes feeding people “on a day of severe hunger”: during the hardships that have struck our brothers and sisters, men and women, young and old. After Allah, they have no one to turn to but people like you.

Here the preacher makes a comparison that needs no explanation: the dollar one of us spends, or the five dollars for a cup of coffee, or the twenty or thirty dollars at a place where we enjoy ourselves. We do not forbid anyone from doing this, and we have not disobeyed our Lord by doing it. But putting our priorities in order requires each of us to ask himself before asking anyone else: have I given the same amount to those in need, or have I kept it all for myself?

### Hands Clinging to the Dust

“Brought low in the dust” means that their hands cling to the earth because they possess nothing else. If one of them puts a finger in his mouth to give his mind some sense of eating, even an imagined one, he eats only the dust stuck to his hand. How great are the blessings we enjoy, and how terrible is the misery of our brothers and sisters!

### The Question Put to the People of Saqar

The preacher then recalls another scene in the Book of Allah. In a translation of its meaning: “Every soul is held in pledge for what it has earned, except the people of the right. In Gardens, they ask about the guilty: ‘What brought you into Saqar?’ They say, ‘We were not among those who prayed. Nor did we feed the poor. We joined those who indulged in falsehood, and we denied the Day of Judgment, until certainty came to us’” [Al-Muddaththir 74:38–47].

If any of us imagined himself standing before Allah, he could hardly describe himself as one of the people of Paradise. In this answer, neglecting to feed the poor is placed alongside neglecting prayer; they are put on the same level as deeds.

One lesson here is that some acts of worship benefit only the person performing them, while the benefit of others reaches beyond that person. If I pray from Isha until Fajr, which of you has benefited from my prayer? No one, unless I pray for someone. But if I take money from my pocket and give it to this person and that person, I draw closer to Allah through charity just as I draw closer to Him through prayer, and its benefit reaches others. This helps us feel the meaning of the Prophet's saying ﷺ, translated here: “Allah continues to help His servant as long as the servant helps his brother.”[^1] Anyone who cannot do without Allah's help for a moment, and who lives only under His protection, should look around him for people who need his help.

### The Slogan of the Disbelievers

Those who make excuses for not giving say: why should we spend? If Allah had wished, He would have fed them! This is the very slogan of the disbelievers in Surat Ya-Sin. In a translation of the verse's meaning: “Should we feed someone whom Allah could have fed if He wished?” [Ya-Sin 36:47]. The preacher says that we join them—may Allah protect us—when we say, “What has it got to do with me? It is not my problem.”

Then why has Allah made them hungry? To test the one who is fed, to test people with money, those with stores of wealth and bank accounts, and to test some people through others.[^2] This is Allah's way with His creation.

### No! Rather, You Do Not Honor the Orphan

In a translation of the Quran's meaning: “As for the human being, when his Lord tests him by honoring him and granting him comfort, he says, ‘My Lord has honored me.’ But when He tests him by restricting his provision, he says, ‘My Lord has humiliated me.’ No! Rather, you do not honor the orphan. You do not encourage one another to feed the poor. You consume inheritance, taking it all, and you love wealth with an intense love” [Al-Fajr 89:15–20].

The point, the preacher says, is that Allah mentions this after speaking of ‘Ad, Iram, and Pharaoh of the stakes: after the tyranny of those who ruled oppressively on earth and spread corruption. He connects these matters to show us that the safeguard for preserving this world and what it contains is this bond between us, urging one another to feed the poor. Someone may say that it is a small matter. The preacher says no: neglecting it in our hearts has led to these divisions. An Egyptian does not speak about the people of Yemen; an Algerian does not stand beside an Egyptian. We have become divided and scattered, without taking to heart our Lord's words, translated in meaning: “This community of yours is one community, and I am your Lord, so worship Me” [Al-Anbiya 21:92], and elsewhere, “And I am your Lord, so be mindful of Me” [Al-Mu'minun 23:52].[^3] One community can live in harmony only when minds and ranks come together. The different acts of worship we offer express our hearts' belonging to Allah. If cooperation between us is absent, that reveals a flaw in this worship itself.

### Wealth Is a Trust and a Trial

What is in your pocket today, which we ask you to give willingly, will stand as evidence against us before Allah if we fall short. Allah will ask about it: why did you not spend in My cause when I had entrusted it to you?[^4] Let those with wealth understand that their money did not come through their instinct or cleverness. Each of us knows someone around him who is more intelligent and more capable of earning money, yet has not been given what he has. Why, then, did Allah single me out for it rather than someone else? It is Allah's test of me through this wealth, His test of another through intelligence, and His test of a third through patience.

### From Warning to Encouragement

The preacher then moves from warning to encouragement. He says that he is speaking to himself before anyone else, asking Allah to help us receive advice well and protect our hearts from turning away from it. Our Lord says, in a translation of the Quran's meaning:

“The righteous will drink from a cup mixed with camphor, from a spring from which Allah's servants drink, making it flow abundantly. They fulfill their vows and fear a Day whose evil spreads far and wide. They give food, despite their own love for it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive: ‘We feed you only for Allah's sake. We want neither reward nor thanks from you. We fear from our Lord a Day of frowns and severe distress.’ So Allah protects them from the evil of that Day and grants them brightness and joy. He rewards them for their patience with a Garden and silk” [Al-Insan 76:5–12].

These are Allah's words; no comment is needed. Whoever wants to reach the goal should give for it. Whoever reaches it well has done the work well. Whoever seeks the desired end will not find it hard to present the evidence. Some of us are in need, and some are in difficulty. Anyone unable to give money today should offer an intention and a prayer. But our Lord knows best what is in our stores of wealth. None of us should think that he is cleverer than the Lord of all worlds and can deceive Him.

### Closing Supplication

O Allah, feed our brothers and sisters and clothe them. O Allah, make them steadfast in the trials You have placed upon them. O Allah, we ask You to give them help, strength, and support, Lord of all worlds. O Allah, we ask for Your pleasure and Paradise, and seek refuge in You from Your anger and the Fire.
