> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at the Dearborn Mosque on 7 March 2025
> Source: A transcription of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif — [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mTEGCTy72Q)

Imagine a hungry child looking at his father and asking him: Father, aren't we Muslims? He says: Yes, my son. He says: Then why are we in this condition? He answers him: This is Allah's test of us with this starvation. The child returns and says: Father, didn't Allah command the Ummah to cooperate? He says: Yes. The child asks: Then where is the Ummah of Islam? Can a child in this condition be expected to keep any belonging to his religion, when the people of his religion have abandoned him?

The preacher evoked this scene in the course of his sermon, which he titled with a verse from the Book of Allah.

Praise be to Allah. We praise Him, we seek His help, His guidance and His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Allah from the evils of our souls and from our bad deeds, and we bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger ﷺ. To proceed:

We are on a blessed day of a great month in which is Laylat al-Qadr, in which servants draw near to their Lord. And the preacher set out from the condition of the children of Yemen who find no food, whose numbers are in the millions that counting cannot tally. He said that our consolation for a loss like this is not a tongue that we speak, for when we say "Indeed we belong to Allah and indeed to Him we will return," we console ourselves too, and then the question remains: what consolation reaches those whose children died of hunger?

### The pass that awaits us

﴿فَلَا اقْتَحَمَ الْعَقَبَةَ ۝ وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْعَقَبَةُ ۝ فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ ۝ أَوْ إِطْعَامٌ فِي يَوْمٍ ذِي مَسْغَبَةٍ ۝ يَتِيمًا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ ۝ أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ﴾ [البلد: 11-16] (But he has not broken through the difficult pass. And what can make you know what is [breaking through] the difficult pass? It is the freeing of a slave, or feeding on a day of severe hunger an orphan of near relationship, or a needy person in misery.)

The people of knowledge say that it is the pass of the Hereafter: the servant stands before Allah and cannot cross to Paradise unless he presents these witnesses. So they are the crosser's proof, as if he were saying: I did such and such, so I deserve to cross.

### Freeing a neck in our time

We learned that freeing a neck is the emancipation of slaves, and that time has passed. But reality speaks otherwise, for there is the neck of a debtor who does not get out from behind the prison walls over the price of a refrigerator or a stove or what we see as trivial. And we, the people of comfort in America, have of means a little or a lot, and none of us denies Allah's favor upon him, and we ask Him to grant us good thanks.

And the neck that is freed may be the neck of a poor man who cannot raise his child well so you raised him for him, or who cannot provide for a girl so you provided for her. Then comes the feeding "on a day of severe hunger," that is, at the hardships that have befallen our brothers and sisters, men and women, young and old, who have no refuge after Allah except people like you.

And here the preacher presents a comparison that needs no explanation: the dollar one of us spends, or the five for a cup of coffee, and the twenty or thirty in a place where we spend a good time. And we do not forbid anyone that, and we did not disobey our Lord in it, but the ordering of priorities requires each of us to ask himself before asking others: have I put the like of this with those in need, or did I keep it for myself alone?

### Hands stuck to the dust

"In misery (dhā matraba)" means that their hands stuck to the dust, and they own nothing else. So if one of them puts his finger in his mouth to make his mind feel something of eating, even if only apparent, he is only eating the dust clinging to his hand. What blessing are we in, and what misery are our brothers in!

### The question of the people of Saqar

Then the preacher evokes another scene in the Book of Allah: ﴿كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ رَهِينَةٌ ۝ إِلَّا أَصْحَابَ الْيَمِينِ ۝ فِي جَنَّاتٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ ۝ عَنِ الْمُجْرِمِينَ ۝ مَا سَلَكَكُمْ فِي سَقَرَ ۝ قَالُوا لَمْ نَكُ مِنَ الْمُصَلِّينَ ۝ وَلَمْ نَكُ نُطْعِمُ الْمِسْكِينَ ۝ وَكُنَّا نَخُوضُ مَعَ الْخَائِضِينَ ۝ وَكُنَّا نُكَذِّبُ بِيَوْمِ الدِّينِ ۝ حَتَّىٰ أَتَانَا الْيَقِينُ﴾ [المدثر: 38-47] (Every soul, for what it has earned, will be retained, except the companions of the right, [who will be] in gardens, questioning each other about the criminals, [and asking them], "What put you into Saqar?" They will say, "We were not of those who prayed, nor did we used to feed the poor, and we used to enter into vain discourse with those who engaged [in it], and we used to deny the Day of Recompense, until there came to us the certainty.")

If one of us imagined himself before Allah, he would not be good at describing himself as one of the people of Paradise. And in the answer, leaving off feeding was coupled with leaving off prayer, so they were placed in one rank of deed.

And part of the understanding of that is that some acts of worship have a benefit limited to their doer, and some have a benefit that extends to others. If I pray from ʿishāʾ to fajr, which of you benefited from my prayer? No one, unless I pray for someone. But if I take out spending from my pocket and give it to so-and-so and so-and-so, I have drawn near to Allah with charity as I drew near to Him with prayer, and its benefit extended to someone other than me. And through this we sense the significance of his saying ﷺ: «Allah continues to aid the servant as long as the servant is aiding his brother.»[^1] So whoever cannot do without Allah's aid for a moment, and lives only under His covering, let him look to those around him who need his aid.

### The slogan of the people of disbelief

Those who make excuses for leaving spending say: why should we spend? If Allah had willed, He would have fed them! And this is exactly the slogan of the disbelievers in Sūrat Yā Sīn: ﴿أَنُطْعِمُ مَن لَّوْ يَشَاءُ اللَّهُ أَطْعَمَهُ﴾ [يس: 47] (Should we feed one whom, if Allah had willed, He would have fed?). The preacher said: and among them are we, we seek refuge in Allah, when we say: it's my money, it's not my concern.

So why did Allah make them hungry? To test the one who is fed, and to test the owners of wealth and whoever has vaults and bank accounts, and to test some of the servants by means of others.[^2] And that is Allah's way in His creation.

### No! But you do not honor the orphan

﴿فَأَمَّا الْإِنسَانُ إِذَا مَا ابْتَلَاهُ رَبُّهُ فَأَكْرَمَهُ وَنَعَّمَهُ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّي أَكْرَمَنِ ۝ وَأَمَّا إِذَا مَا ابْتَلَاهُ فَقَدَرَ عَلَيْهِ رِزْقَهُ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّي أَهَانَنِ ۝ كَلَّا ۖ بَل لَّا تُكْرِمُونَ الْيَتِيمَ ۝ وَلَا تَحَاضُّونَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ ۝ وَتَأْكُلُونَ التُّرَاثَ أَكْلًا لَّمًّا ۝ وَتُحِبُّونَ الْمَالَ حُبًّا جَمًّا﴾ [الفجر: 15-20] (And as for man, when his Lord tries him and [thus] is generous to him and favors him, he says, "My Lord has honored me." But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says, "My Lord has humiliated me." No! But you do not honor the orphan, and you do not encourage one another to feed the poor, and you consume inheritance, devouring [it] altogether, and you love wealth with immense love.)

And the point of the evidence, as he says, is that Allah mentioned this after speaking of ʿĀd and Iram and Pharaoh of the stakes, that is, after the tyranny of the tyrants on earth and the spreading of corruption, to link for us that the safety valve for preserving this world and what is in it is this connection between us through urging the feeding of the poor. And someone may say that it is a simple matter, and the preacher says: no, for neglecting it in hearts has made us split into these divisions; so an Egyptian does not speak about the people of Yemen, and an Algerian does not stand in the rank of an Egyptian, and we have separated and scattered, and we have not felt the words of our Lord: ﴿إِنَّ هَٰذِهِ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَأَنَا رَبُّكُمْ فَاعْبُدُونِ﴾ [الأنبياء: 92] (Indeed this, your religion, is one religion, and I am your Lord, so worship Me), and in another place: ﴿وَأَنَا رَبُّكُمْ فَاتَّقُونِ﴾ [المؤمنون: 52] (and I am your Lord, so fear Me).[^3] The one nation does not cohere except through the coming together of minds and ranks, and what we give of kinds of worship is a translation of the belonging of hearts to Allah; so if cooperation between us is absent, it points to a flaw in this worship itself.

### Wealth is a trust and a trial

And what is in your pocket today, which we ask of you willingly, will be a proof against us before Allah if we fall short, and Allah will ask about it: why did you not spend in My way when I had made you successors in it?[^4] And let the owners of wealth understand that their wealth was not by their intuition or their cleverness, for each of us knows that around him is someone smarter than he and more able to gather wealth who was not given what he was given. So why did Allah single me out with it and not others? It is Allah's test of me with this wealth, and His test of another with this intelligence, and of a third with this patience.

### From warning to encouragement

Then the preacher moved from warning to encouragement, and said that he is speaking to himself before speaking to anyone, and he asks Allah to grant us good acceptance and protect us from hearts' turning away from advice. Our Lord said:

﴿إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِن كَأْسٍ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا ۝ عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا عِبَادُ اللَّهِ يُفَجِّرُونَهَا تَفْجِيرًا ۝ يُوفُونَ بِالنَّذْرِ وَيَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا كَانَ شَرُّهُ مُسْتَطِيرًا ۝ وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا ۝ إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا ۝ إِنَّا نَخَافُ مِن رَّبِّنَا يَوْمًا عَبُوسًا قَمْطَرِيرًا ۝ فَوَقَاهُمُ اللَّهُ شَرَّ ذَٰلِكَ الْيَوْمِ وَلَقَّاهُمْ نَضْرَةً وَسُرُورًا ۝ وَجَزَاهُم بِمَا صَبَرُوا جَنَّةً وَحَرِيرًا﴾ [الإنسان: 5-12] (Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup [of wine] whose mixture is of Kafur, a spring of which the [righteous] servants of Allah will drink; they will make it gush forth in force [and abundance]. They [are those who] fulfill [their] vows and fear a Day whose evil will be widespread, and they give food in spite of love for it to the needy, the orphan, and the captive.)

The word of Allah, and no comment. Whoever wants to reach the goal, let him give for it, and whoever is good at arriving has done good work, and whoever seeks the aim, presenting the proof is not hard for him. And among us are the needy and among us are those in a squeeze, so whoever cannot give money today, let him give an intention and let him give a supplication. But our Lord knows best what is in our vaults, and let none of us think he is cleverer than the Lord of the worlds and so try to trick Him.

### Conclusion: a supplication

O Allah, feed our brothers and clothe them, O Allah, make them steadfast in what You have tried them with, O Allah, we ask You for them aid, support and backing, O Lord of the worlds. O Allah, we ask You for Your good pleasure and Paradise and we seek refuge in You from Your anger and the Fire.
