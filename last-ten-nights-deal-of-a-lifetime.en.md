> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered on 1 June 2018, on the threshold of the last ten nights of Ramadan
> Source: A transcription of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif — [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ylYMtnFLE-I)

Praise be to Allah. We praise Him, we seek His help, His guidance and His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Allah from the evils of our souls and from our bad deeds. Whomever Allah guides, none can lead astray, and whomever He leads astray, none can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger ﷺ. To proceed:

We are on the threshold of the last ten nights of Ramadan, and preachers treat this sermon as a "cocktail" that must include certain familiar topics every year: the Sunnah of iʿtikāf, zakāt al-fiṭr, seeking Laylat al-Qadr, and striving in worship as far as one is able, following the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ. But I invite you to look at the matter from another angle: the angle of profit and loss.

### An opportunity in which only the negligent loses

We stand before a great opportunity that the successful will seize. What is the logic of a Muslim who believes in Allah and His Messenger, in the Book, in Paradise and the Fire, and then leaves the spoils that Allah has deposited in these days? Come, let us calculate the ratio of profit and loss. Consider your day in Ramadan as you live it: you break your fast with your family or at your friends', then you pray tarāwīḥ, then you return home and sleep a little or stay up, and the dawn comes and you pray it, then you set off to your work. I am speaking here about the pattern of the righteous, not about the one who has left the whole matter and comes only for the Friday prayer. Twenty-four hours turning at the speed of the wind, and so life itself passes. Is that all there is to it?

So what do I lose if I withdraw from the world for some days? I lose days in which I kept away from various kinds of enjoyment, while in my life I am in any case one of the "poor souls" who struggle and are struck and strike back. So if I withdraw from this great burden, even for a short time or partially, it is better.

And what do I gain? I gain myself. The least I gain is that the rust is lifted from my heart, the haze that has accumulated on it year after year. And if we did not carry out a cleaning last year, imagine what has built up over years. Our Lord said: ﴿كَلَّا بَلْ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم﴾ [المطففين: 14] (No! Rather, the stain has covered their hearts), that is, sins have covered them, even the small ones, which pile up and become a balance upon the heart. So the first thing we should gain in these nights is to gain ourselves.

In the past few days social media circulated the story of a young Australian who was a billionaire, whom the doctors told in 2015 that he had cancer and that he had seven months at the very most. So he recorded clips in which he says that the greatest blessing that ever befell him was being afflicted with this illness, because he discovered then that the goals were not what he had been seeking, with sports cars and vast sums. So he left that whole way of life and lived among the poor in Africa, striving to make them happy, and Allah extended his life to nearly three years, which he spent devoting his life and his money to the happiness of those people. That is a practical experience I mention for young people who, I know, have money, ambitions and hope in life, and I ask Allah to bless them in it. But is life itself the goal of our deeds?

### How the Prophet ﷺ teaches us by silent example

See how the books of Sunnah have conveyed to us his state in the last ten nights. He ﷺ does not speak about himself or about his worship; rather, those around him describe him: he used to strive in the last ten.[^1] As if he is presenting a silent practical model, teaching by example and by being a role model.

And we may ask: what kind of striving is this? He ﷺ fasted by day and prayed by night until those around him felt sorry for him and said: O Messenger of Allah, Allah has forgiven you what came before of your sin and what will come after, so be gentle with yourself. And he would answer them: «Should I not be a grateful servant?»[^2] And you hear in the reports that he used to seek Allah's forgiveness more than seventy times in a single day,[^3] so can you imagine a moment passing without remembrance of Allah and worship? And yet he used to strive in the last ten. And I see, and Allah knows best, that this striving took the form of two main things.

### The first thing: iʿtikāf, surrendering yourself to Allah

When you enter prayer and say "Allāhu akbar," you surrender yourself before Allah, but it is a temporary surrender that ends with the words "al-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāh." And when you fast you cut yourself off from food, drink and desire, but that too is a temporary cutting off, and the self still has its share and portion. So when is the self's share broken? When the servant leaves all of that and surrenders himself to Allah completely, not minutes in the Maghrib or ʿIshāʾ prayer, nor an hour or two in tarāwīḥ, but ten days in which he joins night to day while with his Lord, with nothing disturbing him. And in the year in which he ﷺ died, he spent twenty days in iʿtikāf.

The word "muʿtakif" suggests that he wants to be alone with himself and to be with his Lord, with nothing with him to occupy his mind. So there is no path to Facebook or Instagram or WhatsApp in these days; they blur the vision. And ask yourself: what is it that you intend? If you are sincere in your claim to be seeking Paradise, do not let these disturbances distract you from your goal. This is the first thing: purifying the self.

### The second thing: generosity, spending while you are in need

In Ramadan he ﷺ was more generous than the blowing wind,[^4] giving the giving of one who does not fear poverty. And when the last ten came his spending increased. And the question returns: what did he have in his hand to increase from? Did he have a bank account? Did people come to him every night, this one donating to Yemen and that one to Palestine, and then we say: we spent a lot, and keep a little for the Eid? Real spending comes only out of scarcity.

Ask a wealthy man: what is your spending budget in Ramadan? If he has a great deal of money he spends a small part of it, and gives those who come to him small sums here and there, and the world goes on to the end of the month. So where is the proportion between what he spent and what he owns? But the picture changes when there is nothing in the pocket but a little and its owner gives all of it. And in the ḥadīth: «Protect yourselves from the Fire, even with half a date.»[^5] Have we asked ourselves: where is the other half? It is in the mouth of the eater; he bit off half the date and the other half remained in his hand, and so he ﷺ says to him: protect yourself from the Fire with what remains in your hand, when you are most in need of it. That is, spending takes precedence even over finishing your meal, because through it the self is purified and cleansed: ﴿خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ ۖ إِنَّ صَلَاتَكَ سَكَنٌ لَّهُمْ﴾ [التوبة: 103] (Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase, and invoke [Allah's blessings] upon them. Indeed, your invocations are reassurance for them.)

### Do not be stingy with yourself over a few little hours

So do not be stingy with yourselves, even over hours of the day. Come to the mosque with whatever morsel you can carry in your hand, we pray Maghrib together and break our fast together, then we stand in the night prayer, prostrating to Allah sincerely. And I am amazed at people whom Allah chooses to be among those praying tarāwīḥ, and then one of them leaves after four rakʿas. What makes you leave? You may say: I have work. And I respect that, which is why we take care that our prayer is not long, out of respect for people's work the next day. But what do a few minutes change? And what is waiting for you outside? And whoever stays with his imam until he leaves has obtained the standing of a night,[^6] so if you went home after that you would sleep at ease that you had fulfilled and added.

### The idea of insurance, as you put forward for yourself with Allah

Servants of Allah, Allah is free of need of your worship and your money, indeed free of need of all of us, but He is the Self-Sufficient, the Possessor of mercy. And part of His mercy is that He opened for us the doors of worship, made charity a path to His good pleasure, and the Prophet ﷺ set down for us the night prayer in addition to the obligatory. So do not be stingy with yourself; the days are speeding up and events follow one another, and the self is weak, and nothing strengthens it in the crush of life except its connection to Allah. And the servant does not know what illness hides in his body whose time to appear has not yet come, and what complaint will come from him after days, months or years.

So let us put before us the idea of "insurance." Insurance companies are all built on convincing people that they will be with them when they need them, and they boast that they are the fastest in service and the first to come to your aid. And they give you nothing except at the time of need, however large their savings. So why do we not achieve the same substance with Allah? ﴿أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُ﴾ [الزمر: 36] (Is not Allah sufficient for His servant?). Allah has guaranteed the provision of creation, so what notion is this, that He would leave you to need? Far be it from Him, glory be to Him. So let what is in your hand be guaranteed with Allah, and it is He who says: ﴿وَلْيَخْشَ الَّذِينَ لَوْ تَرَكُوا مِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّةً ضِعَافًا خَافُوا عَلَيْهِمْ فَلْيَتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَلْيَقُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا﴾ [النساء: 9] (And let those [executors and guardians] fear [injustice] as if they had left behind them weak offspring, for whom they would fear. So let them fear Allah and speak words of appropriate justice.) Whoever gives in charity secures the lives of his children by what he gives.

### Zakāt al-fiṭr: what feeds and what purifies

And I will not leave the Friday without a word on zakāt al-fiṭr, for it is a matter of the utmost importance. Let none of you say: I paid a small amount and was relieved. The matter is relative between your condition and the conditions of the poor, and the Prophet ﷺ set two aims: «a purification for the fasting person from idle talk and obscenity, and food for the poor.»[^7] And the basic idea of food is that it should satisfy, so the spending should be according to what satisfies the poor person. And some of the shaykhs used to say: since the ḥadīth says "a purification for the fasting person," look at what you committed throughout the month and purify in the amount that suits the gaps in what proceeded from you.

And Allah made the poor person a part of life only as a test for the rich, and had He willed He would have enriched all people. And the One who gave you is able to give someone else, so if He singled you out with giving, He is testing you. So do not be stingy with yourself, for you are only giving your future and securing your life and the lives of your children.

### Conclusion: a supplication

We ask Allah to guide us to the deeds most beloved to Him, to take us by our forelocks toward Him, to keep our religion and our worldly life safe for us, to accept from us and from you righteous deeds in these blessed days, and not to bring us out of Ramadan except having forgiven our sins and written us with Him among the righteous. O Allah, prepare for the nation of Your Prophet a matter of right guidance, in which the people of obedience to You are honored and the people of disobedience to You are guided. And praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds.
