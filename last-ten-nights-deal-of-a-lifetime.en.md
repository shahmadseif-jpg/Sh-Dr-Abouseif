> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered on June 1, 2018, as the last ten days of Ramadan approached.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ylYMtnFLE-I).

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. Whoever Allah guides, no one can lead astray; whoever He leads astray, no one can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

We are approaching the last ten days of Ramadan. Preachers tend to see this sermon as a “cocktail” that must include the same familiar subjects every year: the practice of i‘tikaf, retreating to the mosque for worship; zakat al-fitr; seeking the Night of Decree; and doing as much worship as we can, following the Prophet's example ﷺ. But I invite you to look at the matter from another angle: profit and loss.

### An Opportunity Lost Only by Those Who Neglect It

We face a great opportunity, one that successful people will seize. How does it make sense for a Muslim to believe in Allah, His Messenger, the Book, Paradise and Hell, then leave behind the reward Allah has placed in these days? Let us work out the balance of gains and losses. Think about your day in Ramadan as you live it. You break your fast with your family or at a friend's home, pray Tarawih, then return home to sleep a little or stay awake. Fajr comes, you pray it, then go to work. I am speaking here about the routine of righteous people, not those who have left everything aside and come only for Friday prayer. Twenty-four hours race past like the wind, and life itself passes in just this way. Is that all there is?

What would I lose if I stepped away from worldly life for a few days? I would lose a few days of its pleasures. In my everyday life, I am already one of the ordinary people struggling to get by, taking blows and striking back. Stepping away from this great burden, even briefly or for part of the time, would be a good thing.

And what would I gain? I would gain myself. At the very least, the covering over my heart would be lifted: the haze that has built up year after year. If we did not clean it away last year, imagine how much has gathered over several years. Our Lord says, in a translation of the meaning: “No! Rather, a covering has formed over their hearts” [Al-Mutaffifin 83:14]. Sins have covered them; even small sins build up into a weight upon the heart. The first gain we should seek in these nights is to regain ourselves.

In recent days, social media has shared the story of a young Australian billionaire. In 2015, doctors told him that he had cancer and no more than seven months to live. He recorded videos saying that the greatest blessing he had received was being tested with this illness. It had shown him that life's goals were not the sports cars and huge sums of money he had pursued. He left that whole way of life and lived among poor people in Africa, working to bring them happiness. Allah extended his life to almost three years, which he spent devoting his life and wealth to making those people happy. I mention this real-life experience to young people whom I know have money, ambitions and hopes for life, and I ask Allah to bless these for them. But is life itself the goal of our work?

### How the Prophet ﷺ Teaches through a Quiet Example

Look at how the books of Sunnah describe him during the last ten days. He ﷺ does not speak about himself or his worship. Instead, those around him describe him: he would strive harder during the last ten days.[^1] It is as though he offers a quiet, practical model, teaching through example.

We may ask: what kind of extra effort was this? He ﷺ already fasted by day and stood in prayer by night, until those around him felt for him and said: Messenger of Allah, Allah has forgiven your past and future sins, so be gentle with yourself. He would answer, in meaning: “Should I not be a grateful servant?”[^2] You hear in the narrations that he sought Allah's forgiveness more than seventy times in a single day.[^3] Can you imagine a second passing without remembrance of Allah and worship? Even so, he would strive harder during the last ten days. In my view—and Allah knows best—this extra effort took two main forms.

### The First: I‘tikaf—Giving Yourself to Allah

When you begin prayer and say, “Allah is greatest,” you place yourself before Allah. But this surrender is temporary; it ends with the words, “Peace and Allah's mercy be upon you.” When you fast, you keep yourself away from food, drink and desire. This too is temporary, and the self still has its share. When, then, is the self's demand for its own share broken? When a servant leaves all of this and gives himself completely to Allah. Not for a few minutes in Maghrib or Isha prayer, or an hour or two of Tarawih, but for ten days, joining night to day in the company of his Lord, with nothing to distract him. In the year in which he died, the Prophet ﷺ spent twenty days in i‘tikaf.

The word “one in i‘tikaf” suggests a person who wants to be alone with himself and with his Lord, with nothing to occupy his mind. There should be no room for Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp during these days; they cloud the view. Ask yourself: what are you seeking? If you are sincere when you say you seek Paradise, do not let these distractions turn you away from your goal. This is the first matter: purifying the self.

### The Second: Generosity—Giving While You Are in Need

In Ramadan, he ﷺ was more generous than a freely blowing wind.[^4] He gave like someone who did not fear poverty. When the last ten days came, he gave even more. The question returns: what did he have in his hands that allowed him to give more? Did he have a bank account? Did people come to him every night, one donating for Yemen and another for Palestine, after which we say, “We have given a great deal,” and keep a little back for Eid? Real giving comes only when one has little.

Ask a wealthy person: what is your budget for giving in Ramadan? If he has a great deal, he spends a small part of it. He gives small amounts here and there to those who come to him, and life moves on until the end of the month. What is the proportion between what he has given and what he owns? The picture changes when someone has only a little in his pocket and gives it all. The hadith says, in meaning: “Protect yourselves from the Fire, even with half a date.”[^5] Have we asked ourselves where the other half is? It is in the eater's mouth. He has bitten off half the date and still holds the other half. The Prophet ﷺ tells him: protect yourself from the Fire with what remains in your hand, when you need it most. Giving thus comes before even finishing your food, because it purifies and cleanses the self. The Quran says, in meaning: “Take charity from their wealth to cleanse and purify them through it, and pray for them; your prayer is a source of peace for them” [Al-Tawbah 9:103].

### Do Not Deny Yourself a Few Hours

Do not be ungenerous towards yourselves, even with a few hours of the day. Come to the mosque carrying whatever food you can. We will pray Maghrib together, break our fast together, then spend the night in prayer, prostrating sincerely before Allah. I am surprised by people whom Allah chooses to be among those praying Tarawih, only for one of them to leave after four units. What draws you away? You may say, “I have work.” I respect that. It is why we make sure our prayer is not too long, out of respect for people's work the next day. But what difference will a few minutes make? What is waiting for you outside? Whoever stays with his imam until the imam finishes gains the reward of a whole night in prayer.[^6] If you then went home, you could sleep comfortably, knowing that you had fulfilled your duty and done more.

### Thinking of Insurance: Sending Something Ahead with Allah

Servants of Allah, Allah has no need of your worship or wealth. Indeed, He has no need of any of us. Yet He is the Self-Sufficient, full of mercy. In His mercy, He has opened the doors of worship to us and made charity a way to His pleasure. The Prophet ﷺ taught us night prayer in addition to the obligatory prayers. Do not deny yourself this good. Days race past and events follow one another. The self is weak, and amid life's pressures, nothing strengthens it except its bond with Allah. A servant does not know what illness may lie hidden in his body, waiting for its time to appear, or what pain he may complain of after a few days, months or years.

Let us consider the idea of “insurance” for what lies ahead. Insurance companies are all built on persuading people that they will be there when needed. They boast of being the quickest to serve you and the first to come to your aid. However great their reserves, they pay you only when you need them. Why do we not seek this same assurance with Allah? The Quran asks, in meaning: “Is Allah not sufficient for His servant?” [Al-Zumar 39:36]. Allah has guaranteed provision for His creation. How could we imagine that He would abandon you to need? Far exalted is He above that. Let what is in your hand be held safely with Allah. He says, in meaning: “Let those who would fear for weak children left behind them be concerned. Let them be mindful of Allah and speak rightly” [Al-Nisa 4:9]. Whoever gives in charity secures his children's lives through what he gives.

### Zakat al-Fitr: Food That Satisfies, Giving That Purifies

I cannot leave this Friday without a word about zakat al-fitr. It is a matter of great importance. None of you should say, “I paid a small amount, and now I am done.” The matter concerns the relationship between your circumstances and those of the poor. The Prophet ﷺ set out two purposes, in meaning: “Purification for the fasting person from idle and indecent speech, and food for the poor.”[^7] The basic purpose of food is to satisfy hunger, so giving should be enough to satisfy a poor person. Some shaykhs used to say: since the hadith calls it “purification for the fasting person,” look at what you have done during the month and give an amount that suits the shortcomings you have committed.

Allah placed the poor in this life as a test for the rich. Had He willed, He could have made everyone rich. The One who gave to you can give to others. If He has singled you out for a gift, He is testing you. Do not deny yourself this good. You are giving to your own future and securing your life and the lives of your children.

### Conclusion: A Supplication

We ask Allah to guide us to the deeds He loves most, to take us by our forelocks and lead us to Him, and to preserve our religion and worldly life. May He accept our good deeds and yours during these blessed days, and not let us leave Ramadan until He has forgiven our sins and recorded us among the righteous. O Allah, bring about wise guidance for the community of Your Prophet, through which those who obey You are honoured and those who disobey You are guided. All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.
