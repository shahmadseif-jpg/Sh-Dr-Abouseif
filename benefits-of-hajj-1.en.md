All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Him from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. His is all sovereignty and all praise. In His hand are the keys of the heavens and the earth, and He has power over all things. We bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. He conveyed the message, fulfilled the trust, sincerely counselled the community, and through him Allah lifted its distress. He strove for Allah as He should be striven for. May Allah reward him on our behalf and on behalf of the Muslim community with the finest reward. To continue:

### Hearts Longing for the House

Speaking about Hajj stirs a special feeling. Believers look at the House of Allah not with their eyes, but with their hearts.[^1] It is their hearts that move. Whenever Hajj is mentioned, each of us feels a longing unlike our desire for any pleasure of this world.

Allah says, in a translation of the meaning: “Proclaim the pilgrimage to people; they will come to you” [Al-Hajj 22:27]. It has never occurred to anyone that the Lord of all worlds would call one of His creatures and that person would not come. Everyone comes. There is no place for weighing things up, strategy or delay: as long as they believe, they come directly. The one thing that stands between a servant and visiting the House of Allah, as we understand from the verse, is that the call has not reached you yet. When Allah calls, and His caller calls upon the earth, you say: “Here I am.” Your response appears on your tongue when you say, “Here I am, O Allah, here I am.” It appears in your spending when you give everything you own for a glimpse of the House of Allah. It becomes a practical response as your body moves and you hasten towards the place your heart loves, where your soul finds rest and your limbs become still. It is the place Allah made safe, a home for hearts to find calm and peace. It witnessed the mission of the Prophet ﷺ and the prophets before him, and people come on pilgrimage to it from every part of the earth.

### Variety in Worship Is a Mercy from Allah

In His wisdom, Allah, glorified is He, has required people to perform different kinds of worship, to test them. One person finds a particular act of worship suits him and loves it, while another does not suit him. This variety is part of Allah's wisdom and kindness. If we ask about the pillars of Islam, they are: bearing witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah; establishing prayer; giving zakat; fasting Ramadan; and making pilgrimage to the House. These are the five obligations, and each obligation has voluntary acts alongside it.

The obligation of bearing witness is fulfilled once in a lifetime: to acknowledge that Allah is One, that the Messenger is His final Prophet, that the Quran was sent down by the Lord of all worlds, and that Hell and Paradise are real. Its voluntary counterpart is remembrance of Allah. The second obligation is prayer: five prayers by day and night, followed by voluntary prayers—strongly emphasised Sunnah prayers, less emphasised Sunnah prayers, and other voluntary prayers you offer by night or day. Then there is obligatory fasting in Ramadan, with voluntary fasting during the rest of the year if you wish. Hajj is required once in your lifetime; after the obligatory pilgrimage, you may perform Hajj and Umrah as often as you wish.

Each of us finds certain deeds suit us and help us draw close to Allah. Some of us love to stand in prayer in the depths of the night. To such a person, two units of night prayer are worth the treasures of the world, as he speaks privately to his Beloved, glorified is He. Some people simply love to give and see smiles on people's faces. Meeting someone's need or paying off his debt is where they find happiness and closeness to the Lord of all worlds. Others love to fast in the heat or cold of the day. A little hunger and thirst nourish their faith, and they feel as though they are soaring through the heavens. The calls to Maghrib and Fajr prayer are the most beautiful sounds in their lives. Others show no special eagerness for these acts, but love remembrance and supplication. When they sit, their tongues are never without Allah's remembrance. Walking down the road, they seem to be talking to themselves. If you ask, they say: now is a time for seeking forgiveness, or now is a time for praise.

People differ in what suits their nature and character. This variety makes the worship Allah has prescribed a mercy at its foundation. You were not commanded to fast the entire year, but only one month. If you do more, that is what suits you. Night prayer was not made obligatory upon everyone, only the five daily prayers. If you add more, that is what suits you. You were not commanded to pay zakat beyond the amount Allah and His Messenger ﷺ set. If you give more in charity, that is what suits you.

### Allah's Will before Your Own

Yet whatever is required of you, you do it not because it suits you, but because it pleases the Creator of all. There is an important matter we must believe: every believer must give priority to what Allah wants over what he himself wants. If something you love is before you and the caller announces prayer, which will you put first? If you have money to complete some details of your own life, while someone else needs it, you consider which choice pleases Allah more. You follow what pleases Him more, rather than simply satisfying yourself and your desires.

It is reported that the Prophet ﷺ said, in meaning: “None of you believes until his desires follow what I have brought.”[^2] The desire you love then moves beyond its purely human, natural setting and becomes something you act upon because Allah loves it. If you eat, you eat in a way and measure that please Allah. When you are with your spouse, you act in a way that pleases the Lord of all worlds. How do you change your intention from simply living as a human being to living as a servant of Allah? Through this, you gain good deeds from every direction: every word you speak, every movement you make, every effort you undertake on earth, every bite and every drink. Everything in your life is for the Lord of all worlds.

### Ability and a Valid Excuse

Hajj requires that you have the ability to provide what is needed to carry it out. Allah has not made it obligatory upon those who are unable. This inability may be material or non-material. Material inability includes lacking the health to walk or travel, or lacking the money to pay the expenses. Non-material inability includes fear that, if one goes, one's life will be lost, or fear of death. Whatever form such a danger takes, it is recognised in Allah's law. Our Lord knows your true circumstances.

It is established that, during one of his expeditions, the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions, in meaning: “There are people in Medina for whom a reward like yours is recorded for every journey you make and every valley you cross.” The Companions were surprised: how could those people receive this, when we travel, work hard, spend, give, grow tired and face hardship, while they do none of it? The Prophet ﷺ explained the reason: “A valid excuse held them back.”[^3]

[^2]: Editor's source note: Al-Nawawi included it in his Forty Hadith, as hadith 41, attributed it to Kitab al-Hujjah, and said: “A hasan sahih hadith. Among them was Ibn Rajab, in Jami‘ al-Ulum wa al-Hikam (2/393), who said: “Authenticating this hadith is very far-fetched for several reasons.” Al-Albani, in his verification of al-Sunnah by Ibn Abi Asim (15), said: “Its chain is weak.” In al-Qa'id ila Tashih al-Aqa'id, he said: “Its chain includes Nu‘aym ibn Hammad, who has weakness.” Shu‘ayb al-Arna'ut, in his verification of Sharh al-Sunnah, also said: “Its chain is weak.” The original report is in Ibn Abi Asim's al-Sunnah (15), and al-Khatib al-Baghdadi included it in Tarikh Baghdad (4/368), from Abdullah ibn Amr, may Allah be pleased with him and his father. It is therefore introduced here with “it is reported,” without a firm judgement that it is authentic.
[^3]: Editor's source note: Al-Bukhari reports from Anas, may Allah be pleased with him: “There are men in Medina who were with you on every journey you made and every valley you crossed; a valid excuse held them back.” Muslim reports a similar narration from Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him. The preacher's wording is close to their meaning. His description of the Companions' objection and question is his own telling, not part of the hadith's wording.
