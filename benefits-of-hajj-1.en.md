All praise is due to Allah; we praise Him, seek His help, seek His guidance and seek His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Him from the evils of our own selves and from our misdeeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise; in His hand are the keys of the heavens and the earth, and He is able to do all things. And we bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, His Prophet and His Messenger ﷺ, who conveyed the message, fulfilled the trust, gave sincere counsel to the Ummah, through whom Allah lifted the gloom, and who strove in Allah with true striving; so may Allah reward him on our behalf and on behalf of the Ummah of Islam with the best of rewards. To proceed:

### The yearning of hearts toward the House

Speaking of Hajj stirs particular emotions. For the believers do not look at the House of Allah with their eyes but with their hearts,[^1] and it is the hearts that are moved. Whenever the story of Hajj is mentioned, each of us finds in himself a yearning whose significance and meaning differ from his longing for any of the enjoyments of this world.

The Exalted said: ﴿وَأَذِّن فِي النَّاسِ بِالْحَجِّ يَأْتُوكَ﴾ [الحج: 27] (And proclaim to the people the Hajj [pilgrimage]; they will come to you). It never crossed the mind of anyone in humanity that one of the creatures whom the Lord of the worlds calls would then not come. All come, and there is no difference between thinking, strategy and delay: so long as they are believers, they come directly. And the one thing that stands between the servant and visiting the House of Allah, which we understand from the verse, is that the call has not yet come to you. When Allah has called and His caller has called on the earth, you say: Labbayk (At Your service). A response that shows on your tongue when you say "Labbayk Allāhumma labbayk"; a response that shows in your spending when you pay all you own as a ransom for a glance and a view of the House of Allah; and a practical response by which the body moves, so you set out in haste, longing to meet the homeland your heart loves, in which your soul finds rest and your limbs find stillness. This homeland that Allah made a place of security and of the reassurance and calm of souls, which witnessed the sending of the Prophet ﷺ and the prophets before him, and to which people make pilgrimage from all regions of the earth.

### The variety of acts of worship is a mercy from Allah

Allah, glory be to Him, in His wisdom made obligatory upon people different kinds of worship, to test them; so there are people for whom one act of worship is suited and so they love it, and another is not suited to them. This variety is from Allah's wisdom and gentleness. If we ask about the pillars of Islam, they are: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the prayer, giving the zakāh, fasting Ramadan, and the Hajj to the House. These are the five obligations, and every obligation has a voluntary counterpart.

The obligation of testimony needs only once in a lifetime from you: to acknowledge that Allah is One, that the Messenger is His final Prophet, that the Qur'an is sent down from the Lord of the worlds, and that the Fire is true and Paradise is true; and its voluntary counterpart is dhikr. The second obligation is the prayer: five prayers in the day and night, then voluntary ones: the emphasized Sunnas, the non-emphasized Sunnas, and the optional prayer you offer by night or day. Then the fasting obligatory in Ramadan, and for the rest of the year you fast voluntarily if you wish. Then Hajj is obligatory upon you once in a lifetime, and after the obligatory Hajj you perform Hajj as you wish and ʿumra as you wish.

And each of us has what suits him of the deeds that help him draw near to Allah. Among us is one who loves to stand in the depth of the night, and two rakʿas at night are to him equal to the treasures of the world, as he confides in his Beloved, glory be to Him. Among people is one who only loves to spend and to look at the smile of the people on their faces, and when he meets one person's need or pays off his debt, that is the place of his happiness and of his feeling of nearness to the Lord of the worlds. Among them is one who loves fasting in the heat of the day or its cold, who feels some hunger and thirst, and that quenches his faith and he soars as if in the atmosphere of the heavens, and the call to prayer at sunset and the call to prayer at dawn are to him the most beautiful sounds in his life. And among them is one in whom you find no inclination to this or that, but he loves dhikr and supplication, so his tongue is never without the remembrance of Allah when he sits, and when he walks along a road you would think he is talking to himself, and if you ask him he says: Now is the time for seeking forgiveness, or: Now is the time for praise.

People differ in what suits their natures and their innate dispositions, and this variety makes what Allah has legislated of acts of worship, in its root, a mercy from Him: you were not commanded to fast the whole year, but a month of it, and if you add, that suits you. You were not commanded to pray at night as an obligation upon everyone, but the five prayers, and if you add, that is what suits you. And you were not commanded to give zakāh except in the amount that Allah and His Messenger ﷺ determined, and if you increase in charity, this suits you.

### Allah's will before your will

But whatever is required of you, you do it not because it suits your self, but because it pleases the Creator of creation. And we have an important matter that we must believe: that putting Allah's will first is required of every believer over putting your own will first. If you have in front of you something you love and the muezzin calls to prayer, which of the two do you put first? And if you have in front of you money with which to complete the details of your life and another person who needs it, you look at which pleases Allah more, and you go with what pleases Him more rather than satisfying yourself and your own self.

And it is related from the Prophet ﷺ that he said: «None of you truly believes until his desire follows what I have brought.»[^2] So the desire you love turns from its purely human, innate frame into something you do because Allah loves it: if you eat, you eat to the extent that pleases Allah, and if you are with your family, you are so to the extent that makes the Lord of the worlds happy. How do you turn your intention from practising your life as a human being to practising it as a servant of Allah? In this way you take rewards from every side: every word that comes out, every movement you make, every effort with which you strike out in the land, every meal and every drink; everything in your life is for the Lord of the worlds.

### Capacity and excuse

And the condition of Hajj is that you have the ability to give what achieves its purpose, for Allah did not make Hajj obligatory upon the incapable. This incapacity is either material or moral. The material is that a person does not have the health with which to walk or travel, or does not have the financial ability to bear the expense. The moral incapacity is that he has fear that if he goes his life will be lost or he fears destruction for himself; whatever the means that express that, they are taken into account in Allah's law. And our Lord assesses what you truly are in.

And it is established that the Prophet ﷺ, while on one of his expeditions, told his Companions, saying: «In Medina there are people: you have not travelled a journey and not crossed a valley except that a reward is written for them like yours.» The Companions were astonished: How do they attain that while we march and strive and spend and give and tire and it is hard on us, and they give nothing? So the Prophet ﷺ made clear that the reason is: «Excuse held them back.»[^3]

[^3]: Editor's documentation: reported by al-Bukhārī from the ḥadīth of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him: "In Medina there are men: you have not travelled a journey and not crossed a valley except that they were with you; excuse held them back," and by Muslim from the ḥadīth of Jābir, may Allah be pleased with him, in similar terms.
