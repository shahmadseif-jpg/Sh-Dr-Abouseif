> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Al-Nahda Mosque, Illinois, on October 17, 2025.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcIxsMWOIXY).

We praise Allah, glorified is He, and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah, blessed and exalted is He, from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. Whoever Allah guides, no one can lead astray; whoever He leads astray, no one can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. His is all sovereignty and all praise. In His hand are the keys of the heavens and the earth, and He has power over all things. We bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. May Allah bless him, his family, his Companions and all who follow his path until the Day of Judgement. To continue:

I cannot pass over what our shaykh kindly said before Friday prayer about maintaining family ties. It belongs within the subject of our sermon: “The final outcome belongs to taqwa”—being mindful of Allah. Maintaining family ties is part of a complete system of divine law through which Allah intended to guide people's lives and conduct. It is not a grip that restricts movement, but a way that keeps us on course. Long ago, we were taught that our family is the safeguard in our lives: our relatives, maternal and paternal uncles, and cousins on both sides. They surround us with care, and we do the same for them.

### When People Began to Fear Their Relatives

Yet things have become so bad that people are afraid to show their blessings before their own family and relatives. We tell our children: “Do not say that in front of your aunt. Do not mention this in front of your uncle. Be careful: the home has its secrets.” Some of that is right, but not all of it; our religion does require us to keep confidences. The question remains: how did we reach this degree of bitterness between relatives, this envy between brothers and sisters born of the same mother and father?

The answer is that when religious devotion leaves people's hearts, they are driven to follow Satan. These are his actions and his whispers. That is why the first conflict on earth was between two brothers. Allah says, in meaning: “Recount to them truthfully the story of Adam's two sons. Each offered a sacrifice, but it was accepted from one and not from the other. He said, ‘I will certainly kill you.’ The other replied, ‘Allah accepts only from those mindful of Him. If you stretch out your hand to kill me, I will not stretch out mine to kill you. I fear Allah, Lord of all worlds. I would rather you bear the sin of killing me along with your own sin, and become one of the people of the Fire. That is the recompense of wrongdoers’” [Al-Ma'idah 5:27–29]. Here we see the same disease that had appeared earlier between Satan and Adam. Satan managed to spread his poison within one of the two sons, but not the other.

The words of the wronged brother contain a complete way of thinking. First: how should I respond to harm that comes not from a cousin, but from my own brother? Second: how do I think about outcomes and consequences, rather than the event alone? Third: how do I place the whole matter in Allah's hands alone? And what did the other brother want? There are many accounts, none of them sound. But consider any possible motive: a woman he wanted to marry or money he wanted to take. From beginning to end, you will find worldly concerns: figures, accounts, money, inheritance, land and a farm. All are placed within a very small circle called the circle of worldly life.

### The Circle of Worldly Life: “Why Isn't It Mine?”

It is within this circle that people compete. If someone sees you wearing a new shirt, he looks at the shirt rather than your heart. If you have renewed something in your home, that is what draws his eye. Yet worldly life will pass away. A wise person keeps recalling the meaning of Allah's words: “Allah accepts only from those mindful of Him” [Al-Ma'idah 5:27]. He puts his relationship with Allah before everything else and beyond his dealings with anyone. He does not fix his attention on what people do. Instead, he feels compassion for them because Satan has gained power over their hearts and changed them from what they ought to be.

I say this with pain: when I need someone to stand beside me and support me, I want my brother. He may share my father, my mother, or the bond of faith. I want the brother who will not let me be wronged while he is beside me, or humiliated while he can uphold my dignity. I will not be left in need while he has money in his pocket, because it is mine too. He will raise my child if I die, protect my honour if I am absent, help my wealth grow if I neglect it, and guard my equipment and supplies if my mind is elsewhere. These understandings have disappeared from people's lives because the principles of faith that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ established among them have disappeared. Satan has become the force moving minds and hearts. People's thoughts now follow the pattern he first set in the world when he said, “I am better than him.” In the same way, people around you look at what you have and say: “Why isn't it mine? He is beneath me. He owns a building—why him and not me? His children do well in school, and his life is settled. Why?” The same thought returns in different words. If we devoted our efforts to asking Allah to give us blessings like those He has given others, we would succeed and follow the Prophet's way ﷺ.

### Mutual Love and Mercy: Like One Body

The Prophet ﷺ taught people how to live as brothers and sisters. Our shaykh reminded us of his saying, translated in meaning: “The believers, in their mutual love, mercy and compassion, are like a body: when one part suffers, the rest of the body responds with sleeplessness and fever.”[^1] Remember those two words: love and mercy. In the Quran, Allah says, in meaning: “And He placed love and mercy between you” [Ar-Rum 30:21]. Our Lord is speaking about the relationship between spouses. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ speaks about the relationship between believers and describes it in terms of love and mercy. It is as though he raises my relationship with you to the level of my relationship with my own household: those whose lives are joined to mine day and night, with whom I eat, feel, think, grieve and rejoice. This relationship brings together not only thought, but thought and feeling, heart and heartbeat, the movement of the body and the movement of the mind. It brings people together in a bond that should not be broken. If two people must part, whether they are joined by blood or by faith, the principle is expressed in the Quranic words, in meaning: “Either keep them honourably or release them graciously” [Al-Baqarah 2:229]. There should be no fighting and hostility. The law's original purpose does not allow them to fight.

People compete for worldly life, although it is only a small provision that comes to a person and then leaves after a time. Allah bestows blessings on whom He wills and takes them away from whom He wills. Whoever turns to Allah and seeks what He has will receive in full what his Lord grants him. Whoever cuts himself off from Allah deprives himself of every worldly blessing. Even if he possessed the wealth of Qarun, he would enjoy none of it, because his heart is empty.

Servants of Allah, I urge myself and you to be mindful of Allah in how we treat our families, children, mothers, fathers, and paternal and maternal uncles. Life turns. Whoever does good today will find goodness tomorrow. If that goodness does not come in this world, it will come in the Hereafter, and the One who rewards him will be the Lord of Might, glorified is He.

I say these words and ask Allah to forgive me and you.

### What Purpose Did We Come Here to Fulfil?

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds. The final outcome belongs to those mindful of Him, and hostility is only against wrongdoers. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, the Sovereign, the clear Truth. I bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. May Allah bless him, his family and all his Companions.

When our vision narrowed to worldly life and its contents—to figures and counting them, stores of wealth and filling them, banks and accounts—we lost sight of the central purpose for which we came into this life and for which we will be held accountable when we leave it. Our Lord says, in meaning: “When your Lord said to the angels, ‘I am placing a steward on earth,’ they said, ‘Will You place there someone who spreads corruption and sheds blood, while we glorify You with praise and declare Your holiness?’ He said, ‘I know what you do not know’” [Al-Baqarah 2:30]. In this verse, Allah tells us that He made you, me and each one of us a steward who carries out what Allah wills. We abandoned that and turned towards what we ourselves wanted. We stopped looking outward and then inward, and lost sight of the purpose for which we are here.

Let us take a simple example. If one of us comes from far away, driving for more than eighteen hours to attend this mosque, how could he fail to arrive early? How could he fail to be alert, listen attentively to his shaykh, and respect those sitting before him? This is what he came to do. If he does not do it, he has failed. We were sent into life so that each of us would be a steward for Allah on His earth. If we lose sight of this, we fail. People lost sight of what Allah wanted from them and turned to what Satan wanted from them. They lost sight of the highest values and became absorbed in the trivial things of this earth.

### Yusuf: He Did Not Forget His Purpose in Prison

Yusuf, peace be upon him, suffered a terrible injustice. He faced a shameful accusation and was wrongly sent to prison. How did he respond? He did not forget his purpose. In prison, he said to his two companions, in the meaning of the Quran: “My two fellow prisoners, are many separate lords better, or Allah, the One, the All-Subduing?” [Yusuf 12:39]. Later, when he was asked, “Explain to us the meaning of seven fat cows” [Yusuf 12:46, meaning], and saw an opportunity to leave prison, he did not say, “I am here unjustly. I want to get out.” He did not say that at all. Instead, he said, “You will sow diligently for seven years” [Yusuf 12:47, meaning]. He had not forgotten his purpose: I am not in this world to be absorbed in myself. I am here to build up a world that Allah commanded me to build, spread a religion that Allah commanded me to spread, and help people do what is right. Whoever loses sight of this purpose argues with his wife, quarrels with his children, faces life with a frown and feels trapped by his circumstances. But whoever lives for Allah lives with security, stability, calm and peace. He cares for people, shares what he has and gives to others.

### Abu Bakr and Umar: The Believer's Way of Thinking

I will close with an example so that I do not keep you too long. What was Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, thinking when he gave the Prophet ﷺ everything he had? He was a very wealthy man. Through trade, he could earn in a short time what others could not. Whenever the Prophet ﷺ asked for contributions, he would bring everything he had. The Prophet would ask him, “What have you left for your family?” He would reply, “I have left them Allah and His Messenger.”[^2] How was he thinking? He was thinking of heaven, of Paradise, and that this might be the last gift he could give. He wanted to gain Paradise at the highest price, whatever the cost. If one of us developed heart disease and was told that an operation would cost a certain amount, he would say, “Whatever it costs, I will spend everything I have to regain my health.” Have you ever seen a sensible person keep his money and refuse to spend anything to save himself? This was Abu Bakr al-Siddiq's way of thinking.

Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, tried to compete once or twice. He brought half his wealth. When the Prophet ﷺ asked him what he had left for his family, he replied, “I left half my wealth.” Consider this: who is the winner in worldly terms? The one who still has half his money can develop his trade and increase his accounts. The one who gave everything will begin again from nothing. Yet this is the believer's way of thinking.

O Allah, bring the affairs of our people in Gaza to a good conclusion, Lord of all worlds. O Allah, do not let their efforts go to waste. Do not scatter them, divide their voice or break their ranks. O Allah, make them steadfast, help them and sustain them with Your support. O Allah, comfort their hearts, guide their efforts and bless their steps. Forgive our failures towards them and towards the call to You and Your religion. O Allah, open our hearts to obey You and fill them with certainty in what You have promised. O Allah, keep our homes safe from Satan's schemes. Do not give power over us to anyone who neither fears You in dealing with us nor shows us mercy. Comfort our hearts, guide our efforts and bless our steps, Lord of all worlds. Our final prayer is: all praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.
