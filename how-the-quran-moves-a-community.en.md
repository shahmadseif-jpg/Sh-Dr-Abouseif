> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Al-Isra Islamic Center on March 30, 2018.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rsrNfwZn58).

Why are we in the mosque? What do we take away from it? What do we do in our shops and homes, when we are busy and when we are free? Where are we going, and what will we leave this world with?

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

Let us spend a few minutes discussing how communities move forward through the Noble Quran. I begin with a question about the reality in which the Muslim community and the societies around it live: can we describe what we see as a new jahiliyyah, a state of ignorance of divine guidance, requiring us to begin the work of repair anew?

### An Ignorance Worse than the First

Compare the ignorance of Mecca when the Prophet ﷺ was sent there with the ignorance people live in today. You will find the ignorance of Mecca's people modest beside what we see now. When Allah spoke of the display of beauty in the first age of ignorance,[^1] that display was modest compared with the flagrant exposure that has entered our lands and homes. We ask Allah to protect our sons, daughters and women, and grant them chastity. Compare the usury practised by Jews when the Prophet ﷺ was sent, and the Quran's strongly worded war against trading in it, with the forms we live with today, devised by Satan in many people's minds. You will find their usury very modest compared with the usury around us.

Perhaps we need to reshape life from the beginning. That is no cause for despair: the effort is ours, while success comes from Allah; the action is ours, while guidance comes from Allah. We do not own people's hearts, but we can strive to reach them. We cannot move people's minds at will, but we can place ideas before them that lead them to Allah's guidance, then to setting out on the right path. This is what we can do.

### The First Revelation: Knowledge

The first words Allah revealed were, in a translation of their meaning: “Read in the name of your Lord who created” [Al-Alaq 96:1]. I ask: why this picture at the beginning? I believe that communities collapse completely when knowledge is absent from people's minds and they live in disorder, like cattle or worse. When ignorance takes hold, and people practise religious rituals day and night as mere acts of worship, empty of feeling and real action, they live like cattle. That is why the first message sent to the Prophet ﷺ was to rise above ignorance and illiteracy, and to be a reader.

What kind of reading is sought? I think—and Allah knows best, and our shaykh may correct us if we are wrong—that it is not simply reading a line of writing. The Prophet ﷺ did not read written lines; rather, he would tell his Companions to write. It is a reading of the universe and everything in it. Whether reading a line or reading the universe, Allah commanded Muslim society to be a learned society so that it could rise to the top. Without knowledge there is no summit, excellence or distinction. If people become ignorant, travelling with the caravan without knowing where they are going, what they are doing or what they hope for, their place will be on the rubbish heap—may Allah honour and protect you.

### The Second Revelation: Character

Once knowledge is sound, we need a second element. Many Quran commentators say that the second revelation was Surat al-Qalam:[^2] “You are indeed of great character” [Al-Qalam 68:4, translation of meaning]. A society with knowledge but no character uses knowledge for vice, spreading indecency, corrupting minds and undermining the foundations of life. Knowledge without character amounts to nothing.

Allah then draws a picture that seems almost unreal to us, so that we can imagine a society in this form. In a translation of the meaning: “They wish you would compromise, so that they would compromise. Do not obey every worthless habitual swearer, a fault-finder who goes about spreading gossip, one who holds back good, an aggressor, a sinner” [Al-Qalam 68:9–12].[^3] These nine qualities describe a hateful society, full of lies, deceit and showing off. A man cannot feel safe about his wife, or about himself in a doctor's care. He cannot trust a teacher's words to his pupil or to his son, the goods he buys, or the information he receives. Nor do people trust me. We fear one another all the time, doubting each other at every stage. It is a frightening society. Yet Allah begins the command with “Do not obey”: keep away from this kind of society and build one unlike it. Truthfulness is our starting point, clarity our means, and reaching our goals directly our way.

### The Third Revelation: Wealth, Then the Inner Self

Once knowledge and then character are complete, people look in Surat al-Qalam at the source of temptation in societies: wealth. Allah tells us about the owners of the garden, who plotted together to go to their garden in the morning and pick its fruit. They moved secretly, like ants passing over the ground, attracting no one's attention. This is where the disease lies. Beware of it, and do not be deceived by it.

But another obstacle stands in the way of those working for Allah. We have spent much time discussing it when we speak about our struggles in dealing with the people around us. People's corrupt character weakens the resolve of those working for the faith. It leaves me constantly wavering: one step I take and another I hold back; one word I speak and another I keep inside; testimony I give and a reservation I maintain. So the person calling others to Allah—and that means each of you—needs a source of confidence that fills the heart with certainty. After this came the revelation, in a translation of its meaning: “You who are wrapped up, stand in prayer through the night, except for a little: half of it, or a little less, or a little more; and recite the Quran with care. We will place upon you a weighty message. Rising in the night is firmer in its effect and more upright in speech. By day, you have much to occupy you” [Al-Muzzammil 73:1–7]. This is where the inner self receives what supports it and makes it firm. The caller can then stand with confidence, speaking the truth openly before people's eyes and ears: a “weighty message” received at night, then carried out by day.

### The Fourth Revelation: Conveying the Message

Once these three come together—knowledge, character, and a stable inner self at peace—you are ready to speak about what you hold and begin spreading your idea. Then comes the call, in a translation of its meaning: “You who are covered up, rise and warn” [Al-Muddaththir 74:1–2]. Now your knowledge is complete. Those who inherited revelation from him ﷺ also inherited the duty of conveying it. His knowledge ﷺ was complete. His character was complete, as his Lord Himself testified. His inner self was complete and at peace. He knew that the way to peace and calm in the heart was prayer, so he would say, in meaning: “Give us rest through it, Bilal.”[^4] Then comes the task of openly declaring the truth, and the role of communication in spreading the word of Islam.

The media are a tool of enormous consequence. They can support and strengthen falsehood, or crush the truth and undermine its foundations. The Prophet ﷺ used this tool alongside the sword and forceful action: skilled speech and noble poets around him formed the communications front that defended Islam. There is also communication within the community, whose voice rises among people: “There is no god but Allah. Come to prayer. Come to success.” They answer the call and come to find the voice of truth spreading Allah's teachings among them.

### The Fifth Revelation: All Praise Belongs to Allah, Lord of All Worlds

When these four foundations are complete—knowledge, character, inner strength and communication—the Muslim, as an individual and a community, can say: all praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds. Here comes the fifth revelation, in a translation of its meaning: “All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgement” [Al-Fatihah 1:2–4]. This was not a passing revelation. It settled at the heart of religious law and worship, until a Muslim could not complete prayer without reciting this declaration, raising it at least seventeen times a day. This feeling means I do not speak only about myself or for myself. I speak on behalf of the community when I say, in meaning: “Guide us to the straight path” [Al-Fatihah 1:6], and “You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help” [Al-Fatihah 1:5]. I am not alone; I stand with my brothers and those around me.

### Conclusion: A Quran Walking upon the Earth

This is what we wanted to set out in reshaping the Muslim way of thinking: how to approach the Quran, how to view it, how to find in its surahs answers to our everyday problems, and how to make it a true guiding light. The greatest and briefest description of the Prophet's ﷺ way of life is that short statement from the person closest to him, his wife: his character was the Quran.[^5] Every action began from a verse; every time he sat, it was with a Quranic scene before him. No verse passed him without his putting a command into practice, stopping at a prohibition, seeking forgiveness in a given situation, asking for a reward, or seeking protection from punishment. The Sunnah is the mirror in which the Quran was reflected when it came down.

Hold firmly to what has been revealed to you and cling to this way. Do not imagine that when you have gathered a hundred thousand, or fifty thousand, or become a millionaire, you have achieved something. It is only a part of what Qarun possessed. Then the command comes in less than a moment. The Quran says, in meaning: “We caused the earth to swallow him and his home. He had no group to help him against Allah, nor could he defend himself” [Al-Qasas 28:81]. I want to be among those who prevail, and this is not the way. The way is to know this Book thoroughly, how to receive guidance from it, and the verses and lessons it contains. We ask Allah to lead us by the hand to what will save us in this world and the Hereafter, keep our steps safe, and ease our affairs. O Allah, we seek refuge in You from worry and grief, helplessness and laziness, cowardice and stinginess, the burden of debt and being overpowered by others.[^6] All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.
