> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hy3lMEAKe7Q).

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. His is all sovereignty and all praise. In His hand are the keys of the heavens and the earth, and He has power over all things. I bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant and Messenger, His chosen one from among His creation and His beloved. He conveyed the message, fulfilled the trust and gave sincere counsel to the community. Through him Allah lifted its distress. May Allah grant him the best reward on our behalf and on behalf of the Muslim community. To continue:

The future is a word many people do not think about. Our main concern is the day we are living and what we have now. If some of us do think about the future, our thoughts are limited to children and money: what to save for tomorrow, what to build in a certain place and what to leave our children. Let us look at a scene from the Prophet's life ﷺ to learn how his Companions shaped their future.

### The Pampered Young Man of Quraysh

One day, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was sitting with his Companions when a young Companion named Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr passed by, wearing a rough, patched garment. The Prophet's eyes filled with tears out of concern for this young man, once known as the pampered youth of Quraysh. He had been so pampered that he could not walk barefoot. People could smell his fragrance from a distance and know that it was Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr, may Allah be pleased with him, walking down the road. Many accounts describe the extent of the material comfort in which he had lived.

Then one day he heard that the Prophet ﷺ was sitting among his Companions in a small house on the outskirts of Mecca, called the house of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam. He went to him, listened and accepted the faith through him. But he faced a great obstacle: his mother would be extremely angry if she learned that he had accepted Islam with Muhammad ibn ‘Abdullah ﷺ. He hid his Islam from her and acted as though his life were unchanged. But one of the idolaters saw him praying and told her, so she cut off the comforts she had provided. This is the first weapon we use to pressure someone who goes against our wishes: taking away the comfort they enjoy. It is a weapon used even against people in positions of power everywhere. They do as they are told for fear of losing their seat. But Mus‘ab, may Allah be pleased with him, refused to give in to her and submitted to what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wanted for him.

A further stage of restriction began: she ordered him to be confined. He became a prisoner after having been the one person free to do whatever he wished, with full authority over his parents' wealth. Now he was in a small room, brought a little food and water, denied the perfumes and bedding he had enjoyed, while the young women of Quraysh had once flocked around him. Then he heard that the Prophet's Companions ﷺ were emigrating to Abyssinia. He wanted to escape his confinement and found ways to get out and join them. On the journey, as the account relates, his feet were seen dripping blood from walking on the sand. Someone gave him leather footwear so he could continue. His feet were soft and unused to hardship, but he went on until he completed his emigration to Abyssinia. He later returned with those who came back, then went to Medina under a direct instruction from the Prophet ﷺ: he was to go with the honoured delegation to Yathrib and teach them Islam.

Mus‘ab's work in calling people to Allah in Medina was remarkable. To this day, we learn from it the arts of speaking and calling others to the faith: how to draw in an opponent, begin a conversation where none existed, win over the person before you and persuade through evidence and reason. It is a whole school of calling people to Allah: the school of Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr. The Prophet ﷺ chose him as the envoy to the people of Medina on behalf of Islam as a whole. He went, and through him Allah opened their hearts, so they entered Islam. Their deeds would add to Mus‘ab's record when he met Allah.

### “What Will You Be Like When You Wear One Outfit in the Morning and Another in the Evening?”

One day he passed before the Prophet ﷺ in patched clothing like this, and the Prophet ﷺ wept at his condition. Perhaps the Companions noticed his tears and were surprised. As the report relates, he ﷺ addressed them: “What will you be like when one of you goes out in one outfit in the morning and another in the evening, when one dish is set before him and another taken away, and when you cover your houses as the Kaaba is covered?”[^1] In other words: what if worldly plenty opens up to us, so that one of you comes to Fajr prayer in a fine shirt and to Isha in different clothes, you sit at tables with many foods and dishes, and your homes have curtains?

The Companions said, “Messenger of Allah, we will be better then than we are today.” Their reasoning was that they would then have time to devote to worship. He ﷺ replied, “By Allah, you are better today than you will be then.” Here we pause over these words to understand what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was showing us when he compared Mus‘ab's two conditions. As the preacher relates, the Prophet ﷺ said of him: I never saw anyone in Mecca with finer hair, more delicate clothing or greater comfort than Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr. That was the wealth and worldly comfort he had enjoyed before Islam. When he entered Islam, nothing could have persuaded him to accept this change except that he had left everything for Allah.

### “Do Not Throw Yourselves into Destruction”

Now let us watch how the Companions dealt with the means of this world and the Hereafter, and how they shaped the future. Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, may Allah be pleased with him, stood at the gates of Constantinople when he was nearly eighty. Its fortifications were so strong that no one could breach them. A soldier burst into the scene and threw himself into the enemy ranks until Allah granted an opening through him. People said, “This man has risked his life, yet Allah has said,” in a translation of the Quran's meaning, “‘Do not throw yourselves into destruction.’” On the surface, their reasoning seemed right. Abu Ayyub said: enough; stop. We are the Prophet's Companions ﷺ, and this verse was revealed about us. One day we Ansar gathered and spoke among ourselves: we had done our duty, given and sacrificed alongside the Prophet ﷺ, until Allah opened the way for Islam and established it on earth. We discussed staying at home to attend to our wealth, circumstances and children. Then Allah revealed about us, in a translation of the meaning: “Spend in Allah's way, and do not throw yourselves into destruction. Do good with excellence; Allah loves those who do good with excellence” [Al-Baqarah 2:195].[^2]

The verse was revealed with a meaning different from what people imagine. It is as though one of us says: I have given and done my part. They ask me to help build the mosque, but I have already given what I have given. Do not ask me again; I have done my duty. Those people had gone out with their lives, wealth, blood and everything they owned, emigrating, fighting and spending. They had done their duty and more. Yet when they said, “Enough; let us rest a little,” the words were revealed about them: “Do not throw yourselves into destruction.” Where, then, is the destruction? It is in sitting back from work and stopping the use of your hands, mind, body and effort for Allah. That is when you destroy yourself. When you go out, work, give and join those taking part, you bring life to yourself, your heart, your body and your mind. Abu Ayyub cited the verse because it was revealed when they wanted to stay behind, not when they wanted to go out. Every step towards what is right and every effort in Allah's way brings this soul to life.

### One of Seven

One simple scene that shows us what the Companions sacrificed comes in a report by Khalid ibn ‘Umayr. He said that ‘Utbah ibn Ghazwan, may Allah be pleased with him, one of the early Muslims, addressed them and said, in meaning: “To continue: this world has announced its departure and is turning away swiftly. Nothing remains of it but a little, like the drops left in a vessel that its owner tries to pour out.” He then said: “I remember being one of seven with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. We had no food but tree leaves, until the insides of our mouths became sore. I found a cloak and split it between myself and Sa‘d ibn Malik. I wrapped half around my waist and Sa‘d wrapped the other half around his. Yet today every one of us has become governor of a city.”[^3]

Seven people around the Prophet ﷺ could find nothing but tree leaves, until their throats dried and their mouths became sore. They had no drink of water to moisten them. What made them patient? A faith that filled their hearts. They chose to feed their hearts with the sweetness of faith and the purity of the Quran, even when their stomachs were hungry. Here was ‘Utbah addressing people as governor of all Basra, reminding them what the earliest believers had given for this faith.

How did they shape this future? They shaped it by sacrificing for it. They shaped it by taking part in political, social and economic life. They shaped it by proving to the whole world that Islam could raise men of real character. They shaped it by knowing they would make a difference in life, leave a mark and teach something good. They shaped it when they understood their own worth through “There is no god but Allah; Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.” They shaped it when each one felt that he should teach his child good character as he taught him a surah of the Quran, leaving behind righteous descendants who would carry “There is no god but Allah” on their shoulders throughout life. They shaped it when they felt that Paradise was near and delighted in its presence as naturally as they breathed the air around them. They shaped the future because they wanted to shape it.

Those who sit back are different. It is enough for them to fill their stomachs three times a day, or more, or less. They are content to consider different coffees, their sweetness and bitterness, select brands of clothing and other things, and become skilled at these choices. This is their only concern. I do not forbid anyone Allah's blessings. But combining the two paths is possible for those who keep a balance between this world and the Hereafter. That is the way of those mindful of Allah.

I say these words and ask Allah to forgive me and you.

### The Second Sermon: Sa‘id ibn ‘Amir as Governor of Homs

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds. The final outcome belongs to those mindful of Him, and hostility is only against wrongdoers. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, the Sovereign, the clear Truth. I bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

One final account concerns those in authority. ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, once appointed Sa‘id ibn ‘Amir governor of Homs. What a land Greater Syria is—a land of plenty! Yet the people of Homs complained about Sa‘id to the caliph. They said: we have four complaints. First, he does not come out to us until after mid-morning. Second, he answers no one at night. Third, he does not come out to us on one day each month. Fourth, from time to time he faints.

‘Umar had chosen one of the people he trusted most to govern Homs. He sat with him and the people, praying that Allah would not let his trust in him prove misplaced. He asked them, and they repeated their complaints. Then he asked Sa‘id to answer. He said: by Allah, I did not want to reveal to people what lies between me and Allah. But, Commander of the Faithful, I have no servant, so I am delayed until I prepare my family's food before going out to them. As for the second complaint, I have given the day to them and the night to Allah. Why do they want to share my portion with my Lord? As for the third, I have only this garment. Once a month I wash it and wait for it to dry. As for the fourth, I was with the unbelievers when they killed Khubayb ibn ‘Adi. They were cutting pieces from his body and asking him, “Would you like Muhammad to be in your place?” He replied, “By Allah, I would not want the Prophet ﷺ to be pricked by a thorn while I was safe with my family and wealth.”[^4]

Such was these people's sacrifice, and such were the qualities of their lives. They wanted the Hereafter, worked for it as it should be worked for and sought the path to it.

### Two Questions for Each of Us

Servants of Allah, we must pause and ask ourselves two questions. First: do we really want to shape the future, or are we content with what others shape for us? Do we truly want to contribute our names, deeds and effort to what we will leave future generations? Or will we leave them to people who invent devices and phones that capture children's minds and use them to promote whatever they wish?

Second: are you ready to help and willing to sacrifice time, effort or money? Each person can answer only for himself. I cannot answer for anyone else. Only Allah knows what lies between you and Him.

O Allah, make up for our failures towards Your religion and the call to it, Lord of all worlds. O Allah, use us in Your service and do not replace us.
