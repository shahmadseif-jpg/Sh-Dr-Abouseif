> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CXgeppKQ9VU).

Demonstrations fill the streets of France. Economic crises shake Europe. Inflation figures announced by the Federal Reserve rise and fall, while people do not know what lies behind them. Look further afield, and you see people who bear witness that there is no god but Allah dying under bombardment in Muslim lands. Look closer to home, and you face the worry of raising children in the West, what our children encounter at school, and campaigns launched against Muslims or claiming to speak for them. Anxiety is natural in such circumstances. We are human.

Yet the Book of Allah contains words that some people use merely to soothe themselves, while those who understand them find a source of strength. In a translation of their meaning: “Whoever relies on Allah, He is sufficient for them. Allah will surely accomplish His purpose. Allah has set a measure for everything” [Al-Talaq 65:3]. These three statements move our whole way of thinking from anxiety toward positive action.

### Three statements that show the way

“He is sufficient for them”: Allah acts on your behalf, managing what is hidden and what is visible, in full control of the situation. “Allah will surely accomplish His purpose”: nothing comes about except by His command, and no one acts within His dominion without His permission. The weak have no power over the strong, the poor over the rich, or those with few resources over those in authority. “Allah has set a measure for everything”: the emergence of a trend, a person, a word, a program, or persecution—all of it is decreed by the Lord of Glory.

The place of this verse in Surat Al-Talaq deserves a pause. The crises men face usually test their minds and bodies. We were created as men to struggle with the world, face its challenges, endure its difficulties, and rise after every fall—not to weep or withdraw. In this surah, feelings become intertwined with ways of thinking. My relationship with work is a matter of figures, but my relationship with my wife is “a solemn covenant.”[^1] When separation happens, it is as though the Lord of all worlds is telling me that trust in Him belongs in the hardest and most difficult times, even for men.

### Trust that does not mean sitting still

If you follow the passages about trust in Allah throughout the Quran, you find its expressions in great abundance. According to the preacher, the term itself appears more than seventy times in different forms. Let us begin at the beginning.

Noah, peace be upon him, says to his people, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “My people, if my presence among you and my reminders of Allah’s signs have become too much for you, then in Allah I have placed my trust” [Yunus 10:71]. Could this have been all he said? His trust did not mean withdrawing from his people. It meant continuous effort over nine hundred and fifty years.[^2] It was as though he took his heart out of his chest and placed it in Allah’s hand, leaving only a body and mind at work. When the heart spoke, it said, “My Lord.” When the hands spoke, they said, “Practical means.”

Hud, peace be upon him, says the same thing and adds another dimension. In a translation of the verse’s meaning: “I have placed my trust in Allah, my Lord and your Lord. There is no living creature but He holds it by its forelock. My Lord is surely on a straight path” [Hud 11:56]. What does “holds it by its forelock” mean? There is not a single person on earth, whether you love them or hate them, appearing on screens or writing tweets, whose forelock is not in Allah’s hand. He holds the forelock of the aggressor and of the person who patiently endures; of the tyrant who invents lies and of the person who makes seeking Allah’s reward a way of life. Every moving creature, including you and me, has its forelock in His hand. Then consider the ending, whose meaning is: “My Lord is surely on a straight path.” We repeat “Guide us to the straight path” at least seventeen times every day. This verse tells us that the straight path is the way Allah has established. Whoever turns away from it does not understand trust in Him.

### Shuayb: trust, then prayer, then effort

Let no one think that saying, “We trust in Allah,” means the matter is settled and they can go to sleep. Here is Shuayb, peace be upon him, saying, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “We would have invented a lie against Allah if we returned to your religion after Allah has saved us from it. We cannot return to it unless Allah, our Lord, wills. Our Lord encompasses everything in knowledge. In Allah we have placed our trust. Our Lord, judge between us and our people in truth, for You are the best of judges” [Al-Araf 7:89].

Notice that the statement did not end with “In Allah we have placed our trust.” It continued with “Our Lord, judge between us.” This is the language of someone who calls people to Allah, draws close to them, enters their gatherings, and offers them good. Trust does not mean saying, “I trust in Allah,” then going out into the world hoping Allah will send me two or three customers. What are we giving? I keep asking myself and others this question: what are we giving for this religion? Elsewhere, Shuayb says, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “I seek only to set things right as far as I can” [Hud 11:88]. This is the language of an active, hardworking leader who believes in his cause and carries out his Lord’s will; someone who knows only the language of work joined to trust in Allah.

### Moses: surrender yourself, rather than merely believe in a cause

When Moses, peace be upon him, speaks of trust in Allah, he puts us in an uncomfortable position. In a translation of the verse’s meaning: “Moses said, ‘My people, if you believe in Allah, then place your trust in Him, if you are Muslims’” [Yunus 10:84]. How often fear, hiding from view, and avoiding confrontation hold us back! “We do not want trouble with anyone. We will keep our heads down and earn a living. We came to America to save a little money and go back.” This is what keeps us from acting.

Notice that he said, “if you are Muslims,” rather than stopping at “believers.” In some contexts, the word Islam expresses commitment to the truth more strongly than the word faith. Many people believe in causes without acting on them. There is a difference between believing in a cause and surrendering yourself to it. Islam requires you to surrender yourself: not to claim an independent choice for yourself over the time, the place, the means, or the action. The call to prayer comes and changes your plans for the day. Choices open before you, and you say: this is lawful, so I accept it; this is forbidden, so I will not go near it; this way of earning money is unacceptable; this is doubtful, so I will avoid it out of caution. That is Islam.

### The messengers, Jacob, and the two men: use the means, then trust in Allah

In Surat Ibrahim, the messengers say to their peoples, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “Why should we not trust in Allah when He has guided us to our paths? We will surely endure patiently the harm you cause us. And in Allah let those who trust place their trust” [Ibrahim 14:12].[^3] All we have done today is gather a few Quranic statements on one subject, and a map of the way ahead appears before you. A true believer is someone who takes charge of their heart and acts within society with strength drawn from Allah.

There came a time when Jacob, peace be upon him, could no longer control any of the means available to him. The matter had been fully surrendered, and he had no way left to change it. Even so, he told his sons, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “Do not enter through one gate; enter through different gates. I cannot protect you at all against Allah. Judgment belongs to Allah alone. In Him I have placed my trust, and in Him let those who trust place their trust” [Yusuf 12:67]. He used the means, then entrusted the matter to Allah.

In a difficult situation, the two men whom Allah had favored said, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “Enter upon them through the gate. Once you enter it, you will be victorious. And place your trust in Allah, if you are believers” [Al-Maidah 5:23]. “Enter”: do not sit at home. In times of crisis, confusion, and a lack of grounding in facts, we have none but Allah. Yet the question remains: what are we giving?

### Where is our place in society?

When will every one of us have a recognized place in this society? We should not think that the most we can do is stand at a ballot box. What is needed is a real presence throughout society, with participation that grows again and again. This may bring further challenges, but it is the path of the prophets and messengers. We will not attain Paradise so easily.

### The Ever-Living who does not die

In a direct command to trust in Him, our Lord says, in a translation of the verse’s meaning: “Place your trust in the Ever-Living who does not die, and glorify Him with praise. He is sufficient as the One fully aware of His servants’ sins” [Al-Furqan 25:58]. The point is that everyone dies: a president, a vice president, a minister of war, a minister of defense, a professor, an adviser, a governor. A person may go to great lengths in tyranny, lies, aggression, and attacks, then die. But I place my trust in the One who does not die.

This is a remarkable verse. Only through faith can you properly connect its parts: “Place your trust,” then “glorify Him with praise,” then “He is sufficient as the One fully aware of His servants’ sins.” The connection between sins, trust in Allah, and using practical means is this: whoever takes care to avoid sin turns their attention to obedience. Whoever turns to obedience seeks the greatest paths to Paradise. There is no greater path than calling people to Allah, with all its difficulties.

There is a similar verse in Hud. In a translation of its meaning: “To Allah belongs the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and to Him every matter is returned. So worship Him and place your trust in Him. Your Lord is not unaware of what you do” [Hud 11:123]. Nothing happens in Allah’s dominion except what He knows. Nothing occurs in His creation except what He wills. We will face nothing except what has been allotted to us. So repent to Allah of feeling afraid. Repent to Allah of relying on something other than His power. Repent to Allah of failing to use the means and take part. Make your presence felt where you are.

### Do not dismiss any means

Throughout his mission as a messenger, our Messenger ﷺ never stopped sharing in the details of people’s lives. Neither events nor attacks held him back. So make your presence felt with whatever means you have. Let me give you a simple example that makes people laugh. Imagine someone saying, “By Allah, my team at work became Muslim because of sweets!” How? Every day I carry a few sweets in my pocket, bring a smile to someone’s face, and utter a word of praise to Allah. People ask, “What is this sweetness? Who are you?” I say, “A Muslim.” Alongside that are good character, prayers offered on time, proper manners, a lowered gaze, and respect for people. Then someone bears witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. I give this example so that we learn not to dismiss any means. Establish your own presence, and Allah will establish it in the hearts of those around you. Put Islam into practice in your character, and Allah will make it influence those around you. Do not dismiss a word you often say: it echoes in the hearts of those around you.

Remember that the call to Islam began with words the Prophet ﷺ spoke from a hill: “Tell me, if I told you that horsemen beyond this valley intended to attack you, would you believe me?” They replied, “We have never known you to lie.”[^4] Then he called them to Islam. Consider the impression you have left in people’s hearts. Have they never known you to lie, betray a trust, act dishonestly, cheat, or falsify anything?

### What is in your hands

We will be asked before the Sovereign, the Giver of all gifts, about the means Allah has placed at our disposal. O Allah, put us to work through the means You have given us in our lives, and let us see good from ourselves before You. O Allah, do not leave us to ourselves, to the promptings of our devils, or to our own desires. Keep us firm through the steadfast word in this life and the Hereafter, and protect Muslim lives in every part of the earth.
