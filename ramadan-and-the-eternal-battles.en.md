> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tIynGoNxo_k).

Listen now to the voices of the students of the Noble Quran. Their voices fill a place that would not have existed without an elderly brother of ours, may Allah shower him with mercy, who helped bring it and its people into being. Our first words today are a renewed prayer for him: may Allah place this centre in the balance of his good deeds.

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

How happy a person is when he leaves behind a lasting means of remembering Allah. How great his loss when worldly life deceives him into storing up things he will leave behind, for others to enjoy while he is called to account for them. Glory be to the One who has distributed provision and understanding among His creatures, giving each person a course and a way to follow. He has distributed feelings among people: one feels close to the Lord of all worlds, while another feels close to Satan. We seek refuge in Allah.

### Half Has Passed and Half Remains

Servants of Allah, half of Ramadan has passed and the second half has begun. What remains will not be like what has gone. Someone who had a plan from the start may have set work to match the time available. The time has now passed, and the work remains: whether it has been done or not, Allah knows best. Each of us must still increase his efforts. Why should we not? These days came as “a limited number of days,” as our Lord called them [al-Baqarah 2:184, meaning], suited to what is required of the month “in which the Quran was sent down” [al-Baqarah 2:185, meaning]. Allah made it a whole month. If something is missing from it, its effect is reduced. If the servant completes his work in it, he fulfils what Allah wants from him.

How do you feel now? Do you find a kind of connection with Allah within yourself? Or has the soul stayed where it was before Ramadan began? Has your mind awakened to the body of truths to which we should turn? Or have we stayed where we were, unchanged? Do not be surprised by this question. Some of us have lived sixty, seventy or fifty years, yet our condition is the same and our habits remain as they began. We do not want to change for the better, develop ourselves or achieve a purpose before the Lord of Might.

### Tufayl ibn Amr: A Man Who Blocked His Ears, Then Listened

I compare people like this with a man who once entered Makkah amid loud warnings from those who presented themselves as protectors of the world at the time, such as Abu Jahl and Abu Lahab. They stopped him at the entrance to Makkah and warned him about the man who divided husbands from wives and parents from children. It reached the point where he put cotton in his ears—*kursuf*, as the account calls it—warning himself most strongly against hearing even one word from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

While he was circling the Kaaba in the manner of the pre-Islamic people, he said to himself: Shame on you, Tufayl! You are a sensible man. You know right from wrong and what is valuable from what is worthless. Why not listen to the man and see what he has to say? If it is right, you can join him. If not, you are clever and intelligent enough to recognise it. He went to Muhammad ﷺ where he was, sat before him, and the Prophet ﷺ began to recite to him. At once, without a moment's delay, he said: I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that you are the Messenger of Allah.

What did he do next? He simply said: Messenger of Allah, sexual immorality has spread among Daws. Send me to them as your messenger, Messenger of Allah. Here was a man whose resolve drove him to prevail and move with lightning speed towards his highest goal. He wanted to reach Paradise as quickly as possible. He did not know whether he would live until tomorrow.

Now look at us. What follows our acceptance of Islam? We sit by the wall waiting for prayer to begin. When it begins, we feel some degree of boredom depending on the length of the recitation or the quality of the delivery and voice. What comes next? If a door to Paradise is offered to us through charity or something else, we think again. We consider our needs and what our wife will say when we go home and tell her: I gave in charity. That would mean an unbearable punishment! For now, I am safe from it. If an opportunity for good is offered to me, I put it off. I place the needs of my body, myself and my children ahead of the needs of the Muslim community and the means it needs to function properly. What comes next? Nothing beyond plenty of food, many desires and talk on all sorts of subjects. Nothing beyond a worldly life that will pass away.

### He Lived to See Ramadan

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ tells us an inspiring example: two men who were close companions. One was killed in battle, and people called him a martyr, with reason and the facts on their side. His companion died a year later. One of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions saw them in a dream, and the man who had died later had reached the gate of Paradise first. He was amazed. He woke up and went to the Prophet ﷺ to ask: Messenger of Allah, we saw the first man die a martyr in battle. Then the second died a year later. How could he enter Paradise before him? The answer was something no one there, or anyone who would come after them, had thought of: Did he not live for another year? The narration says that he reached Ramadan and fasted it.[^1] It is as though living to see Ramadan can equal striving on a battlefield.

Yes, Ramadan has come to reshape the human soul, reform the human mind, and reorder your ambitions, believer who seeks the pleasure of the Lord of Might. Many of us do not think of Ramadan as a ladder by which we rise towards Allah, blessed and exalted is He. That is why the verse about supplication comes among the verses about fasting:

In a translation of the meaning: “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the one who prays when he calls upon Me” [al-Baqarah 2:186].

It is as though these blessed days are among the most fitting settings for an accepted prayer. No times offer greater hope for supplication than these.

### Age-Old Battles

We face age-old battles from beginning to end. Anyone who thinks he has escaped them has ruined his judgement and thinking. Whoever takes them seriously stays constantly awake and fully alert. His resolve does not weaken for a moment, nor does his determination fade for a second. An alert man, a woman of burning resolve—why? Because the hostility is ancient. It has existed since the beginning of creation:

In a translation of the meaning: “Satan whispered to him, saying: Adam, shall I show you the tree of everlasting life and a kingdom that never fades?” [Ta-Ha 20:120].

These symbolise two desires that will remain in the human race until the Day of Resurrection. They hold the strongest grip on people. One is the love of living forever. It takes the form of each sex's attraction to the other, a shared desire that will remain until the Day of Resurrection. Among women, it can lead to displaying their beauty or departing from what is proper and customary; among men, to lust or looking at what is forbidden. The other is the love of possession, with the theft, cheating, forgery, false testimony, dishonest dealings and wrongful taking of people's wealth that accompany it, and all the other crooked paths through which a person imagines he has gained his goal.

That was the beginning. Then the Lord of all worlds shows us another enemy lying in wait, this time deep within you:

In a translation of the meaning: “The soul surely commands evil, except where my Lord shows mercy” [Yusuf 12:53].

To whom does Allah show mercy if not to the person who seeks His mercy and walks towards it, not only with his feet, but with his resolve, mind and feelings? We strive for this and give for it. Otherwise, we are lying, and we seek refuge in Allah from that. Taking steps towards Allah is no easy matter. It means giving what is precious and what is ordinary, and giving our very lives. We see this in scenes from the lives of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions.

Here is a man at the edge of battle, asking himself a very simple question: Is all that stands between me and Paradise the time it takes to eat these dates? Picture the scene with me. The army is preparing to advance soon. Arrows will come from every direction. The man is strengthening his body with a few dates in his hand. No lunch, no roast, no elaborate preparation—only dates stained with dust. Then he says to himself: By Allah, that would be a long life! He throws them away and moves forward.[^2] What drove him to do that? The fuller question is: What holds us back?

As we sit here in this place, we are not facing physical battles in which we carry weapons. Our battles are with our own souls, which prefer worldly life to the Hereafter, bodily pleasure to the joy of the spirit, and the cultivation of outward faculties to the purification of hearts and feelings. Then there are those around us: people with bad manners who draw some of us into responding in kind, against our own moral character. Others stir up indecency around us while we watch. We can either take warning or become immersed in it. We seek refuge in Allah from being far from Him.

### The Quran Teaches Us That Worldly Life Is Only Worldly Life

The Quran is a delight through the ages, a garden for souls, gardens of Paradise recited before you. In it, Allah presents the stories of those who came before and those who come after. He helps us reassess ourselves as we look through its pages, study its verses and take lessons from its words. It is Allah's greatest blessing upon all creation.

It teaches us very simply that worldly life is only worldly life. Anyone who wants to rise must leave it for something higher. If we understand this, we look at the largest house, the greatest company, the most valuable product and the widest stretch of land, at life with all its figures, buildings and savings, as worldly life: something we see by looking down, not up. The Quran teaches us that what will leave this world is not the body you care for day and night, dress as well as you can, clean from time to time, and wish to keep soft and supple. That body is what will remain beneath the soil. There is a soul, a spirit, that will one day rise. It will either go to Allah glowing with bliss, or leave miserable and pale, with no trace of it. The Quran teaches us that the description of Paradise is the greatest vision offered to my eyes before my heart.

### Hanzalah and His Fear of Hypocrisy

In a moment of self-examination, one of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions asks himself: How far into hypocrisy have I fallen? He leaves his house without knowing where to go and meets Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him. He says: I have become a hypocrite, Abu Bakr! He asks: What is wrong, man? He says: When we are with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, he speaks to us about Paradise and the Fire as though we can see them. We live that state and feel it. We enjoy the Paradise he describes and fear the Fire he warns us about. But when we return home, we play with the children, spend time with our wives and attend to our property. He is doing nothing forbidden. Yet he regarded this temporary departure from awareness of the Hereafter as a kind of hypocrisy.

Abu Bakr listens to his words and considers them. He says: By Allah, we all experience that. If this is hypocrisy, we share the same charge. Come, let us go to the guide who never deceives his people. They go to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. The man cannot hold back his words, repeating: Hanzalah has become a hypocrite! Hanzalah has become a hypocrite, Messenger of Allah! The Prophet ﷺ calms his fear: Sit down. Speak. What is wrong? In his answer, he tells him, translated here:

“By the One in whose hand is my soul, if you remained as you are when you are with me and in remembrance, the angels would shake your hands in your beds and along your paths. But, Hanzalah, a time for this and a time for that.”[^3]

The Messenger's ﷺ answer came to give this man greater peace and calm his fear. The state you reach in his company, seeing Paradise and the Fire and feeling that awareness, is precious. We only experience it a little.

I wonder, and I ask myself before anyone else: When was the last time you felt the sweetness of a prostration and stayed there longer because you could hardly bear to leave it, because it was hard to lift your head from the ground? When was the last time you felt so absorbed in glorifying Allah that you saw your soul connect directly with heaven? When was the last time you took such delight in a verse of the Quran that you kept repeating it, like a melody bringing joy to heart and mind, living it and enjoying it? Then we would not find it strange to hear of someone repeating one verse throughout a whole night of prayer. One verse! This is the delight of faith, beloved followers of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

### What Remains of Our Lives

Servants of Allah, in the time we have left, I do not mean Ramadan alone. Ramadan is only part of the time through which we pass and which passes over us. We want truly to identify our purpose in life and draw up a wise plan to cross this world towards the Hereafter. That brother has gone to his Lord, moving from the first home to the final home, while many tongues continue to pray for him. Who among us will stand where he stood? He was only one righteous member of the Muslim community. Others before us went further in good works, their resolve raising them towards what is higher, purer and better. All praise belongs to Allah for what He has given and what He has decreed for us.

Today I have come to you once again, seeking through you support for Allah's cause. We have an institute to prepare callers to the faith and train new imams, here near you in Sachse. The Imams Academy specialises in developing a generation that carries the concern of calling people to Allah, stands in a place like this and speaks to people in this way. That is our goal and this is our project.

O Allah, bring us back to You in a beautiful return. O Allah, protect Muslim lives in every place on earth, and make us firm with the steadfast word in this worldly life and in the Hereafter. O Allah, do not let anything hold us back from obeying You, nor let our resolve fall short of working in Your cause. O Allah, make us a support for Your religion in these times and places.
