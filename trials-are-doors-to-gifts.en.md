> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Astoria Islamic Center on May 17, 2024.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXdddMWz7G0).

Is hardship something that interrupts life, or is it part of what life is made of? Let us first look at ourselves. None of us entered the wide world without first passing through the narrow space of the womb. None of us could eat without effort, or move forward in life without learning a great deal. Only someone who has fallen many times learns to walk with strength.

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance, and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. Whoever Allah guides, no one can lead astray; whoever He leads astray, no one can guide. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. I urge you and myself to be mindful of Allah and hope for what He holds. To continue:

You know that the subject of this sermon is that gifts come through trials. This is a truth throughout the history of Islam; we would also be right to say throughout the history of humanity. We have come to this place in obedience to Allah's words, translated in meaning: “You who believe, when the call to prayer is made on Friday, make your way to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade” [Al-Jumu‘ah 62:9]. For its sake, we have left our other affairs behind.

### Hardship Is a Pattern Everyone Experiences

Looking at the community of humanity from the beginning of creation, you see Adam, peace be upon him. Allah created him and settled him in His Garden, a place like which everyone wishes to live. He speaks with the angels and they speak with him, and his Lord teaches him all the names. Amid this happiness, the features of life change. Suddenly Iblis emerges from where you do not expect to oppose this human being. Yesterday he was beside me; today he stands against me. Life passes, and you see two brothers born of one womb, eating the same food and raised by the same hand. Then one becomes his brother's enemy and kills him. This is the pattern of life in its broad course.

Then you stand before Noah, peace be upon him: for nine hundred and fifty years he tries to convince his people, while they try to break his strength and destroy his abilities. You move on to Abraham, peace be upon him, whose people became so hostile to him that they threw him into the fire. You pass through the succession of prophets and messengers: Yusuf, peace be upon him, and the woman who tried to seduce him and became the cause of his imprisonment. Trials come to people without exception.

Moses, peace be upon him, grew up in the king's house. Someone like him belonged to the most privileged of the privileged. No one would stand in his way or refuse his request. Then, in a moment, the Quran tells us, in a translation of its meaning: “A man came running from the farthest part of the city. He said, ‘Moses, the leading men are plotting to kill you. Leave! I am giving you sincere advice’” [Al-Qasas 28:20]. “So he left it in fear, watching warily” [Al-Qasas 28:21, meaning]. Yesterday's son of the palace leaves afraid, looking around him. Why does the world turn in this way, and why do events change so greatly? Then there is Jesus, peace be upon him, calling people to Allah and reminding them of the earlier Book of Moses. They plotted against him and nearly crucified him, had Allah not raised him to Himself.

### The Trials of the Prophet ﷺ and His Companions

As time moves on, you see the Prophet ﷺ from the very beginning. One of the disbelievers says to him, translated here: “May you be ruined all day! Is this what you gathered us for?”[^1] He leaves one crisis only to enter another. He passes through one stage only to face challenge after challenge. You see him among his people in the valley of Abu Talib, under such a harsh blockade that they could find nothing to eat. Glory be to the All-Knowing Creator, who gives people strength and inspires them with patience and the resolve to seek His reward.

I remember the account of Khabbab, may Allah be pleased with him, when he came to the Prophet ﷺ as he sat near the Kaaba. He said, “Messenger of Allah, do you not see what has happened to us? Do you not see our condition?” He uncovered his back, hurt by their lashes and severe torture. He asked, “Will you not seek help for us? Will you not pray to Allah?”[^2] Here we realize that all Khabbab asked for was a prayer. He did not ask the Prophet ﷺ to fight or take up a sword. It would have been easy for him ﷺ to offer that prayer at the time. But Khabbab was being taught, and not Khabbab alone: the community after him needs to learn this lesson. When hardships descend, they call for action, not merely words. The Prophet ﷺ gives Khabbab an example, translated here: “A man among those before you would be combed with iron combs ... that would not turn him away from his faith ... but you are in a hurry.”

May Allah bless His Prophet and be pleased with his Companions. How much they suffered to uphold the truth and establish Allah's word! How much effort they gave so that this Islam would reach us, which we now enjoy, yet often rebel against and fail to practice even in its simplest forms.

### Life Is Hardship and Toil

Are trials part of life, or an interruption of it? A simple understanding of Allah's words makes this clear. In a translation of their meaning: “We have indeed created the human being in hardship” [Al-Balad 90:4]. This hardship is constant suffering, without a break or rest from it. You leave one matter for another, then ask: until when? Look at people's lives. A person wants to grow up; once grown, he wants to graduate; once graduated, he wants to marry, then work, then have children, then see them grow up, then get them married. He says, “O Allah, how long must I carry this concern?” His Lord answers, in a translation of the verse's meaning: “Human being, you are toiling toward your Lord with great effort, and you will meet Him” [Al-Inshiqaq 84:6].

Look at what the wording conveys. Had it said “then you will meet Him,” the Arabic word thumma, “then,” would indicate a delay, giving us a period of rest. But Allah said fa-mulaqih, “and you will meet Him,” using fa, which indicates sequence and an immediate following. So you keep running until you fall. In life as a whole, there is no moment called the moment of rest. Allah therefore showed kindness to the believers by making remembrance of Him their place of rest. In a translation of the Quran's meaning: “Surely it is through the remembrance of Allah that hearts find peace” [Al-Ra‘d 13:28].

### The Goal of Those Who Understand This World

When you look around and see these hardships passing over the community—hardships that I do not call disasters—each stage is a passage to the one after it. I recall an incident involving one of the Prophet's Companions ﷺ, if the account is sound. He said, “Messenger of Allah, we have reached the sky in honor and praise, yet we seek a station higher still.” He ﷺ asked, “Where?” He replied, “Paradise, Messenger of Allah.”[^3] That is the goal of those who know Allah and understand the reality of this world, and who want to leave a lasting mark behind them. They measured this world's length and breadth and found it worth zero, unless you leave behind a tongue that remembers your Lord and fills the world with more worship of the Creator of the heavens and the earth. Life is worth zero unless you leave it with complete salvation, your name recorded among those saved for Paradise, not for any other destination.

We say a great deal, repeat slogans, and raise our voices with words. Few put them into practice, and fewer still keep doing so. Be servants of Allah: when afflictions clear away, those who stand are the ones made strong by their Lord. One of the hardest times in the Prophet's life ﷺ was the day of Badr. He was among his people when Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, came and said, “Messenger of Allah, sit in the shelter and pray to Allah for us.”[^4] The work divides into two parts: people there strike with their hands, while a tongue here turns constantly toward heaven in prayer and appeals for help: O Lord, all for Your servants. Which kind of work are you doing?

### A Question for the People of These Lands

People of New York, which has become the world's center of wealth, what are you doing here? What are we doing in America? What do we want from these lands? What good or benefit are we bringing about here? What are we leaving behind for our children? How much money people have gathered and spent! How many buildings we have acquired and built, then left behind us! A person does not know whether he will draw another breath, live to see the night, or see another morning. What kind of worldly concerns fill these heads? What is drifting through these minds? There is work before us. There is work before us.

A saying is attributed to the Prophet ﷺ in his home, on his bed. It spread and became a slogan for those working in Allah's cause, not only in these lands but everywhere: “The time for sleep has passed, Khadijah.”[^5] People are being killed and slaughtered here and there, struck from above and below. You see the people of Palestine. Muslims suffer in every land, and no place escapes hardship, whether visible or hidden. So what are we doing? By Allah, besides whom there is no god, we are answerable before Him for every breath that leaves our mouths, every word we speak, and every moment that passes us. O Lord, grant us salvation.

### Closing Supplication

O Allah, bring us to our goal in this place and grant us the ability to achieve it, by Your permission and generosity. O Allah, grant victory to Your servants in Palestine and steadfastness to Your servants on earth, Most Merciful of those who show mercy. O Allah, make what You have permitted enough for us so that we have no need of what You have forbidden, and make Your favor enough for us so that we have no need of anyone besides You. O Allah, inspire us to sound judgment, ease our affairs, and fulfill our needs. All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.
