> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at the Imams Academy on May 13, 2022.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-6ZmeWqXzA).
> Editor's note: This material summarizes what could be established from the lecture; it is not a word-for-word transcript. No explanation absent from the lecture has been added.

What did you eat yesterday? If you remember, where is it today? Think back a week: what did you eat, and where is that food now? Then think back a year, five years and ten. We have eaten an enormous amount of food, and it has all gone.

Through this simple question, the preacher reflects on a statement repeated in two places in the Quran. In a translation of the meaning: “The lasting good deeds are better with your Lord in reward and better as a source of hope” [Al-Kahf 18:46], and “The lasting good deeds are better with your Lord in reward and better in their final return” [Maryam 19:76]. He draws particular attention to the setting in Al-Kahf: Allah tells the story of the owner of two gardens, follows it with a comparison for worldly life, then says, in a translation of the meaning: “Wealth and children are the adornment of worldly life” [Al-Kahf 18:46].

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant, Prophet and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

### A Man Who Thought Worldly Life Would Never Pass Away

A reader reflecting on the setting understands that Allah has shown us a man who treated this life as everything. He saved for it, loved it and lived for it alone. He even imagined that it would never slip from his hands. In a translation of the Quran's meaning, he said: “I do not think this will ever perish, nor do I think the Hour will come” [Al-Kahf 18:35–36]. Many of us think the same way today when we live with such passion for worldly life. Then Allah shows us how the world that looked green and flourishing to its owner became a ruin beneath his feet: “bare, slippery ground” [Al-Kahf 18:40, meaning], with nothing of it left to be seen.

Allah then gives us a comparison to wake us up. In a translation of the meaning: “Give them the example of worldly life: it is like water We send down from the sky, with which the plants of the earth mingle, then they become dry fragments scattered by the wind. Allah has full power over everything” [Al-Kahf 18:45].

### Water Sent Down from the Sky

The preacher sees this water as a very simple comparison for the source of the provision you have, whether you are a doctor, a university professor, a teacher or an astronomer. Each has a source like the water Allah sent down, which you did not make yourself. “With which the plants of the earth mingle”: with it come the food and drink at home, spending on your wife and children, the needs of schools and students, what you wear, eat and sleep on—everything. Then “they become dry fragments.”

He does not mean that this only happens through death or the complete loss of a blessing. Go back to the question about food: everything we ate has passed away. Yet a mouthful you ate that gave your body strength to obey Allah became one of the lasting good deeds. Money you gave your child to develop a skill or help him gain a good habit became lasting good deeds. We began by looking at food and how it disappeared, then considered the effect it left behind.

Look at the record of your spending over your lifetime: house payments, food and drink, electricity, water and gas. Add up ten years and see how much you spent: hundreds of thousands. Do you know why we complain of poverty? Because we look only at the moment we are living in. If you reviewed what you had spent on travel, gifts, food and the clothes piled up around us, you would see that all of this is wealth. But is it among the lasting good deeds, or is it not? This is the decisive question, for Muslims and others alike.

### How Did the Prophet ﷺ Explain Them?

Among the reports the preacher relates from the Prophet ﷺ to explain the lasting good deeds is this saying, translated in meaning: “Take up your protection.” They asked, “Messenger of Allah, against an enemy who has arrived?” He replied, “No; take up your protection against the Fire.” They asked, “What is our protection against the Fire?” He said, “Glory be to Allah; all praise belongs to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is greatest. These will come on the Day of Resurrection to save you and follow behind you. They are the lasting good deeds.”[^1]

His words ﷺ suggest that the lasting good deeds are these four phrases. How, then, do we reconcile this with saying that a mouthful of food may or may not be among the lasting good deeds? The preacher answers very simply: when a servant glorifies his Lord, he honours Him above every desire in life. All his spending becomes for the sake of the One he glorifies, and his whole life turns into lasting good deeds. When a servant praises his Lord, he is content with what he has. He does not take unfair advantage of opportunities or of other people's resources, and he is not greedy. His wants are modest: little is enough for him, while he does much, and the world benefits widely from his presence. When he declares Allah's greatness, he feels that he belongs to the supreme power, with no other power like it. He becomes stronger than anyone in this world, bold in facing difficulties and overcoming hardship, because he depends on Allah, the Greatest. If a calamity strikes him or an obstacle stops him, he turns to “There is no power and no strength except through Allah.” When he acted, he did not act by his own power, but through Allah's support.

### Remembrance That Leaves No Effect

The lasting good deeds, the preacher says, do not mean repeating words of remembrance alone and then doing nothing more. He does not like this at all: a man sits from dawn until mid-morning reciting and listening to the Quran, then returns home to insult and curse his wife and mistreat her. What have those words of remembrance produced within you? Have you planted them in salty ground that does not respond, or in hard ground that resists cultivation and grows nothing? Imagine a man standing in the prayer row, weeping deeply because the verses move him. Then you see him treating people terribly in matters of money, breaking his appointments without keeping a single one, or failing to give people even a kind word. What lasting good deeds are these? What remembrance has made a difference in his life?

Every form of remembrance taught by the Prophet ﷺ has a great effect on a person's life through the order of its words, their meaning and the choice of expressions. A single remembrance can completely change your character, as though you were a different person—provided that you understand what the words mean and are open to being changed by them. The lasting good deeds are words, but those words must produce an effect.

### Every Goal Has a Price

People's goals differ according to their work and temperament. Every goal has a price and every aim has a path. Reaching a goal takes effort. Whoever makes Paradise his goal must work for it; otherwise, he is lying to himself and to Allah.

Here is a simple example of someone leaving a real effect behind. A university professor sees a gifted student sitting by a wall, troubled. He greets him, but the student does not answer, so he comes closer and pats his shoulder. The student apologizes for not noticing him and explains that money problems and university expenses are weighing on him. The professor tells him not to worry, speaks to student affairs and supports him for four or five years. Time passes, and the student graduates as his professor had hoped. He then becomes a professor at the same university. When the first professor's son studies there, he readily helps him with what he needs. The first professor left lasting good deeds by leaving his son something like the good he had done in his own life. So too with someone who supports an orphan until he becomes a strong man able to contribute, or someone who teaches a student of knowledge. There are many examples we all know.

### Wealth and Children Are an Adornment

The preacher reflects on the verse, in a translation of its meaning: “Wealth and children are the adornment of worldly life.” The Arabic word for wealth appears in the singular because, however much there is, it seems little. Someone with a hundred thousand hopes for two hundred thousand; when he reaches a million, the thought of a billion buzzes in his head like a bee. He is never satisfied. He has a certain sum in the bank and a card in his pocket, yet feels nothing. “Children” appears in the plural because the happiness they bring is greater and the sense of strength through having them is more powerful. You see a very poor man always taking pride in his children, while a very rich man with no children often hangs his head: he has no one to share the burden of this great inheritance with him.

If you ask, “How can they be an adornment when they support me in my present life?” look at what your child does when you are in pain. At most, he says, “Get well, Father. May you be free of harm; I ask Allah to heal you,” or brings you a doctor. What can the doctor do? He treats you with painkillers, yet the pain remains, and no one shares it with you at all. Your son has provided every financial and medical means, but the pain remains within you as something brought about by the Lord of all worlds. Then you raised your son and built up a great human being, but a good woman came along and took him to another country or to work somewhere else. Most of those present—I do not want to give a percentage—are far from their families. We left them behind and set out across the earth: some of us for Allah, some for worldly life, and some to escape something.

Wealth and children, then, are an adornment, like ornaments attached to clothing. If removed, they do not change the fabric itself; they complete its outward appearance. What remains is what you yourself have: your inner structure. If we are to have lasting good deeds, let them be found in righteous action.

### Those Who Benefit People Most

In the second sermon, the preacher relates a hadith from Ibn ‘Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father. A man came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and asked, “Which people are dearest to Allah?” The preacher first pauses over this questioner's high ambition. He wants to rise to the deeds dearest to Allah; his eyes are fixed above what draws other people's eyes. So too, the Companions looked directly towards heaven. No limit in their bodies, hearts or minds stood in their way. They swept across the world because their eyes were on heaven.

The Prophet ﷺ replied, in a translation of the meaning: “The people dearest to Allah are those who benefit people most.”[^2] The preacher notes that in some versions the question concerns deeds, yet the Prophet ﷺ speaks to him about people. It is as though he says: before you do a deed, you must build yourself and first change yourself so that you become ready to act. He compares it to a device whose port will not work with another device until we provide an adapter. Before a good deed can come from the Companion, the connection within him needs some adjustment.

Then he ﷺ said, “Those who benefit people most,” not “those who take most from people.” If your outlook is directed towards heaven, give what you have for the Lord of all worlds. Let your goodness reach those around you, and Allah's goodness will reach you. Your Lord gives in proportion to what you give His servants around you. A wise father loves most the child who loves his siblings most. Yusuf was dearest to his father, while the others resented this. The father felt pain at a selfish child who wanted to get as much as possible: he takes his allowance first, runs to snatch the biggest thing and picks out the sweets, treating his siblings as though they lived in a jungle. Imagine, then, what the Lord of all worlds does for the people dearest to Him: those who benefit His creation most.

### Conclusion: Leave Something Behind

O Allah, benefit us through what You have taught us and teach us what benefits us. O Allah, guide us to goodness and where it is found, and make us among the first to reach it. O Allah, we seek refuge in You from losing what You have given us; do not deprive us of Your gifts. O Allah, do not disgrace us or expose us, in this world or the Hereafter.

In a word after the prayer, he said: take care not to die without leaving something behind.
