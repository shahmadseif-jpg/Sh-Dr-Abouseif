What did you eat yesterday? If you remember, where is it today? Take your memory back a week: what did you eat, and where is that food? Then to a year, and five years, and ten. We have eaten an enormous quantity of food and then all of it went.

With this simple question the preacher reflects on a verse that the Qur'an repeats in two places: ﴿وَالْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ أَمَلًا﴾ [الكهف: 46] (and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life. But the enduring good deeds are better to your Lord for reward and better for [one's] hope), and ﴿وَالْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا﴾ [مريم: 76] (And the enduring good deeds are better to your Lord for reward and better for recourse). And in the context of al-Kahf in particular, as he points out, Allah cited the story of the owner of the two gardens, then followed it with the likeness of the life of this world, then said: ﴿الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا﴾ [الكهف: 46] (Wealth and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life).

Praise be to Allah, we praise Him, seek His help, seek His guidance and seek His forgiveness, and we bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant, His prophet and His messenger ﷺ. To proceed:

### A man who thought the world would never end

The one who reflects understands from the context that Allah showed us a model of a man who dealt with life as though it were everything; he saved for it, loved it passionately, lived for it alone, and even imagined that it would never pass away from his hands: ﴿مَا أَظُنُّ أَن تَبِيدَ هَٰذِهِ أَبَدًا ۝ وَمَا أَظُنُّ السَّاعَةَ قَائِمَةً﴾ [الكهف: 35-36] (I do not think that this will perish - ever. And I do not think the Hour will occur). And so many of us imagine today when they live their worldly lives with this passion. Then Allah shows us how the world that had turned green and flourished in the eyes of its owner became ruined under his feet, ﴿صَعِيدًا زَلَقًا﴾ [الكهف: 40] (barren ground), with no trace in it and no eye remaining.

Then Allah gave us the likeness so that we may awaken: ﴿وَاضْرِبْ لَهُم مَّثَلَ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا كَمَاءٍ أَنزَلْنَاهُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ فَاخْتَلَطَ بِهِ نَبَاتُ الْأَرْضِ فَأَصْبَحَ هَشِيمًا تَذْرُوهُ الرِّيَاحُ ۗ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مُّقْتَدِرًا﴾ [الكهف: 45] (And present to them the example of the life of this world, [its being] like rain which We send down from the sky, and the vegetation of the earth mingles with it and [then] it becomes dry remnants, scattered by the winds. And Allah is ever, over all things, Perfect in Ability).

### Water that came down from the sky

The preacher sees in this water a very simplified likeness of the source of provision in your hands, whether you are a physician, a university professor, a teacher or an astronomer; for each there is a source that represents the water that Allah sent down and that you did not make. ﴿فَاخْتَلَطَ بِهِ نَبَاتُ الْأَرْضِ﴾ (and the vegetation of the earth mingles with it): the foods and drinks in the house mingled with it, and the expenses of the wife and the dependents, and the requirements of schools and students, and what you wear and what you eat and what you sleep on, everything. Then ﴿فَأَصْبَحَ هَشِيمًا﴾ (and then becomes dry stubble).

He does not want to say that this is by death or by the complete departure of the blessing. But go back to the question of food: everything we ate has passed. Yet the morsel you ate and which bore fruit in your body as obedience became one of the enduring good deeds. And the coin you gave to your son to develop in him a skill or to give him a good habit, became enduring good deeds; because we measured by food and saw how it vanished, then we looked at what remained of it as a trace.

And look at the record of your expenditures throughout life: the payments for the house, the food and drink, the electricity, water and gas. Add up your accounts for ten years and see how much you spent, hundreds of thousands. Do you know why we complain of poverty? Because we only look at the moment we are living. If you reviewed what you spent on travel, gifts, food, and the clothes piled up with us like heaps, you would see that all of that is wealth. But is it among the enduring good deeds or not among them? This is the decisive point for the Muslim and for others.

### How did the Prophet ﷺ explain them?

Among what the preacher narrates in the explanation of the enduring good deeds from the Prophet ﷺ: «خُذُوا جُنَّتَكُمْ» (Take your shield). They said: O Messenger of Allah, from an enemy who has come? He said: «لَا، وَلَكِنْ خُذُوا جُنَّتَكُمْ مِنَ النَّارِ» (No, but take your shield from the Fire). They said: And what is our shield from the Fire? He said: «سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ؛ فَإِنَّهُنَّ يَأْتِينَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ مُنَجِّيَاتٍ وَمُعَقِّبَاتٍ، وَهُنَّ الْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ» (Glory be to Allah, praise be to Allah, there is no god but Allah, and Allah is the Greatest; for they will come on the Day of Resurrection as rescuers and followers, and they are the enduring good deeds).[^1]

It is understood from his words ﷺ that the enduring good deeds are these four phrases. So how do we reconcile this with the fact that a morsel may be among the enduring good deeds and may not be? The preacher answers with utmost simplicity: when the servant glorifies his Lord, he magnifies Him above every aim he has in life, so every expenditure of his becomes for the sake of the One he glorifies, and life as a whole turns into enduring good deeds. And when the servant praises his Lord, he is content with what is in his hands, so he does not seize opportunities, does not exploit the capacities of people and is not greedy, but is modest in his demands, the little suffices him and he does much, so the world is made better by his existence. And when he declares Allah greatest, he senses that he belongs to the supreme power than which there is no power like it, so he is in the world stronger than anyone, daring in the face of difficulties and breaking through hardships, because he leans on Allah, the Greatest. And if a calamity strikes him or an obstacle stops him, "there is no power and no strength except with Allah" comes, because when he moved he did not move by himself, but by Allah's support of him.

### Remembrance without effect

The enduring good deeds, the preacher says, are not repeating remembrance in seclusion and then nothing after that. He does not like this at all: that a man sits from his dawn to the forenoon reading and listening to the Qur'an, and then when he returns home he insults his wife, curses her and wrongs her. What have the words of remembrance produced in yourself? Did you put the remembrance in saline ground that does not respond, or in hard ground that resists the seed and brings forth no plant? Imagine a man who stands in the row and weeps intensely from being moved by the verses, then you see him dealing with people in dinar and dirham in the worst way possible, or breaking his appointments and not being truthful in one of them, or not repaying people with a kind word. What enduring good deeds are these? And what remembrance has affected his life?

There is no remembrance that the Prophet ﷺ specified except that in the order of its words, its import and the choice of its expressions is a great effect on a person's life. A single remembrance is able to change the features of your personality completely, as if you were another creation, provided that you take in the meaning of the words and have a readiness to be affected. For the enduring good deeds are words, but they must turn into effect.

### Every goal has a price

Goals differ among people according to their crafts and temperaments, and every goal has a price and every aim has a road. And reaching the aim needs effort. Whoever wants Paradise, and it is his aim, must spend for it, otherwise he is lying to himself and lying to Allah.

And here is a simple example of one who leaves behind a real trace: a university professor sees a gifted student sitting beside the wall, worried, so he greets him and he does not reply, so he approaches him and pats his shoulder. The student apologizes that he did not notice, and says that his financial situation and the demands of the university weigh on him. The professor says to him: do not carry worry, then he speaks to the student affairs administration and takes responsibility for this boy for four years or five. The days pass and the student graduates as his professor expected of him, then becomes a professor in the same university, and the son of that first professor is studying there and finds no worry in having his affairs made easy. He left enduring good deeds when he left for his son the like of what he had done in his life. And likewise one who took responsibility for an orphan until he became a strong man capable of producing, and one who taught a student of knowledge. There are many examples known to everyone.

### Wealth and children are adornment

The preacher reflects on the verse: ﴿الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا﴾ (Wealth and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life). "Wealth" is mentioned in the singular because however much there is, it is little; whoever has a hundred thousand aspires to two hundred, and when he reaches the million the billion rings in his head like a bee, and he is not satisfied. An account in the bank of such-and-such an amount and a card in the pocket and he feels nothing. And "children" is in the plural because the joy in them is happier, and the sense of dignity from having them is stronger; you see a very poor man always boasting of his children, and you see a very rich man with no children bowing his head in most of his states, having no one to carry this great inheritance with him.

If you say: how can they be adornment when they are my support in my present days? Then look at what your son does when you are in pain. The most he does is to say: may you be well, Father, no harm to you, I ask Allah to heal you, or he brings you a doctor. And what can the doctor do? He treats with painkillers, and the pain remains, which no one shares with you at all. The son spent for you all the material and medical means, and the pain remained in you, a work of the Lord of the worlds. Then you raised your son and built a great edifice of human beings, and the right girl came and took him to another country or to a job in another place. And most of those present, I do not want to count a percentage, are far from their families, we left them behind our backs and set out in the earth, some of us striving for Allah and some striving for the world and some striving in flight from something.

So wealth and children are adornment, like an ornament fastened on a garment; if it is removed it does not affect the garment itself, it is only an adornment that completes the overall appearance. And what remains is what is with you, your edifice from within. So if we have enduring good deeds, let them be in righteous action.

### The most beneficial of people to people

In the second sermon the preacher narrates the ḥadīth of Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with them both) that a man came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and asked him: which people are most beloved to Allah? And he first stops at the loftiness of this questioner's ambition, who wants to rise to the deeds most beloved to Allah, his eye above where the eyes of the rest of creation fall. And so was the Companions' gaze at the sky directly, so no limit stood before them in their bodies or their hearts or their minds, and they swept the world because they were fixing their eyes on the sky.

The Prophet ﷺ said: «أَحَبُّ النَّاسِ إِلَى اللَّهِ أَنْفَعُهُمْ لِلنَّاسِ» (The most beloved of people to Allah are the most beneficial of them to people).[^2] The preacher notes that the question in some narrations is about deeds, and yet the Prophet ﷺ speaks to him about people, as though he were saying to him: before you do a deed you must make yourself, and change yourself first so that you are able to act. He gives an example of this with a device whose port does not work with another device until we bring an adapter; so before a righteous deed issues from the Companion, the connector inside needs some adjustment.

Then he ﷺ said: "the most beneficial to people," and did not say: the one who takes most from people. So if your considerations are toward the sky, then spend what is in your hands for the Lord of the worlds, and let your good reach those around you, and Allah's good will be upon you, for your Lord gives according to what you give to the servants around you. And the wise father loves most among his children the one with the most love for his brothers. Yūsuf was more beloved to his father, and the others were resentful, and in the father's heart was pain over an opportunistic son who wants to get the greatest amount, takes the allowance first and runs to snatch the big thing and picks out the sweets, treating his brothers as if in a jungle. So imagine what the Lord of the worlds does with the one most beloved to Him, who is the most beneficial of people to creation.

### Conclusion: leave something behind you

O Allah, benefit us with what You have taught us and teach us what benefits us, O Allah, guide us to good and to its places and make us among those who race to it, O Allah, we seek refuge in You from removal after giving, and do not deprive us of what You have granted us, O Allah, do not disgrace us and do not expose us, neither in this world nor in the Hereafter.

And he said in a word after the prayer: be careful not to die until you have left something behind you.
