> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered at Coldwater Mosque, Michigan, on July 12, 2024.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W7XFzQNf0D8).

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance, and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. I bear witness that our master and Prophet Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. To continue:

Congratulations on this blessed building. I ask Allah to place it in the scales of your good deeds. Whenever I come to this mosque, I see the future of Islam in the eyes of its young people.

A speaker in these days should not pass over the Prophet's Hijra ﷺ, his migration. It was a turning point in the whole life of Islam: things were one way before it and another way afterward. Yet it was only one stage in the Prophet's work for this faith ﷺ. I say it again: a stage of the work, not its end and not a rest from it. Is there anyone among you who wants to follow the same plan that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ followed?

### A Life Without a Day Off

Throughout his life, the Prophet ﷺ never had a single day devoted entirely to rest. His life was never free of problems, and he never slept for a night without carrying the concern of Islam and its call. Not a moment passed without his thinking about how to deliver this great message.

Anyone who wants to follow his steps should tell his Lord from the beginning that he belongs to Allah, with no share of himself reserved for his own sake or his family's. His family and relatives are among the people he calls to Allah. He works to keep them firm upon the truth, guide them, and take their hands to the shore of safety. Anyone whose concern is this will not argue with his wife over a clean or dirty cup. He will not occupy his mind with a spider on the wall or be held up by the trivial concerns of ordinary people who have no purpose or goal.

I remember my boyhood. We would long to leave the study table and play in the street. My father would tell me, “You have a mission. You should not turn away from your book. Your future is not like theirs. If you join their games today, you will join their loss of purpose tomorrow. So stand firm; may Allah make you firm.” Days turned and years passed. We found ourselves taking on positions of responsibility and leading people firmly toward guidance, by the permission of the Lord of all worlds, while others had no concern beyond what appeared on television screens.

Some of our friends ask me, “When do you sleep?” My answer, though it carries a joke, is, “In the grave.” No one will come there to knock on the door and wake me. We will have had a very long time for sleep before then. We are not here to sleep. We are not here to sleep. We are not here to sleep—much less to disagree over trivial things and quarrel about matters that have no basis and no effect.

### Like the Prophet's Companions ﷺ at the House of al-Arqam

So bring yourselves together. Be like the Prophet's Companions ﷺ who gathered around him at the house of al-Arqam. What were they like? They would make their way there quietly until they reached the place where guidance was heard, and their hearts listened closely to the voice of prophethood. By night or day, they had no concern but Islam. They thought only of calling others to the faith, and their precautions were for the safety of the one conveying that call ﷺ.

Then time passes. I visited this place years ago. Today I see new faces and children who have become young adults, as Allah has willed. The days turn, time ends, health passes, and the mark we leave remains. What have you put forward for yourself before Allah? The question on that Day will not be only about the effort you made, but about the blessing you were given. If someone was a gifted speaker, his Lord will call him to account: why did you not give thanks for that gift? If someone had wealth and was asked to give but withheld it, his Lord will ask: why did you not offer what I gave you? What you hold does not come from you. Our Lord has told us that He has entrusted us with the wealth in our hands.

### Migration Was Not the First Solution

The Prophet's migration ﷺ from Mecca to Medina was not the first solution. Someone might say: these people do not listen to us, so let us simply go somewhere else. But he ﷺ did so only when it was the last solution. It was not to save himself, his body, or his comfort, but to preserve the faith.

The first thing required of us, if we are to carry out the task as the Prophet and his Companions did, is a sense of responsibility. Do you truly feel responsible for Islam? By Allah, that is a difficult question. Some among us do not even feel responsible for their homes. A man's wife cries and his children are hungry while he does not care, spending his money on what is forbidden or whatever he desires. We need men who are honest with themselves. What does the word “man” mean? Manhood is not dressing up at night. It is working by day and night, giving what I can, growing tired and worn out. For what? For what lasts forever, or for what passes and ends? What remains for us, servants of Allah, is the Hereafter, Paradise, and meeting the Lord of all worlds. It is not the phone screens on which we waste so much time, or the television and the films and series we sit watching. What remains is a page of the Quran, read not merely to fill time or seek the blessing of the moment, but to learn how to act upon it and apply its teachings in our lives.

After feeling responsible for this faith, we must care about bringing our children together in Allah's house, to learn His word and receive the counsel of His Messenger ﷺ.

This sense of responsibility was one of the most important qualities of the Prophet's Companions ﷺ. The Prophet himself ﷺ carried such deep concern that his Lord addressed him with these words, translated in meaning: “Perhaps you will grieve yourself to death over them if they do not believe in this message” [Al-Kahf 18:6]. Have we read this verse as we recite Surat al-Kahf each week? Imagine the Prophet ﷺ so deeply troubled by people's refusal to respond that he nearly destroys himself. Gabriel then comes down, and the Lord of all worlds reassures him and eases his burden. Yet one of us wakes with no concern beyond the kinds of food on the table, when he will reach work, whom he will meet, and with whom he will spend his time. These questions are empty of substance, for this is a life that passes and a lifetime that ends.

### Khabbab and the Prophet's Answer ﷺ

The Prophet ﷺ trained his Companions in this sense of responsibility and in giving for this faith. One day Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, may Allah be pleased with him, came while the disbelievers were torturing them severely. The Prophet ﷺ was beside the Kaaba, resting his head on his cloak. Khabbab uncovered his back, showing the marks of the lashes, and said, “Messenger of Allah, do you not see what has happened to us? Will you not pray to Allah for us?” What did Khabbab ask for? Only a prayer. Yet, as the preacher relates the account, the Prophet ﷺ became angry and said, in meaning, “Khabbab, a man among those before you would be combed with iron combs from the crown of his head to the soles of his feet, and cut in two, yet that would not turn him away from his faith.”[^1]

We might ask: why was Khabbab met with such a stern tone? Because he would go back to his Companions and tell them the Prophet's answer. It would strengthen the men's resolve and renew their determination after a loss of drive, or after thoughts of finding a way to escape. That is not our principle. Our Islam, our life, and our call are one. Our purpose is to give for Allah as long as we live, and we have nothing else before us to occupy us.

If you are of this kind, seek a place for yourself beside the Prophet ﷺ, to his right or his left. If you are among them, know that you will shake hands with Abu Bakr and recall stories with the Prophet, while you tell your own story of calling others to the faith. Perhaps you will sit with ‘Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, as he speaks about his spending in Allah's cause. Perhaps you will see Khalid ibn al-Walid telling you of his brave deeds with the sword, while you tell him of your brave deeds in calling people, even if they were through email and social media. What matters is how you use these tools.

### Expecting Thanks from No One

That is the purpose of the believers, and those other concerns are the condition of people occupied with trivialities. I do not think anyone sitting here now belongs to that group. We are people with a call to convey. We offer what we have to Allah, expecting neither thanks nor anything else from people. Our concern is Islam, finding salvation through it, and reaching Allah. This is the purpose of sensible people who understand reality and are not deceived by this world and what it holds.

I say these words and ask Allah to forgive me and you.

---
[^1]: Reported by al-Bukhari from Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, may Allah be pleased with him. The original hadith in the Sahih is longer. Not all the details related here by the preacher, such as the condition of Khabbab's back and the Prophet's anger ﷺ, are part of al-Bukhari's wording. What is given here conveys the meaning.
