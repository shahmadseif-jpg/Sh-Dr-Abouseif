*A study of a root that occurs thirty-five times in the Qur'an and is never affirmed of God in a single place. Heedlessness (ghafla) is portrayed as a state in which a person lives without knowing it, until the veil is removed and the truth he was always facing becomes visible.*

By Dr. Ahmed Muhammad Ali Abouseif, President of the American Imams Academy (AIA)

People are driven to the place of standing: ﴿وَجَاءَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَّعَهَا سَائِقٌ وَشَهِيدٌ﴾ "And every soul will come, with it a driver and a witness" (50:21), and the whole world falls silent, as a clamor does when the curtain is raised. In that moment a single sentence is spoken to the human being, one that sums up an entire lifetime: ﴿لَّقَدْ كُنتَ فِي غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ هَٰذَا فَكَشَفْنَا عَنكَ غِطَاءَكَ فَبَصَرُكَ الْيَوْمَ حَدِيدٌ﴾ "You were heedless of this, and We have removed from you your covering, so your sight today is sharp" (50:22). He is not told, "You were a denier," nor "You were ignorant," but "You were in heedlessness." It is as though the truth had stood before him all along, and what has changed today is not the truth but his eye. Al-Ṭabarī explained the unveiling here with the words: "So We made that plain to you and showed it to your eyes, until you saw it and beheld it, and the heedlessness left you"[^1]. In a nearby scene another echo rises from the mouths of those who disbelieved: ﴿يَا وَيْلَنَا قَدْ كُنَّا فِي غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ هَٰذَا بَلْ كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ﴾ "Woe to us! We were indeed heedless of this; rather, we were wrongdoers" (21:97). In their words "rather, we were wrongdoers" there is a hint that heedlessness is no longer an excuse to shelter behind, but has become a confession of fault.

### Thirty-Five Places, and Not One Affirms Heedlessness of God

The root gh-f-l occurs thirty-five times in the Qur'an[^2]: one simple verb, ﴿لَوْ تَغْفُلُونَ عَنْ أَسْلِحَتِكُمْ وَأَمْتِعَتِكُمْ﴾ "if only you would be heedless of your weapons and your baggage" (4:102); one quadriliteral verb, *aghfalnā* in Sūrat al-Kahf (18:28); five nouns, *ghafla* (19:39, 21:1, 21:97, 28:15, 50:22); twenty-seven forms of the masculine active participle *ghāfil* and its derivatives; and one feminine form, *al-ghāfilāt*, in Sūrat al-Nūr (24:23).

Tracing these forms reveals something that is not apparent to a reader who takes a verse in isolation. Of the twenty-eight places where the active participle occurs, heedlessness is negated of God, exalted is He, in eleven verses: six of them "«وما الله بغافل»" ("God is not unaware"), three "«وما ربك بغافل»" ("your Lord is not unaware"), the verse ﴿وَلَا تَحْسَبَنَّ اللَّهَ غَافِلًا عَمَّا يَعْمَلُ الظَّالِمُونَ﴾ "And never think that God is unaware of what the wrongdoers do" (14:42), and the verse "«وما كنا عن الخلق غافلين»" ("and We were never unaware of creation") (23:17). As for affirming heedlessness of God, there is not a single place in the whole Qur'an. In Qur'anic usage heedlessness belongs to creatures, and its negation from God is at once a form of declaring Him transcendent and a form of warning.

### "In" Heedlessness, and "Away From" Remembrance of Him: A State One Dwells In and a Turning Away One Intends

In Qur'anic Arabic, heedlessness takes two paths that are not interchangeable. The noun comes with *fī* ("in"): ﴿اقْتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ مُّعْرِضُونَ﴾ "The reckoning of mankind has drawn near to them, while they are in heedlessness, turning away" (21:1), and ﴿وَأَنذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ﴾ "And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will be decided, while they are in heedlessness" (19:39). The verb and the active participle, by contrast, come with *ʿan* ("away from"): "heedless of Our signs," ﴿لَوْ تَغْفُلُونَ عَنْ أَسْلِحَتِكُمْ﴾ "if only you would be heedless of your weapons". The particle *ʿan* (or *ʿammā*) accompanies twenty-one of the twenty-eight, seven occur absolutely without a preposition, and the Qur'an never says "he was heedless in" or "heedless in".

This is my own reading of the difference, and I place it before the reader as a deduction and not as a transmitted opinion: "in heedlessness" is a vessel one dwells in, a state its owner lives as one lives in a darkened room without knowing it is dark; "heedless of" is a turning away from a specific object, that is, from a sign, the Hereafter, or remembrance. The first describes a state, the second names what is abandoned. What supports this is that the scene we saw in Sūrat Qāf presents heedlessness as a state from which one is pulled out by the lifting of the covering, not as a fixed nature with no escape. Similar is God's word about the city Moses, peace be upon him, entered: ﴿وَدَخَلَ الْمَدِينَةَ عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا﴾ "And he entered the city at a time of heedlessness on the part of its people" (28:15), which al-Ṭabarī takes to mean "at the midday rest, the noon hour"[^3], an hour when people settle into ease and attention slackens; and from this heedlessness of place and time came the event that changed the whole course of Moses's life.

### What Bears No Mark: Heedlessness in the Speech of the Arabs

Al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī defines heedlessness as "an inadvertence that overtakes a person from lack of care and vigilance"[^4]. For him, then, mere inadvertence is not enough; it must be accompanied by a shortfall in guarding oneself. Ibn Fāris holds that the letters ghayn, fāʾ and lām are "a sound root indicating leaving a thing out of inadvertence, and sometimes deliberately"[^5]. He then adds a striking distinction: "*Ghaftu ʿan al-shayʾ ghaflatan wa-ghufūlan*, which is when you leave it while inadvertent. And *aghfaltuhu*, when you leave it while remembering it." So *ighfāl* is leaving with remembrance, and *ghafla* is leaving with inadvertence, and between the two there is a distance we shall need shortly in the verse of al-Kahf.

In the two lexicons there is a concrete image that opens the door: "A *ghufl* land: one that has no landmark. A *ghufl* she-camel: one that bears no brand. A *ghufl* man: one who has not tried affairs," and in al-Rāghib, "a *ghufl* man: one whom experiences have not branded"[^6]. So the *ghufl* in the original language is what bears no mark: a land without a signpost, a she-camel without a brand, a person on whom experience has engraved no trace. It is as though the heedless one were a heart that has not been branded with wakefulness. This is a beautiful key to the verse of al-Kahf.

### "We Made His Heart Heedless": Three Readings of One Verb

﴿وَاصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِالْغَدَاةِ وَالْعَشِيِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُ ۖ وَلَا تَعْدُ عَيْنَاكَ عَنْهُمْ تُرِيدُ زِينَةَ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۖ وَلَا تُطِعْ مَنْ أَغْفَلْنَا قَلْبَهُ عَن ذِكْرِنَا وَاتَّبَعَ هَوَاهُ وَكَانَ أَمْرُهُ فُرُطًا﴾ "And keep yourself patiently with those who call upon their Lord morning and evening, seeking His face, and let not your eyes pass beyond them, desiring the adornment of worldly life; and do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance, who follows his own desire and whose affair has become neglect" (18:28). This is the only verse in which *ighfāl* is ascribed to God. Al-Zamakhsharī paused over it and gave three readings of *aghfalnā*: that it means "We made his heart heedless of remembrance by forsaking him"; or "We found him heedless of it," as you say *ajbantuhu* and *abkhaltuhu* when you find him cowardly and miserly; or from "he *aghfala* his camels" when he leaves them without a brand, that is, "We did not brand him with remembrance and did not make them among those in whose hearts We wrote faith." Then he said: "And God has refuted the fancy of the determinists with His words ﴿وَاتَّبَعَ هَوَاهُ﴾ 'and followed his own desire'"[^7].

Al-Rāghib inclines to the third reading, which is the closest to what we saw in the origin of the root: "We left him without faith written in him," and then he mentions, with the hedging formula "it is said," "and it is said: it means, whom We made heedless of the realities"[^8]. This, in my reading, is no small disagreement: the first makes the *ighfāl* a punishment for what came before, the second describes a state already present, and the third makes the heart a land on which faith has not been engraved. In all cases the heart does not first turn heedless and then get blamed; rather, following desire was the act of its owner, and with this al-Zamakhsharī refuted the fancy of determinism. The context of the verse adds clarity: at its opening is a command to remain patient with those who remember morning and evening, then a prohibition against obeying one whose heart was made heedless, so the contrast stands between a heart branded with remembrance and a heart with no brand.

### The Prophet ﷺ and Heedlessness: Established in the Past, Forbidden in the Present

As for the Prophet ﷺ, he is not described in the Qur'an as "heedless" in a present description. The verse of Yūsuf ﴿وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِ لَمِنَ الْغَافِلِينَ﴾ "although before it you were among the unaware" (12:3) speaks of the time before revelation, and al-Ṭabarī explained it by saying: "And although you, Muhammad, before We revealed it to you, were among those unaware of that, not knowing it nor any part of it"[^9]. In Ibn ʿĀshūr: "And heedlessness: the absence of knowledge because the mind was not directed toward the thing known"[^10]. It is therefore a heedlessness of not knowing, not a heedlessness of negligence, and there is no blame in it; it stands in the setting of a favour: We relate to you the best of narratives through what We have revealed to you.

The other place is a prohibition, not a description: ﴿وَاذْكُر رَّبَّكَ فِي نَفْسِكَ تَضَرُّعًا وَخِيفَةً وَدُونَ الْجَهْرِ مِنَ الْقَوْلِ بِالْغُدُوِّ وَالْآصَالِ وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ الْغَافِلِينَ﴾ "And remember your Lord within yourself, humbly and with fear, and without loudness in speech, in the mornings and the evenings, and do not be among the heedless" (7:205). Here the two masters of exegesis differed. Al-Ṭabarī holds that the meaning is: "And do not be among those distracted, when the Qur'an is recited, from its admonitions and lessons," so for him it concerns the listener who attends in silence to the Qur'an in prayer or sermon. Ibn Kathīr disagreed and said: "Ibn Jarīr claimed, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam before him, that this verse means the command to the one listening to the Qur'an, while listening, to remember in this manner. This is far-fetched and contradicts the silence that is commanded," and then: "Rather the meaning is the urging of servants to remember much in the mornings and evenings, so that they not be among the heedless"[^11]. Al-Qurṭubī put it briefly: "'And do not be among the heedless,' that is, of remembrance"[^12].

What concerns us in the disagreement is that both sides agreed that the cure for heedlessness is remembrance. For al-Ṭabarī it is a humble remembrance at hearing the Qur'an; for Ibn Kathīr it is a remembrance extended between the two ends of the day. In my view, the strength of the verse in al-Aʿrāf is not in fixing the time but in the phrasing: "and do not be among the heedless," that is, do not enter a company of whom this is the character. The particle *min* here is for belonging to a group, not for marking a point of origin, so the prohibition is against joining a caravan, as though heedlessness were a current that sweeps away whoever has not clung to remembrance.

### Who Is Addressed by the Unveiling?

When we return to the verse of Qāf, we find that the exegetes paused over a delicate question: to whom is it said, "You were in heedlessness"? Al-Ṭabarī mentioned three opinions: that it is the disbeliever, that it is the Prophet of God ﷺ, and that it is all creation, jinn and humankind. Ibn Kathīr related the three opinions and mentioned that the view that it is every person (righteous and wicked) is the choice of Ibn Jarīr, "because the Hereafter, relative to this world, is like waking, and this world is like sleep," and that the view that it is the Prophet ﷺ is held by Zayd ibn Aslam and his son, and then he said: "The apparent sense of the context is contrary to this; rather the address is to the human being as such"[^13].

This disagreement has its effect on the meaning. If the address is to every human being, the scene becomes a mirror for every reader, and this world becomes, in Ibn Kathīr's phrase, like sleep and the Hereafter like waking. If it is to the disbeliever alone, the unveiling remains a punishment. I incline to the reading of Ibn Kathīr because the verse came after the scene of the driver and the witness, a scene that includes every soul.

### Heedlessness Joined to Turning Away, and Joined to Sealing

The Qur'an joins heedlessness to turning away at the opening of al-Anbiyāʾ, and al-Zamakhsharī pauses at their combination: "He described them as heedless along with turning away, in the sense that they are heedless of their reckoning, inattentive, not reflecting on their end, nor perceiving what the end of their affair leads to, while their reason requires that there must be recompense for the doer of good and the doer of evil"[^14]. This last sentence is the key to the door: heedlessness is not excused because reason itself gives warning. For this reason al-Ṭabarī said of the verse that they turned away, "so they abandoned reflecting on it and preparing for it."

Stronger than this are the two verses in which the phrase "those are the heedless" occurs: ﴿أُولَٰئِكَ كَالْأَنْعَامِ بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْغَافِلُونَ﴾ "Those are like livestock; rather, they are more astray. Those are the heedless" (7:179), and ﴿أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ ۖ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْغَافِلُونَ﴾ "Those are the ones upon whose hearts, hearing, and sight God has set a seal; and those are the heedless" (16:108). In the first, al-Ṭabarī, relaying Mujāhid, makes them hearts that do not understand and eyes that do not see, then "He informed that they are the heedless"; and al-Qurṭubī explained heedlessness there as that they "abandoned reflection and turned away from Paradise and the Fire"[^15]. Heedlessness here, as I read it, is the last degree on a ladder whose first rung is not listening and whose end is not feeling. In both verses it is a concluding description, as if it were the comprehensive name for what came before.

### "Your Lord Is Not Unaware": Reassurance and Warning

When we move to the eleven places where heedlessness is negated of God, we find a construction that almost repeats word for word: "of what you do." In Sūrat al-Baqara, after the description of the hardness of hearts: ﴿وَإِنَّ مِنْهَا لَمَا يَهْبِطُ مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ﴾ "and indeed, among them are those that fall down for fear of God. And God is not unaware of what you do" (2:74). At the end of Hūd, after the command to rely on Him: ﴿فَاعْبُدْهُ وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَيْهِ ۚ وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ﴾ "So worship Him and rely upon Him. And your Lord is not unaware of what you do" (11:123). And in Ibrāhīm: ﴿وَلَا تَحْسَبَنَّ اللَّهَ غَافِلًا عَمَّا يَعْمَلُ الظَّالِمُونَ ۚ إِنَّمَا يُؤَخِّرُهُمْ لِيَوْمٍ تَشْخَصُ فِيهِ الْأَبْصَارُ﴾ "And never think that God is unaware of what the wrongdoers do. He only delays them for a Day on which eyes will stare in horror" (14:42).

The negation has two aspects in the verses: a warning to the wrongdoer who supposed that the delay of punishment was neglect, so God made clear that the delay is not heedlessness but an appointed time; and a reassurance to the oppressed believer who relies on Him, lest he think his work is lost. Consider how reliance is joined to the negation of heedlessness in Hūd: as though reliance is valid only for one who knows that the One he relies upon does not become heedless. This too is a deduction of mine from the text of the verse, not a saying of an exegete that I relay. Here the contrast on which this chapter rests is completed: the human being becomes heedless and needs remembrance; God does not become heedless, so it is enough that He be remembered.

### What Is Established from the Prophet ﷺ: Heedlessness, Friday, and Night Prayer

The Sunna supports the contours of this meaning. In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (865) he ﷺ said: «لينتهين أقوام عن ودعهم الجمعات أو ليختمن الله على قلوبهم ثم ليكونن من الغافلين» "Let people stop leaving off the Friday prayers, or God will surely seal their hearts, and then they will surely be among the heedless"[^16]. Note how sealing the hearts is joined with heedlessness, as in the verse of al-Naḥl sealing is joined with heedlessness: leaving the Friday prayers through carelessness ends in a sealed heart, and the sealed heart is the heedless one. And in the hadith that Abū Dāwūd relates from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, he ﷺ said: «من قام بعشر آيات لم يكتب من الغافلين ومن قام بمائة آية كتب من القانتين ومن قام بألف آية كتب من المقنطرين» "Whoever stands in prayer with ten verses is not recorded among the heedless, whoever stands with a hundred verses is recorded among the devout, and whoever stands with a thousand verses is recorded among those who amass great reward," which al-Albānī graded sound[^17]. The wording is "whoever stands," not "whoever recites"; the measure is a standing with the verses in which the heart is present. It is as though ten verses with which the night is kept are a testimony that takes their owner out of the list of the heedless. This is from my reading of the hadith and not from a transmitted commentary: that the door is open to one whose provision is small.

### Ḥanẓala, may God be pleased with him: When the Believer Fears His Own Heedlessness

The finest human model of this meaning is a Companion who thought his heedlessness was hypocrisy. Ḥanẓala al-Usaydī, may God be pleased with him, came saying: «نافق حنظلة» "Ḥanẓala has become a hypocrite." The reason was that "we are with the Messenger of God ﷺ, he reminds us of the Fire and Paradise until it is as though we see them with our own eyes; then when we leave him, we busy ourselves with wives, children and farms, and we forget much." The Prophet ﷺ answered him: «والذي نفسي بيده إن لو تدومون على ما تكونون عندي وفي الذكر لصافحتكم الملائكة على فرشكم وفي طرقكم، ولكن يا حنظلة ساعة وساعة» "By Him in whose hand is my soul, if you were to remain as you are with me and in remembrance, the angels would shake hands with you on your beds and in your paths; but, Ḥanẓala, an hour and an hour," three times[^18].

The word "heedlessness" does not occur in the hadith, but the state is precisely its state. It contains what sets the measure of this study: the heedlessness that the Qur'an condemns is persisting in it, being content with it, and turning away from its remedy, whereas mere occupation with family and land belongs to human nature. Ḥanẓala was alarmed because his heart is alive and holds itself to account, while the truly heedless one is he to whom it never occurs to be afraid. "An hour and an hour" is not leniency toward heedlessness but an acknowledgment that wakefulness breathes, so long as the person returns.

### From the Verse to Life: How Do We Guard Ourselves from the Heedlessness of the Crowd?

What follows from the above is that heedlessness in the Qur'an is a state one dwells in without its owner knowing he is in it; that it is not ascribed to God but negated of Him; that its remedy is remembrance; and that God will lift it one day by removing the covering, while it remains for us in this world to ask Him to lift it before then. From here we turn to application.

The first thing an educator should learn is that the heedless is not treated by rebuke alone, for he does not see what he is being rebuked for. He needs someone to lift the covering from him a little in this world: with a question that awakens, a scene that reminds, or a remembering companionship, as in the verse of al-Kahf that commanded patience with those who remember. Then the summons to prayer, Friday and night standing are not extra burdens, but are what brands the heart so that it does not remain *ghufl*, with no mark on it. In an age when screens crowd out moments of reflection, heedlessness may become a collective way of living that no one feels, because everyone is in it. Here His words show their force: "and do not be among the heedless," a warning against belonging before falling.

A person may take "an hour and an hour" as a method: not demanding of himself constant presence, but setting fixed times to which he returns, such as the mornings and evenings, ten verses at night, and a Friday that is never left. Whoever keeps these anchors is not swept away by the current.

### Conclusion

Heedlessness begins quietly, with no sound heard from it, and ends with the cry "Woe to us!" Between the beginning and the end lies an opportunity that does not last: that the covering be lifted while we are still in this world, so that we see while we are able to act, not at a standing where sight is sharp and its owner can do nothing but confess. O God, do not make us among the heedless, do not entrust us to our heedlessness, wake our hearts with Your remembrance before the terrors wake them, and lift our covering in this world by Your mercy, for You were never unaware of Your creation, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.
