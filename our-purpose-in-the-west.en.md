> Occasion: A summary of a Friday sermon delivered on July 31, 2026.
> Source: A transcript of a video sermon by Dr. Ahmed Abouseif. [Watch the original video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqtlD0mCExo).

Imagine the Prophet ﷺ sitting near the Kaaba in the early days of his mission, thinking about the future of this faith. Nothing in his life concerned him more. Then imagine him entering your mosque today and seeing the different colors, languages, and backgrounds of its people: the countries they came from and the many accents gathered in one place. Would he not be happy to see you, and take comfort in knowing that Allah had prepared people on earth to serve His faith? How blessed is someone who gives the Prophet ﷺ a reason for joy before his Lord. From this scene, the preacher asks his question: What is our purpose in these lands?

### Why Are You Here?

From the moment you decided to leave your country and come here, and then made an even more serious decision—to have your children here—what was your concern? What was your purpose? What was your reason? The preacher says that our daily dealings have taught us something: those who came to America to gather great wealth eventually returned to the Lord of all worlds and left their wealth behind. These lands, he says, know how to give to you and take from you again at the very same moment, leaving no one with anything. Everyone competes and runs after an illusion called gathering wealth, improving one's circumstances, and increasing one's income.

Yet this happens everywhere on earth. People's days pass with the comfortable life Allah has provided, which they grow used to in time, or with hardship, which they learn to live with in time. Look at the people of Gaza: how they live, how their day passes, and where they find food. Then turn to Somalia, Mali, or other lands where life is hard. You will see smiles on many faces; people cry at times and laugh at others. The preacher then puts it briefly: this world's end is death; life's end is a grave; and the end of our days is to stand before the Lord of all worlds.

### Allah Knows Best Where to Place His Message

The question remains: Why are we here? Anyone who says, “I brought myself here,” has misjudged the matter. Allah gathers people in the place He wants for them, at the time when He knows it is right for them to be there. The Quran says, in a translation of its meaning: “Allah knows best where to place His message. Those who have committed crimes will suffer humiliation before Allah and severe punishment for the plots they used to make” [al-An‘am 6:124]. Reflect on this short passage. Allah knows best which heart can take in the message, which mind can reflect on it, which tongue will be entrusted with it, and which body will carry its concern. He also knows which city, which state, which country, and which time.

### Do We Carry This Faith or Simply Follow It?

Here is a simple question: Do you carry this faith, or do you simply follow it? There is a great difference between someone who enters Islam and finds personal comfort, peace of heart, calm nerves, and a system for living—Fajr prayer, then peace; Isha prayer, then restful sleep with a contented heart, and everything is settled—and someone who is addressed as the Prophet ﷺ was in Surat al-Kahf. In a translation of the verse's meaning: “Perhaps you will grieve yourself to death over them if they do not believe in this message” [al-Kahf 18:6]. Will you wear yourself out with worry and responsibility because they have not accepted Islam? This is the great responsibility placed upon Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the prophets before him. They were bound by a covenant to convey the message. They carried it as a trust and delivered it to people with the same excellence shown by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

And what about us? We leave for work in the morning, troubled by a long day and weighed down by many duties. We return home at the end of the day with our strength spent and our drive gone. A person brings his worries home, sleeps until morning, and opens his eyes worried about another day. Yet the bond connecting my heart and mind to heaven is missing its greatest concern: conveying the faith.

The preacher gives an example. If I asked about your job, you would say that you are a doctor, an engineer, or a teacher, and explain what the job requires. But that job comes second. My first task in life is that the Lord of all worlds has charged me with conveying the message, and I am an heir to the Prophet's ﷺ legacy. Do we bring this understanding to people's minds and hearts so that they rise to the task of calling others to the faith and accept its demands on their money, effort, time, and giving? Or do we sit around a table of food called our livelihood, gather around it, and guard it, refusing to take any risk lest we put the table at risk? We fear for our children at the slightest word or touch, and for our livelihood at every possible threat.

### Abu Bakr: “I Left Allah and His Messenger for Them”

This was not how the Prophet's ﷺ Companions lived. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ would stand on his pulpit and say: People, we need money for such and such a purpose. Who would be the first to raise his hand? Abu Bakr al-Siddiq: the first to answer the Messenger of Allah, not only with money, but whenever the Prophet ﷺ called. He would go home and return with money—all his money. Even if his capital had grown many times over, he would bring it all. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked him, “What have you left for your family?” his answer was: “I left Allah and His Messenger for them.”[^1]

He was a skilled trader. But a business mindset says: I must have funds, a financial reserve, strategies, and stock. Abu Bakr, by contrast, would almost always start again from zero. He trusted that Allah is the One who sends provision. He did not look at the money in his pocket as what would see him through the day or the week. The One who makes these means work is their Creator, glorified is He. Someone with skill in trade and, alongside it, firm certainty in what the Lord of all worlds holds, puts 100 percent of his wealth into the transaction, certain that it will return to him.

### A Hundred Years from Now

Servants of Allah, we are here to deliver a trust, fulfill a mission, and carry this faith. We will all die: in ten years, or even ten days, or perhaps in a few hours. If we ask who among us thinks he will still be above the soil a hundred years from today, the answer is no one. Once we are beneath the soil, we will either have left behind an Islam alive and active on earth, as the Prophet's ﷺ Companions did, or we will have gone beneath the soil and relieved humanity of the burden we had placed upon it.

We also live in a time that turns people's lives upside down. The one who holds firmly to his faith is the one who will be saved. As the preacher explains in paraphrasing a hadith, a time will come when remaining patient in one's faith will be like holding a burning ember.[^2] Imagine holding an ember that you cannot bear for even a few seconds. It burns your hand, yet you hold on to it. That is the matter of faith: whoever guards it is saved, and whoever neglects it is lost.

### A Mosque That Trains Imams

This is the same purpose the preacher and his brothers chose to follow many years ago, when they came to these lands. They pledged to the Lord of all worlds that they would be among those who carry this trust in this land. He says that they now have a mosque in the Dallas area of Texas that is more than a place of prayer. It is a place for training imams: teaching them how to stand on these pulpits, lead people in prayer, resolve problems between spouses, and secure justice for those who have been wronged. “We train them in what we were trained in, and more.” The only difference is one of language and culture. To what they learned at al-Azhar, they add what is needed to address the new circumstances of life in these lands.

He explains that meeting the students' needs and keeping the mosque's doors open, its lights on, and its carpets laid can only happen through the giving of righteous people like his listeners. He asks Allah to make them among those who are first to do good.

He closes with a supplication: O Allah, grant us contentment through Your pleasure, and make us firm with strength from You. Wherever we may be, enable us to fulfill Your purpose, and guide us to the good You want us to do. O Allah, stand by everyone who is oppressed on earth, and stand by our people in Palestine and Gaza.

The preacher's wording is “تركت لهم الله ورسوله,” which carries the same meaning in English.
[^2]: Editor's source note: Reported by al-Tirmidhi from Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, with the wording, translated here: “A time will come upon people when the one who remains patient in his faith will be like someone holding a burning ember.” The preacher used the Arabic phrase “القابض فيه على دينه” (“holding on to his faith at that time”).
