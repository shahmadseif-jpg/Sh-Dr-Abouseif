Praise be to Allah, and blessings and peace upon our noble Messenger. O Allah, we ask You for the good of this night, the good of what it holds and the good of what follows it, and we seek refuge in You from its evil, the evil of what it holds and the evil of what follows it. O Allah, set right our hearts and our minds, make our tongues speak what pleases You, and do not disgrace us in our worldly life or in our hereafter. May Allah bless your gathering and reward your effort in coming to these blessed places, and we ask Him, blessed and exalted, that we be among those whom mercy envelops, upon whom tranquility descends, and whom Allah mentions among those who are with Him.

Our actions depend on how a person has been brought up: either they are considered, reasonable actions, or they are random actions. Among the random ones we notice is that a man comes to you weeping and says: Help me, Shaykh, the word of divorce has slipped out of me, I have divorced my wife! So what does this word mean? And what follows from it? And what do matters come to at such words? They come, for the most part, to ruin in the children's future, the breaking up of this family, hardship in the level of spending on its members, and so on, all that can follow from a single word that came out in a random, unconsidered way: he said "you are divorced" and the matter was over!

And take another example: a man in a quarrel with another pulls out his pistol and fires a bullet at the one in front of him. What followed from this act? Only that the breadwinner of a family went where you know, that a member of society was gone, and that a family was left homeless. And what follows from a quarrel between two parties over something trivial, or over some matter that occurs to one person and grows in his head until he goes out and acts randomly?

### From the Beginnings of the Call: The Prophet ﷺ Teaches Looking to the Outcome

**Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī (may Allah be pleased with him).** In the beginnings of the da'wah movement in the time of the Prophet ﷺ we saw a prominent incident: Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī, may Allah be pleased with him, a man of Ghifār, came to become Muslim, so he entered upon the Prophet ﷺ and testified that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. So what condition did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ impose on him? Do you remember? He required him to go out among the people openly declaring his Islam.[^1] So what did Abū Dharr do? He went out among the people openly declaring his Islam. So what followed from that? They beat him very severely, until al-ʿAbbās or someone else came and said: Woe to you, are you killing a man of Ghifār, when your trade passes by them going and coming? Then he began to warn them of the consequences and outcomes of the act they were committing when they beat a man like Abū Dharr, who is from the tribe of Ghifār, and you know that Ghifār used to waylay travelers. For this is a man who, even if he were not Muslim, is from a tribe whose basic character is to attack people, so how do you expose yourselves to such things? And I see in this incident that the Prophet ﷺ, through his first stipulation to Abū Dharr, wanted to teach him what is called the jurisprudence of consequences: what will matters come to?

**The Trench and Ḥudhayfah (may Allah be pleased with him).** And on the day of the Trench the Prophet ﷺ says: «من يأتينا بخبر القوم؟» (Who will bring us news of the people?). Imagine the scene: the Muslims behind the trench, the polytheists gathered to the last man beyond the trench, and the Prophet ﷺ wants to grasp the dimensions of what matters are coming to over there: what is happening? So he sent Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān, may Allah be pleased with him, from among his Companions, and he set out toward the people. And when the Prophet ﷺ commanded him to investigate the features of the situation for him, he said to him: Do not provoke the people against us, or a phrase like it that the Prophet ﷺ said, meaning: do not stir up their grudges against us.[^2]

Ḥudhayfah says: So I went out, and while I was looking toward the people, there was Abū Sufyān warming his back by a fire, the night bitterly cold and the wind fierce, and the man was seeking warmth, leaning his back against the fire until he was warm. He says: So I looked at him from afar, and I placed my arrow in my quiver; had I loosed it, it would have landed between his shoulders, that is, at the very core of his neck, but I remembered the words of the Prophet ﷺ: do not provoke the people against us.

Why did the Prophet ﷺ say that? Because the people had come only to fight Islam, and they were now studying the situation and reviewing: what shall we do? Shall we continue? Shall we go back? They were planning and reviewing. And imagine Abū Sufyān being killed in such a place! These people would have stormed this trench like lions seeking revenge, because their chief and leader would have been killed treacherously while in their midst, and they would have taunted the Muslims with it for all time, and would never have let such revenge pass. So when the Prophet ﷺ uttered that one word, he uttered it with regard to what follows from a single act. Ḥudhayfah says: So I remembered the words of the Prophet ﷺ, so I put my arrow in my quiver and then continued on my way. This is how Allah, blessed and exalted, put this man's mind at his service until he regulated his action and looked to the consequences of the matters before him.

And if we mention Abū Sufyān, what do we add? Do we say "may Allah be pleased with him"? Had he been killed in such a place we would not have said "may Allah be pleased with him," because he would have died a disbeliever.[^3] And you can imagine all that would have followed from that.

**The Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah.** Likewise the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah, which the Lord of the worlds called a clear victory. The Prophet ﷺ hears from the Lord of Might, glory be to Him, His words: ﴿إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا﴾ (Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory),[^4] and in this sūrah there came down: ﴿لَقَدْ صَدَقَ اللَّهُ رَسُولَهُ الرُّؤْيَا بِالْحَقِّ ۖ لَتَدْخُلُنَّ الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ آمِنِينَ مُحَلِّقِينَ رُءُوسَكُمْ وَمُقَصِّرِينَ لَا تَخَافُونَ ۖ فَعَلِمَ مَا لَمْ تَعْلَمُوا فَجَعَلَ مِن دُونِ ذَٰلِكَ فَتْحًا قَرِيبًا﴾ (Allah has certainly fulfilled the vision for His Messenger in truth: you will surely enter al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, if Allah wills, in security, with your heads shaved and hair shortened, not fearing; for He knew what you did not know, and He placed before that a near victory).[^5] The polytheists came out to the Prophet ﷺ, preventing him from entering Mecca, while the Prophet ﷺ had come only to perform ʿumrah; he came driving the sacrificial animals before him, but the polytheists prevented him. And Suhayl sat before the Prophet ﷺ to set out the indications of this agreement and its contents, so they wrote a draft of a preliminary agreement: that you return from where you came, and the Prophet ﷺ agreed. And that you not accept anyone from Quraysh who comes to you, while we accept whoever comes from you to us, and the Prophet ﷺ agreed. And that it be such and such and that it be such and such, and all of this the Prophet ﷺ accepted.

And more severe than that: when the Prophet ﷺ commanded that "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" be written, Suhayl objected to this phrase and said: "In Your name, O Allah," and that is what we know. And when the Prophet ﷺ said: "This is what Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, has agreed," Suhayl said: If we knew you to be a messenger we would have followed you, so erase this. And ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, may Allah be pleased with him, refuses to erase the words "Messenger of Allah," and the Prophet ﷺ does not read, so he commands him, saying: Where is it? So he points to it, and the Prophet ﷺ takes the sheet and wipes it with his own hands ﷺ.[^6] Why did the Prophet ﷺ do all that, while overlooking many errors that were occurring before him? Because they were things that could be borne, and from their people, because he looks to the consequences.

Then it happened afterward that a man came out of Mecca seeking the Prophet ﷺ because he had become Muslim, and within the framework of the truce, in the time of the treaty, and under these terms, a man went out from Mecca to Medina aiming for the Prophet ﷺ: Abū Jandal. So he came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ says to him, in effect: You know that there is a covenant between us and the people. Imagine the matter: someone leaves Mecca for Medina as a Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ sends him back again! Imagine this matter. So what did Abū Jandal do? He went to the roadside, on the sea road as they say, and camped there, and people learned of him, so everyone who became Muslim in Mecca would head to Abū Jandal and not to the Prophet ﷺ, until they became a band, and they began lying in wait for those who came from Mecca, and it became like an ambush set in the middle of the road.[^7] The point is that it was Mecca that sent to the Prophet ﷺ to gather his Companions to him, so the people returned to the Prophet ﷺ as Muslims without any breach of the covenant on his part. And imagine if the Prophet ﷺ had accepted such a man: Quraysh, with those in it and around it, would have risen against him, saying that he is a man who breaks contracts, and that he is this and that he is that, and the odor of disbelief and corruption would have wafted up with claims that had no basis in truth. And this is what people of reason ought to avoid.

**The Oppressed in Mecca.** And the Lord of Might, glory be to Him, tells us in His words, the Mighty and Majestic: ﴿وَلَوْلَا رِجَالٌ مُّؤْمِنُونَ وَنِسَاءٌ مُّؤْمِنَاتٌ لَّمْ تَعْلَمُوهُمْ أَن تَطَئُوهُمْ فَتُصِيبَكُم مِّنْهُم مَّعَرَّةٌ﴾ (And if not for believing men and believing women whom you did not know, that you might trample them and thus incur guilt on their account).[^8] And this too is connected with the matter of the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah. His Lord, glory be to Him, informed the Prophet ﷺ that if he wished to raid Mecca, Allah the Mighty and Majestic would help him in that. But imagine, for example, that in Mecca there were Muslims who had not declared their Islam; so if the Prophet ﷺ had entered Mecca as a fighter, some people would have been killed, and among them might be Muslims, and then the Prophet ﷺ would have been taunted for the rest of his life with the Muslims who were killed.

And here I would like to stop for one minute to reflect that Allah the Exalted only preserved the blood of Muslims within the disbelieving society. Allah the Exalted wanted to preserve Muslim blood within the disbelieving society, and the Prophet ﷺ at that time had the right to storm this city: we came to perform ʿumrah and were prevented, and we want to defend our religion and move on. But he forbore and refrained ﷺ, in order to preserve the blood of some Muslims who represent a Muslim minority within the polytheist society.

And if the claim of those who are now killing in various countries, bombing sites, and producing what is called terrorism were true, if indeed they can rightly be called Muslims, or were truly Muslims, and not a hired group given this label... I do not think at all that a rational Muslim would extend his hand to harm anyone. So if it were believed that he is a Muslim, or that they belong to this religion, how far are they from such a stance as the Prophet ﷺ undertook, concerning which Allah sent down upon His Messenger ﷺ verses from heaven to be recited until the Day of Resurrection pointing to the preservation of Muslim blood among minorities? The attacks that occur here and there, in many places, have a negative effect: how many women take off the hijab because they do not want to be accused of terrorism after Muslims bombed in certain places! And how many young people turn away from their religion or hide it! And how many Muslim men change their names! And so on, all the constraint that follows from that on mosques and Islamic institutions and relief organizations, and in the transfer of funds from abroad to inside and from inside to abroad. Huge undertakings whose numbers and names we cannot count are all affected, indeed demolished, under a single slogan that wants to say: I raise the slogan "There is no god but Allah," and this is jihad! By Allah, you have lied.

**Other Incidents.** In any case, as we present these proofs, we see for example the words of the Lord of Might, glory be to Him, the Exalted: ﴿وَلَا تَسُبُّوا الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ فَيَسُبُّوا اللَّهَ عَدْوًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ﴾ (And do not insult those they invoke other than Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge).[^9] Did you see this business of consequences? And the story of Zaynab's marriage, may Allah be pleased with her, after she had been the wife of Zayd, may Allah be pleased with them all, came before the Prophet ﷺ, and the Lord of Might, glory be to Him, tells us in His words: ﴿فَلَمَّا قَضَىٰ زَيْدٌ مِّنْهَا وَطَرًا زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا لِكَيْ لَا يَكُونَ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ حَرَجٌ فِي أَزْوَاجِ أَدْعِيَائِهِمْ إِذَا قَضَوْا مِنْهُنَّ وَطَرًا﴾ (So when Zayd had concluded his need of her, We married her to you, so that there would be no difficulty for the believers concerning the wives of their adopted sons when they have concluded their need of them).[^10] Why? So that not. Do you see? It is attentiveness to the matter of what follows from the act.

These are a set of illustrations and proofs that establish for us the credibility of what we are speaking about, from the well-known incidents of the Prophet's biography. Among them that a Bedouin man entered the mosque, the mosque of the Prophet ﷺ, and went to one corner of the mosque and urinated there, and the Companions jumped up! A disturbing scene, what is this man doing? A madman! Imagine a person in the mosque, in a corner of it, urinating and flooding the place, Allahu akbar! And the Companions were about to go for him, when the Prophet ﷺ says a word: «لا تُزْرِموه» (Do not interrupt him), and in one narration: do not cut off his urination.[^11] And as soon as the man had finished, the Prophet ﷺ took him by the hand and said to him: Mosques like these are not suited for this, and he taught him in a few words, and the man thereafter rectified his conduct and learned, though he had violated a number of sanctities: the sanctity of the Prophet's ﷺ presence, the sanctity of the mosque of the Prophet ﷺ, the sanctity of the presence of these Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, and exposing private parts in public places. Imagine what could have followed from this man being rebuked while he was urinating, and people cutting off his urination, which might have led to some kind of bodily harm or illness, whatever it might be!

And I still remember a remark from one of my friends, I ask Allah to bless him, and he is a physician. One day he lifted his son, you know when you swing your son like this and play with him, you lift him above your head, a small child. It seems the place was very comfortable for our little fellow, so he began to urinate, and the urine came down on his father's head. And the child's grandmother was sitting, that is, his mother-in-law, his wife's mother, and she said to him: Put him down, he is doing it on your head! He said to her: No, never mind, let him finish. So our friend waited until the child had finished, and Allah honored him and things settled in his hands, and what was in him was emptied, so he took him and hugged him to his chest, then put him down, then went off and came back, and said to her: Mother-in-law, do you know how many operations we perform in the hospital on a child who cannot pass urine from his body? No fewer than six successive operations until we make it possible for a child to let water out of his body. So that my son urinates by the creation of Allah, blessed and exalted, this is not only on top of my head, but on top of my head and on top of my head. These are the consequences of matters, and this is the mind that thinks about the matter before acting or before speaking.

And we have before us what happened with ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl, and what you know of the hypocrisy that occurred then, and it was suggested to the Prophet ﷺ that he kill him, so he ﷺ says: «أتريدون أن يُقال: محمد يقتل أصحابه؟» (Do you want it to be said: Muhammad kills his companions?).[^12] By Allah, besides whom there is no god, this sentence alone, if those working for Islam, specifically in Western societies, were to take it into account: Do you want it to be said that Muslims do such and such? Or that Muslims differ over such and such? Or that Muslims quarrel over such and such? By Allah, every one of us would hand over his neck to people in submission, because of the words of Allah, blessed and exalted: ﴿أَذِلَّةٍ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَعِزَّةٍ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ﴾ (humble toward the believers, mighty against the disbelievers).[^13] And that is the logic through which we can deal.

### Consequences Within the Home

And among the important consequences is the matter of caring for the future. Let us enter the homes a little, among spouses. **Divorce is not a word; it is a joint decision issued after deliberation and composure.** Imagine the form of divorce recognized in Islam: that the man divorces his wife with a single divorce, in a period of purity in which he has not had relations with her. That is, the woman, may Allah honor you, has her menstruation, for example, then becomes pure from her menstruation, then he holds himself back from her, then he divorces her, then a month passes over her and she menstruates and becomes pure, and he divorces her, then a month passes. This is a decision with premeditation and lying in wait, and not merely a word in a moment: the glass got broken, or she did not make the tea right, or the cooking came out salty or whatever. It is true that divorce may be a word, and it really is a word, but what follows from it? This is the discourse with which we should appeal to the members of the Muslim society, so that they realize that it is not a word, but a decision that should come after living together has become impossible and after seeking all the causes and means by which the two parties could be brought together.

Why? Because we have children in our hands whom we want to raise for the future. If the home breaks, the hearts of these children break, and after that their minds break, because they are torn between two contradictory ways of thinking, and the parents differ, and not that courteous kind of difference. And few are the cases we see, when a man and woman divorce, in which we see each of them keen on the reputation of the other. Glory be to Allah the Almighty! I spoke with one of the brothers yesterday and said to him: By Allah, besides whom there is no god, when I spoke about you with your wife she said: He is the best man in the world, and if a woman were seeking a husband now I would nominate you for her! And you two are already divorced. And when I spoke with you, you said she is the best woman in the world, and if a man were looking for a woman to marry I would find none better to recommend to him! Glory be to Allah, you both say that about each other, so where is the fault? He said: Incompatibility. I said: I believe in Allah, the matter is finished. Incompatibility, they are not compatible, but respect remains, and common ground remains, and the absence of insult remains, and of vilification, and there remains the word that preserves the privacy of bygone days and secures the future of the children who will come out.

Imagine: today I speak about my wife with a word, half a word or any talk, and ten years later someone comes to propose to my daughter, and this word is pulled from the archive of people's memory, and it is said to him: No, listen, this man used to say about her mother such and such! Allahu akbar, after all these years this word is pulled from the archive of memory and recorded in the minds of the suitors who are watching my door to propose to my daughter. And so the days pass, glory be to Allah the Most High, the Almighty.

Dear brothers, marital disputes, when one of us gets worked up and makes a row inside the home, are not "the salt of life," but rather the misery of the children. Children who live in an atmosphere pervaded by quarreling are not sound in any case. So do not imagine, sir, that you spent the night victorious, or began your night saying: I gave her a couple of words in her honor, I did it, I am the victor! Do not imagine that, for the real loser in this battle is not the other party, but the third party: your children. Glory be to Allah the Almighty!

And if we imagine that in most families there arises what is called material conflict, whether over daily expenses to be met among people: the wife wants this, and the husband says I don't have it, or I don't have any on me, or he is stingy toward them or so on. These simple material conflicts that occur within families bring about in us a materialistic generation. By Allah, besides whom there is no god, these material conflicts are planning, with premeditation and lying in wait, for a generation that will disobey us after today. Why? Because when I raise my son on a purely materialistic mentality, then I come after fifty years, or after twenty years, and speak to him: My son, I have become incapable, I cannot work, please give me a tenth of your salary, he says to you: Who are you? I don't know you! Why? Because I never spoke in front of him except about money, and he never saw me speak about money for management or care, but rather saw selfishness and excess in trying to take as much as I could, even if it was from my life partner.

And likewise gossip inside homes is the manufacture of a foul future within the Muslim society, because the children hear the father talking about so-and-so, and the mother talking about so-and-so, and the this and the that. So imagine what the affairs of our children will come to in such atmospheres.

### The Lesson of Maʿrūf al-Karkhī

If we imagine the matter of consequences in the world of those working in the field of da'wah... send blessings upon the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Before I give the answer on this point, I want to recall an incident that happened to a man of the early generations called Maʿrūf al-Karkhī, from the people of Iraq, there in the region on the bank of the Tigris, sitting with a group of disciples around him. Imagine the scene: a shaykh of the righteous predecessors sitting with his disciples around him. One of his disciples said: O Imam, have you not seen these young men in this boat? They are listening to music and playing and singing, and it seems they are in a state of some intoxication. A group of young men who went out at night, with a commotion, singing and enjoying themselves. If only you would pray against them! So he looked at them, then raised his hands to the sky and began to supplicate. What does he supplicate for now? Against these wicked young men? Against these disobedient young men? He said: "O Allah, as You have made them happy in this world, make them happy in the Hereafter." So they reproached him for what he said, and he answered them, saying: If He made them happy in the Hereafter, He would have delivered them to His religion in this world. Are we agreed?

I imagine the consequences here, and I want you to imagine with me: had our man raised his hands and prayed against them, may our Lord take vengeance on them and ruin their homes and so on, and then one of them met him the next morning drunk. You say to him: Are you drunk?! Why? He says to you: I have been drunk all my life because of our master's prayer, he prayed against us so we remain drunk all our lives. Do you see? The result may follow like this. And here we stop: and if someone conveyed to these young men the supplication of that Imam for them, that he had prayed for them for happiness in the Hereafter and happiness in this world, would that not be a motive for them to look for the means of guidance and to attend the Shaykh's sessions and listen to him? Just imagine. And this is merely one picture before we delve into the matter of those working in the field of da'wah and the jurisprudence of consequences for them.

(And one of them interrupted me with a question, so I joked with him: Why are you rushing? You do not know the consequences that will occur after your question! Then I said: Shall we continue, young men, or do you have a question? Fine, in the name of Allah.)

### Consequences for Callers

**Protecting the symbol.** I am speaking here about the moral deviations that occur in the careers of callers, and I am speaking about myself and the brothers who are in the field of da'wah. That is, if we imagine, for example: is it possible that an error will come from me as a human being? Of course, what prevents it? But do you know what prevents it? What prevents it is that a generation of young people, like the respectable young people here, look at one of us as a figure of stature within society, and a generation of young people is being raised at the hands of this brother of ours, and a generation is being raised at the hands of that brother of ours and listens to him in his various settings, and a generation looks to so-and-so, and so on. So I place a barrier between myself and what I desire, guarding against the deviation of these young people from their path, because they see before them a caller who has deviated: any kind of defect, whether a defect in money, or women, or lying, or deception, whatever the defect. Sometimes desire arises inside the man, or the human being in general, and he could do it, even lawfully, and yet he refrains from some of what he wants in order to guard against the reactions that occur in the generation of young people.

And whether it is moral deviations or an exaggerated responsiveness in the world of politics: how many people used to trust the learned Imam Shaykh so-and-so, or the great imam who represents a scholarly stature in such-and-such a place, but he sides with one party in politics, and suddenly he is spilling people's blood, or taunting so-and-so, or doing such and such, so the whole value collapses. And then the young people look at their points of reference and see them as dust, and the symbols fall with them, or the symbols fall in their eyes, and with them falls the phrase "There is no god but Allah" and commitment to the rulings. Imagine, for example, when people see the man with the big turban, in this appearance with the cloak and so on, walking behind politics in such a way, so what makes a woman walk behind the hijab? And what makes her protect herself when she can do this and that? What protects her from that? These are the consequences of matters.

**Discourse far from people's lives.** The second matter: the pattern of religious discourse, and talk about matters totally remote from what people live, creates before us a generation that feels that religion is on one side and life is on another, so it detaches entirely from questions of religion, and rarely asks except about certain things. Glory be to Allah! We are praying, and our dear brother says to the brother: Please, put your foot next to my foot. I said: Glory be to Allah, what is the importance of this matter now? Except that Allah the Exalted made of it a kind of training in general order in the nation, and if this detail were violated, perhaps the general order of the nation would be violated. But the reality and what happens is that he trades in forbidden things, then comes to say: Is this meat slaughtered or not slaughtered? So he totally separates issues that carry weight and danger for the lives of Muslims, and he follows matters I cannot say are secondary, but he separates a life he practices from food he eats.

And I still remember one of the brothers telling me, by Allah: He came in to me with a girl, masha'Allah, of striking beauty, and says to me: Brother, is this meat halal? And he is telling me this and says: By Allah, I was not ashamed. He entered a shop that by default is a shop of Muslims, and asked as if he wanted to verify. When I enter your shop and you are a Muslim, I should not need to ask; and it would be logical, if you came to my house to eat with me, and you know that I am a Muslim, not to ask. But this foolishness, or these trivialities we are speaking about, in our settings... I am not speaking about the matter of the lawful and unlawful in food, but I am speaking about the logic of people in dealing with certain particulars while leaving the other universals. The discourse far from people's lives, which issues from imams or from callers or from shaykhs, whatever the terms used, the discourse far from reality creates this generation that imagines that religion is on one side, and that the life we practice is on another.

**Callers' disputes in front of people.** We have a matter I alluded to briefly: the disputes that occur between callers. We are now speaking about callers and what matters have come to because of their disputes. I now have a problem with my brother, and we speak in the midst of the people, so we make the children watch us. So how do they watch us?

**Not keeping pace with what people live: the crescent.** And we have a second, more serious matter, which is the matter of not keeping pace with what people live, with all respect to those who insist that we see the crescent with our own eyes, and with all my esteem and respect for this matter, but I will repeat to you once more what my children and sons, the Muslims in the community in which we live, said last year in Ramadan. So if I married a woman under certain circumstances, with a righteous intention and a confident mind, then Allah, glory be to Him, the Exalted, decreed that we differ, so how do we differ now, and when do we differ, and what will this difference lead to? If these matters are settled in the contract, then Allah, blessed and exalted, rewards the servant according to the knowledge and intention he has. Are we agreed? Is the answer clear, or a little more? We did not marry so as to be sentenced to death: that we must remain together with this woman all our lives, or she must remain under this man's hand all his life. No, we live in a framework of life of give and take, between peace and so on. But what do you mean? I married in order to go and divorce her a little later after I get citizenship or get the papers or get the two pennies she inherited? Is that my intention? Or is my intention to live, but we differed, differed, differed, without any means of reconciliation? Then if the decision of divorce is taken with the required calm and the excellence that the Lord of the worlds, glory be to Him, the Exalted, sought, then there is no offense for which we seek forgiveness. Are we agreed? And if she comes to say: I cannot live with this man for the rest of my life, fine, why? So we gather the matter and reflect on the subject and so on. For this reason the man has the right of divorce and the woman the right of khulʿ; this is in the woman's hands, she requests it, and that is in the man's hands, he exercises it. And since when the woman requests khulʿ, and she has someone who manages her affairs during marriage, there must be someone who manages her affairs during khulʿ, whether a judge or the ruler of the Muslims or the imam of the community or whoever is in these matters. And Allah, blessed and exalted, knows best. So Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, holds us accountable for random actions, or holds us accountable for bad intention. But if the mind is balanced and the movement controlled, then Allah, blessed and exalted, taught our Prophet ﷺ to tell us: «إنما الأعمال بالنيات، وإنما لكل امرئ…» (Actions are only by intentions, and every person will have only …).[^24]

**Divorce and the children.** And another asked on the same point: Divorce used to happen in the days of the Messenger and the Companions and it was not considered a crime. So I said: No, it is not a crime. He said: I mean that the consequences for the children are sometimes that the spouses continue in marriage while the disagreement among them is great, so the consequences may be worse for the children, they imagine that this is the model of marriage and that this is the correct situation; and one of them added that the children, even after divorce, should not be a weapon of war between the divorced, neither the father falling short toward his children because he divorced their mother, for these are his children in the end and at the start and their maintenance is on him, nor the mother using them as a weapon, inciting them against their father because he divorced. And he added that the consequences are not in divorce itself, and that we have many texts on this subject if we study them with wisdom, because the idea of divorce is based on the fact that there are bad feelings between people, so Islam set controls, and if we truly made Islam the judge in our lives the consequences would not be bad for the children. So I said: Correct; when we spoke about the consequences of divorce we did not say that divorce in itself always has dire consequences, no, rather we spoke about how the decision will be taken, and why, and what will follow from it. And if the divorce results in benefit...

**Divorce by three.** And one of the sisters asked: If the husband says "you are divorced by three," we studied long ago that one divorce takes effect, because one of the conditions of divorce is that it be among three divorces on three separate occasions, but what I hear now is that people say: No, "divorced by three" counts as divorced by three. So I said: The matter has two opinions with us to begin with. The opinion that said the triple divorce takes effect as one divorce is the opinion of Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah the Exalted have mercy on him, and the point that the divorce resulting from the word is tied to the wording is also a respected position; so whoever says that is not mistaken, and whoever says that is not mistaken. So what do we do? We look at the case file, you know the clever lawyer who sees how he can come out with the least losing outcome? The least anticipated loss from the project; so if this is what is beneficial we use this opinion, and if that is what is beneficial we use that opinion. So whoever told you that is correct, and what you studied previously is as it is, unchanged, but it represents one opinion among a number of opinions. (And I was asked: Isn't there a preponderant opinion?) So I said: No, as I told you, we must distinguish between these as existing legal rulings. It works exactly like what? Like the medicines the doctor prescribes, but who hands out the appropriate medicine? The pharmacist. So the pharmacist says to you: You have such and such, he says to you: This will have a side effect, do not take this, take this, here is the generic for you. So the shaykh who is present, who is supposed to issue fatwas for the community or in the Muslim society, is supposed to know his community's circumstances and to follow the movement of these homes inwardly and outwardly and everything, so if a matter is presented to him he is the most entitled to know where to apply and what to apply, and what the ruling to be followed is or what opinion he will adopt. So each case file is tailored on its own and judged on its own, but the opinions in themselves exist. May Allah bless you.

And the gathering concluded with their saying: Glory be to You, O Allah, and praise; we bear witness that there is no god but You, we seek Your forgiveness and repent to You, and with the recitation of Sūrat al-ʿAṣr: ﴿وَالْعَصْرِ ۝ إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا…﴾ (By time, indeed mankind is in loss, except those who believe and do righteous deeds and advise one another …).
