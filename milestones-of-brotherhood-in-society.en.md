All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him and seek His help, guidance and forgiveness. We seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and from our wrongful deeds. We bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger ﷺ. I urge you and myself to be mindful of Allah and conscious of Him in private and in public. To continue:

Imagine a home shared by a man and a woman, joined by the covenant Allah took from them on their wedding day. Each carries years of bitterness towards the other: incidents have piled up, feelings are boiling, minds never stop keeping score, and the accursed Satan leads the whole procession. He leaves them no chance to notice one word: forgiveness.

Everyone knows this word on their tongue, and remembers it whenever they need it from someone else. But when it is their turn to give, their unwillingness appears and their ideas change: I will not give as much as I receive; indeed, I will not give at all. That is why today we are speaking about one of the foundations of society.

### The First Foundations of the Community in Medina

When the Prophet ﷺ arrived in Medina as an emigrant, before doing anything else, he established bonds of brotherhood between the Muhajirun, the emigrants, and the Ansar, their helpers.[^1] He knew that Islam's cause throughout the earth would be carried forward through this new community. If brotherhood was among the first building blocks of Muslim society, we can understand its value.

What, then, were the foundations of brotherhood among the members of that community? If I want to clear the air between two people, I speak of forgiveness. The Quran says, in a translation of its meaning: “To pardon is nearer to being mindful of Allah” [Al-Baqarah 2:237]. This is our motto on this blessed day. Can a society stand without this meaning? To put it briefly: forgiveness is the safety valve of the home and of society.

Tell me: if you are a doctor, how can I place my body in your care while I hold something against you? My heart must be free of ill feeling and my mind at ease before I can trust your advice. How can I learn from a teacher I cannot bear to look at or listen to? If we are neighbours, every sunrise brings a fresh worry because I will have to see him. If we are spouses under one roof, day and night become a source of sorrow for us. May Allah protect us.

### What Is Forgiveness?

Forgiveness means moving beyond a matter without looking back. It means recognizing another person's mistake and then getting past it in your mind and heart. Do not stop at every matter, large or small. Let it pass; may Allah be pleased with you.

In one passage, the Quran brings together pardoning, overlooking and forgiving. It first says, in a translation of the meaning: “You who believe, among your spouses and children are enemies to you, so beware of them.” Up to this point, the words might seem to suggest that living together becomes impossible, dealings end, bonds fall apart and everyone goes their separate way. But the verse continues: “Yet if you pardon, overlook and forgive, Allah is truly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful” [Al-Taghabun 64:14]. So the matter can be put right. It is as though Allah brings all of this together for you: pardon in your heart, overlook in your mind, and forgive with both. Life can then begin again as though nothing had happened.

### Three Passages, Three Levels

Let us follow the Quran in its order.

In Surat al-Baqarah, Allah says, in a translation of the meaning: “Many of the People of the Book wish to turn you back into unbelievers after your faith, out of envy within themselves, after the truth has become clear to them. So pardon and overlook until Allah brings His command” [Al-Baqarah 2:109]. This is a serious offence: people watch for an opportunity against you day and night, looking for weaknesses through which they can enter. Even so, the command is direct: “Pardon and overlook.” You yourself may help bring about the command of Allah that we await: you pardon, treat people well and put yourself right before others, even before doing so in your own eyes. They see a good example in you, and suddenly those who were your enemies declare their Islam in your presence. Here you let things pass because you have a greater goal: that these people accept Islam. You overlook what they do out of concern for them. They do not yet understand the value of belief, the meaning of divine law, or what conveying the message truly involves.

It is like a skilled trader dealing with a difficult customer. He is patient and overlooks everything until the customer signs and the deal is complete. What, then, of someone who is patient in order to win a soul for Islam?

In Surat Al ‘Imran, Allah says, in a translation of the meaning: “Hurry towards forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth, prepared for those mindful of Him: those who spend in ease and hardship, restrain their anger and pardon people. Allah loves those who do good with excellence” [Al ‘Imran 3:133–134]. Where do we place forgiveness here: with mindfulness of Allah and its people, or with excellence in doing good and its people? It lies in the space between the two. The point is that pardoning people's mistakes and offences is not an unusual exception in your life. It is a goal, just as reaching the mosque to pray is a goal. As you gain a good deed here, you attain excellence in doing good there. The meaning becomes complete between a prostration in which we learn humility and lower ourselves before the Lord of Might, and an act of pardoning His creation. Someone soaring in the sky does not want to hold on to something on the ground. So it is with people who pardon: their spirits are high and their hearts rise above such things. They do not lower themselves to the level of those who dwell on foolish people's behaviour. You hear them say, “We are better than this.”

Also in Al ‘Imran, our Lord says to His Prophet ﷺ, in a translation of the meaning: “Through mercy from Allah, you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you. So pardon them, seek forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter” [Al ‘Imran 3:159]. This is a binding command: pardon them. It is not enough to remain silent while their actions stay in your head. Put those memories out of your mind and seek forgiveness for them. The verse was revealed in connection with the events of Uhud, but what counts is the general wording, not only the particular occasion. The guidance remains: in Al-Baqarah, overlook people's errors, however great; in Al ‘Imran, this is a quality of those mindful of Allah; and in the second verse, it is a quality reached only by prophets and those who follow their example. Notice how consultation comes after pardoning and seeking forgiveness. When can you sit with someone to seek their counsel unless your heart is clear, free of old resentment and hatred?

Those who say they want to follow the Sunnah: where do you stand in relation to his character ﷺ? Do not let his guidance pass you by unnoticed.

### Even with Those Who Betray

In Surat al-Ma'idah, Allah says, in a translation of the meaning: “Because they broke their covenant, We cursed them and made their hearts hard. They move words away from their proper places and forgot a share of what they had been reminded of. You will continue to discover betrayal from them, except for a few. So pardon them and overlook. Allah loves those who do good with excellence” [Al-Ma'idah 5:13]. If only it had stopped at disbelief and changing the words; they also keep betraying. Yet the divine command comes: “Pardon them and overlook.” Here again, pardoning is linked with excellence in doing good. It is as though you deal with people from a very high level, even those who betray Allah and His Messenger.

You are, in the first place, someone who calls others to Allah. Do not say that this calling is limited to shaykhs and those who stand on pulpits. You call others where you live, through your clothing, at work and in your dealings with people, wherever you may be. If someone else does wrong, you should not do wrong yourself.

### When the Blow Comes from Someone Close

A blow from a stranger is easier to bear. What is my connection to him? We knew each other for a month or two, and he did one or two things to me; let us move past it. But when it comes from someone close, it is hard.

Listen to Yusuf's words to his brothers, in a translation of the Quran's meaning: “There is no blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you; He is the most merciful of those who show mercy” [Yusuf 12:92]. What had these brothers done? They had wanted to kill their brother. Had Allah not arranged otherwise, he would have been dead. It is remarkable that the brother pardons them; what, then, of the father? Imagine your children before you and one of them wanting to kill his brother. It is as though he is killing you too. Then they say, in a translation of the meaning: “Our father, ask forgiveness for our sins; we were indeed at fault.” What was the answer? It is the language of great souls: “He said, ‘I will ask my Lord to forgive you. He is indeed the All-Forgiving, the Most Merciful’” [Yusuf 12:97–98]. And the matter was settled where it belonged.

If your wife apologizes to you once, the matter has been dealt with and is over. If your husband comforts you and makes amends, the same applies. We should not hold each other to account every day for the history of twenty-five or thirty years of marriage, recalling everything in detail. Sisters, show us a little mercy—and you men as well. Make forgiveness a name, a quality, a way of thinking, a character and a way of dealing with people. And that friend who played a trick on you twenty years ago, whom you still refuse to speak to: let it pass, man. So many days and nights have gone by.

Look at the indescribable harm the Prophet ﷺ suffered in Mecca. Then came the day of its conquest, and his famous words to its people are reported: “Go, for you are free.”[^2] The matter was over. The wife of ‘Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl, the son of Islam's greatest enemy, came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, “Messenger of Allah, grant ‘Ikrimah safety; he has gone to take a boat out to sea.” He said, “I have granted him safety.” No debate or discussion; he did not send the matter into a political security office. ‘Ikrimah later came, and the Prophet ﷺ told his Companions, “ ‘Ikrimah will come to you. Do not insult his father, for insulting the dead hurts the living and does not reach the dead.” Look at the wisdom in his words ﷺ; may my father and mother be given for him. The people held their tongues. The account says that he ﷺ rose to meet him with open arms, saying, “Welcome to the rider who has emigrated.”[^3] It is as though he is saying: where are you going? We share kinship, blood, language and humanity, and we share the prayer in which we have gathered. Some of the harshest divisions are those between people who pray in one mosque, or worshippers of one Lord.

### The Measure before Allah

When will we understand that faults are built into human beings, that they are part of being human, and that our basic condition is imperfection, not perfection? Just as you want people to pardon you, they want you to pardon them. We all seek Allah's pardon. If someone cannot learn to pardon Allah's creation, how can he seek pardon from the Lord of creation? The principles are very simple, but we make matters bigger, especially small things at home. One of us insists on going to sleep upset: he takes in a small matter and magnifies it, and his family suffers with him.

Then we say to ourselves: every day that you can spend with a heart free of resentment, do not spoil its peace. Every night that you can go to bed at ease, without a quarrel with your husband, do yourself and him a kindness. Do not let the children wake up upset. Every moment a person spends free from worrying about someone else's plots against him is a paradise enjoyed by people of faith, unknown to people of bitterness and hatred.

Look at what has happened in our community: one person talks about his paternal uncle, another about his maternal uncle, and another about a relative. Our minds have stopped thinking about our real tasks and become busy with trivial things: talking about one person, accusing another and denying someone else their due. If we had work to do, a goal to reach and an eye on the future, we would not pay attention to these matters.

As much as you like people to address you as “Professor,” “Doctor” or “a respected person,” you should live up to those titles. What if the title is “believer”? You must live up to it. If you wish to rise higher, be among those who do good with excellence: a muhsin. But that is a title only the angels will call you, by the command of the Lord of all worlds. Imagine an angel calling you one of those who do good with excellence. The angels, in a translation of the Quran's meaning, “seek forgiveness for those who believe: ‘Our Lord, Your mercy and knowledge encompass everything. Forgive those who repent and follow Your way, and protect them from the punishment of the blazing Fire’” [Ghafir 40:7]. They know our circumstances better than we know one another's.

I say these words and ask Allah to forgive me and you.

### Supplication

O Allah, preserve the harmony of our hearts and remove anger from our chests. Bring together those who manage households and those in need, and make us among those who love one another for Your sake. O Allah, grant us sound judgement, a good understanding of situations, good conduct and awareness of the consequences in our dealings with our families and brothers and sisters. O Allah, make us among those who will one day say, in a translation of the Quran's meaning: “Before this, among our families, we were full of fear. Then Allah favoured us and protected us from the punishment of the scorching blast” [Al-Tur 52:26–27]. O Allah, be pleased with us, pardon our errors and wrongful deeds, have mercy on our weakness, forgive our stumbles, take charge of our affairs and mend what is broken in us, Most Merciful of those who show mercy.
